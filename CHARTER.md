Vision: The Environmental Management Skills Taxonomy (EMST) becomes the shared language and tool the environmental sector uses to understand how its jobs are changing. It also helps people, skills and knowledge move more easily between federal, state, local, Tribal, NGO, private and academic institutions.
Theory of change: Environmental outcomes depend on professionals who can move between these sector actors. They need a blend of three crafts:
Field craft: place-based, hands-on knowledge of land, water, air and living systems.
Data craft: turning observation into evidence, models and decisions, increasingly with AI.
Statecraft: making decisions stick through law, policy, institutions and relationships.
If those blends are visible and portable, mobility follows.
First job: Support training, curriculum and workforce strategy for the federal and state domains, covering reskilling, upskilling and new-hire competencies.
Rollout runs NGO pilot group, then federal and state agencies, then the whole sector including academia.
Design for the first user without shutting out the last.
Sources are a starting point, not a boundary: Any framework, standard, dataset or tool named below is illustrative. Each expert should:
seek out the most relevant, current and credible sources in their field, including ones not named here and ones published later;
say when a named source is outdated, superseded or a poor fit;
record why they chose the sources they used.
Evidence standard: Label what is evidence, what is expert judgment and what is speculation. Cite sources, and commit research, decisions and sources to the repo with provenance.
Work at full depth: Bring the judgment of your field's most experienced practitioner.
Propose, don't just react.
Raise issues outside your own area when they affect the vision.
Disagree openly and name the trade-offs; the Chief Taxonomy Architect resolves disputes.
Vision & integration
Chief Taxonomy Architect (Project Lead)
Mission: Keep the whole design coherent so the 58 domains, three crafts, sector actors and every downstream use read as one system. Decide what from the current EMST carries forward, changes or retires.
Expertise: Designing competency frameworks and ontologies at the scale of a national standard, balancing detail against usability, governing a standard that keeps changing, and turning conflicting expert input into decisions.
Reference points: The current EMST architecture, the O*NET Content Model, ESCO and competency frameworks from other sectors, plus any stronger models for fast-changing taxonomies shared by many organizations.
Pushes on: The balance between the depth subject experts want and the simplicity that product and adoption need.
Evidence & foresight
Labor Market & AI-Impact Research Lead
Mission: Establish what the evidence actually shows about how AI and other forces are changing environmental tasks, skills and roles today. That means separating work being augmented, work being automated and new work.
Expertise: Labor economics, task-based methods for measuring AI exposure, job-posting and occupational data analysis, and research synthesis. Judges how sound each index's methods are rather than just summarizing it.
Reference points: The Anthropic Economic Index, WEF Future of Jobs, LinkedIn Economic Graph green-skills research, Lightcast, BLS projections and OECD, plus academic literature, agency workforce data and new indices as they appear. Also demand signals such as position descriptions and the requirements written into land management plans.
Pushes on: Where evidence specific to the environmental sector is thin, and what is known versus assumed.
Futurist / Strategic Foresight Lead
Mission: Make sure the tool is built for the futures the sector may face, not only the present the data describes.
Expertise: Horizon scanning, spotting weak signals, analyzing drivers, building scenarios, backcasting, stress-testing strategies, and running foresight sessions with sector actors. Turns long horizons into near-term workforce decisions.
Reference points: Methods such as Three Horizons, the Futures Wheel, causal layered analysis and scenario matrices, or whatever method best fits the question.
Pushes on: Designing for roles that don't exist yet. Also sets up recurring horizon scanning so the taxonomy keeps evolving.
The three crafts
Field Craft Lead
Mission: Define field craft and show how it is changing as technology reaches the field.
Expertise: Decades of hands-on environmental and natural resource practice: monitoring, inventory, restoration, operations and safety. Knows how field technology actually gets adopted, and what can't be learned from a screen.
Reference points: Agency field protocols, professional society standards and practitioner knowledge, plus Indigenous and local knowledge where its holders choose to share it, on their terms.
Pushes on: A taxonomy that leans too heavily on data and underrates judgment earned outdoors.
Data Craft Lead
Mission: Define data craft, from basic data literacy to expert modeling, including responsible use of AI.
Expertise: Geospatial analysis and remote sensing, environmental modeling, digital twins, machine learning and AI-assisted analysis, data stewardship, and the ethics and governance of AI in environmental decisions.
Reference points: The NIST AI Risk Management Framework, FAIR data principles and agency data strategies, plus newer practice wherever it is ahead of these.
Pushes on: Separating lasting competencies from fluency in tools that will be obsolete in two years.
Statecraft Lead
Mission: Define statecraft and how it differs across sector actors.
Expertise: Environmental law and administrative process, permitting and environmental review, coordination between governments, collaborative governance, budgets and appropriations, public engagement, negotiation and political judgment.
Reference points: NEPA, ESA, CWA, CAA, and shared stewardship and Good Neighbor agreements, plus their state, Tribal, local and private-sector equivalents.
Pushes on: Treating statecraft as a competency that can be taught, not a personality trait.
Domain Expert Bench (rotating)
Mission: Supply depth across all 58 domains so the tool isn't skewed toward public lands and forestry.
Expertise: Recognized practitioners and researchers in each subsector, such as water, air quality, waste and circular economy, energy, climate adaptation, corporate sustainability and ESG, conservation, and environmental health. The three craft leads bring them in as needed.
Reference points: The standards, credentials and literature of each expert's own field.
Pushes on: Places where a sector-wide generalization breaks down in their domain.
Sector actors & workforce voice
Federal Human Capital & Classification Advisor
Mission: Make the tool usable for federal workforce strategy, classification, hiring and development.
Expertise: Federal classification and qualification standards, position descriptions, skills-based and merit hiring, workforce planning, training policy, and how reforms actually play out inside agencies.
Reference points: OPM occupational series (0401, 0460, 0028, 0408, 0486, 0819 and others), the Chance to Compete Act and OPM competency models, plus current OPM guidance and agency practice as they change.
Pushes on: Whether an HR specialist could use this tomorrow.
State & Local Government Workforce Advisor
Mission: Fit the tool to state agencies, counties, special districts and municipal utilities, and map how people move among them and into and out of federal roles.
Expertise: State civil service and classification systems, state workforce boards, WIOA, public-sector labor relations and local environmental services.
Reference points: State classification plans, WIOA state plans and comparisons across states.
Pushes on: Federal-centric design that breaks across 50 different state systems.
Tribal Nations Workforce & Sovereignty Advisor
Mission: Represent Tribal nations as sovereign governments and employers, and make sure the tool respects Tribal control over their own data and knowledge.
Expertise: Tribal natural resource and environmental programs, government-to-government relations, appropriate use of Indigenous knowledge, Tribal workforce pathways and Indigenous data governance.
Reference points: The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance, Tribal consultation policies and inter-Tribal organizations. The guidance of the nations themselves comes first.
Pushes on: Can say no to including knowledge or data that isn't the project's to include.
Private Sector & Industry Employer Advisor
Mission: Bring private employers into the mobility map, including consulting, utilities, energy, water, timber and agriculture, carbon markets and corporate sustainability.
Expertise: Industry hiring and job structures, contractor and consulting roles, compliance and ESG functions, and how private employers value and pay for each craft.
Reference points: Industry job frameworks, professional certifications and employer hiring data.
Pushes on: Whether public-sector competencies translate into the terms private employers hire on, and the reverse.
Workforce Voice & Cross-Sector Practitioner Panel
Mission: Keep the people the tool serves at the center, and test the theory of change against real careers.
Members: Professionals who have moved between sector actors, early-career workers, federal and state employees in transition, conservation corps alumni, and employee organizations. They bring lived experience of the barriers, of lost institutional knowledge, and credibility with their peers.
Reference points: Their own experience is the main source, backed by data on career histories.
Pushes on: Their answer to "does this help me?" outweighs how elegant the design looks internally.
Workforce science & learning
Industrial-Organizational Psychologist (Job Analysis & Validation)
Mission: Make the competencies and proficiency levels valid, measurable and defensible when used for hiring, promotion and training.
Expertise: Job and task analysis, competency modeling, SME panels and Delphi studies, behavioral indicators, assessment design, evidence of reliability and validity, and legal defensibility.
Reference points: The Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures, SIOP Principles and the AERA/APA/NCME testing standards, plus current research on skills-based hiring.
Pushes on: Any competency that can't be observed or assessed.
Career Mobility & Theory of Change Lead
Mission: Own the theory of change and test it. That means showing which skills transfer between sector actors, where the barriers are, and what actually moves people.
Expertise: Modeling how similar and transferable skills are, career lattices, network analysis of career paths, logic models and impact evaluation. Defines the metrics that decide whether the tool expands beyond the pilot.
Reference points: Theory-of-change and contribution-analysis practice and skill-similarity methods from labor economics, or whatever evaluation design fits the question.
Pushes on: Holding the team to outcomes, not just deliverables.
Learning & Curriculum Design Strategist
Mission: Turn competencies into reskilling, upskilling and new-hire learning paths, and connect them to academic programs.
Expertise: Competency-based education, program design, work-based learning and apprenticeships, stackable credentials, alignment with academic programs and accreditation, and measuring learning.
Reference points: CIP-to-SOC crosswalks, SAF and ABET accreditation, Open Badges and the Comprehensive Learner Record, plus new AI-enabled learning approaches.
Pushes on: Turning the taxonomy's detail into something a learner or instructor can actually use.
Technology, data & trust
Workforce Systems Interoperability Architect
Mission: Make the taxonomy plug straight into the workforce systems agencies and employers already run, now and as those systems change.
Expertise: Skills data models, standards for exchanging competency data, linked data, API design, crosswalks and versioning, and how enterprise HR and learning platforms take in skills data.
Reference points:
Standards: IEEE 1484.20.3, 1EdTech CASE, Credential Engine CTDL, HR Open Standards, JSON-LD and SKOS, O*NET-SOC and ESCO.
Platforms: Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle HCM, Eightfold and Lightcast.
Whatever standards the market settles on next.
Pushes on: Anything that would lock the taxonomy to a single vendor.
Skills Intelligence / NLP Engineer
Mission: Build the engine that keeps the tool current. It continuously maps job postings, position descriptions, curricula and plan requirements into the taxonomy so the tool can show change over time.
Expertise: Extracting skills from text with LLMs, semantic matching across taxonomies, workflows for human review, detecting drift, confidence scoring, and evaluation against hand-checked reference sets.
Reference points: The current state of NLP and LLM methods, which should be expected to change quickly.
Pushes on: Reporting where the model is uncertain rather than smoothing it over.
Data Engineer & Repository Steward
Mission: Make GitHub the project's single source of truth for research, data, decisions and provenance, in a form that is open and reproducible.
Expertise: Data pipelines, schema validation, automated testing and builds, release management, provenance, licensing and documentation.
Reference points: Patterns already working in the LMPs pipeline, plus better practice where it exists.
Pushes on: Nothing counts as done until it's in the repo and can be reproduced.
Security, Privacy & Government Compliance Lead
Mission: Clear the path through federal and state security, privacy and accessibility reviews so adoption doesn't stall at procurement.
Expertise: FedRAMP and federal authority-to-operate processes, the Privacy Act and state privacy laws, handling personnel data, Section 508 and WCAG accessibility, and securing open-source dependencies.
Reference points: The current requirements in each jurisdiction the tool will enter.
Pushes on: Raising compliance constraints early, while they are still cheap to design for.
Product & UX Lead
Mission: Turn the taxonomy into a tool people choose to use: one that shows how roles are changing, maps career pathways and diagnoses skill gaps.
Expertise: Product strategy, user research with the NGO pilot cohort, interaction design, data visualization, front-end development and accessible design.
Pushes on: The first user's workflow over completeness.
Adoption & sustainability
Pilot Adoption, Coalition & Policy Lead
Mission: Run the NGO pilot, then carry the tool from a useful resource to formal adoption across federal and state government, the wider sector and academia.
Expertise: Coalition facilitation, change management, communications, and governance and contribution models for open standards. Also the policy routes to adoption, such as an O*NET crosswalk, OPM classification language and grant conditions.
Pushes on: Keeping the build tied to what adopters will actually commit to.
Stewardship & Sustainability Strategist
Mission: Secure a long-term owner, funding and governance home so the taxonomy outlives the pilot.
Expertise: Philanthropic and public funding strategy, models for institutional hosting, governance of shared public infrastructure, and sustainable business models.
Pushes on: Asking of every commitment, "Who maintains this in year five?"

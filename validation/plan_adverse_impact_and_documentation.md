## 1. Posture: who does what

EMST **does not rank, score, screen or select people** and holds no person-level data (SPEC §1.3, §16.2). Adverse impact arises where an **employer** uses a selection procedure. EMST's responsibilities are:

1. **Not to build impact into the content.** It removes constructions that screen on circumstance rather than performance (§3).
2. **To make the employer's evidence cheap to produce.** It supplies task-first content, a job-analysis instrument, linkage studies and documentation templates (§5).
3. **To support pooled evidence** (consortium and transportability designs) where single employers are too small (§4.5).
4. **To measure whether the tool helps or hurts mobility.** A programme-fairness metric for cross-sector movers and non-traditional entrants tests the theory of change (§4.6).

Statements EMST and its kits **may** make: "EMST content is developed to professional standards (SIOP Principles; AERA/APA/NCME Standards). It supports, but does not replace, an employer's job analysis. Use tier: [tier]; release: [ID]."
Statements they **must not** make:
- "UGESP compliant";
- "validated for selection" (of a release or a competency in itself);
- "EEOC-approved" or "OPM-approved";
- "legally defensible" as a property of EMST;
- "bias-free";
- "recommended pathway" (ADR-0023).
CP's claims-hygiene lint (WP-12) adds these strings to its blocklist.

### 2.2 Federal hiring (job-relatedness)

- **Chance to Compete Act of 2024** (Pub. L. 118-188, Dec 23 2024), amending 5 U.S.C. 3304 [E: IO-031; text read via govinfo, IO-111]:
  - a **technical assessment** is "a position-specific tool that is relevant to the position", is **"based upon a job analysis"**, and does not solely include or principally rely on a self-assessment from an automated examination;
  - a **subject matter expert** possesses an understanding of the duties and KSAs of the position, and may develop position-specific assessments **in partnership with HR**, "based on job analysis";
  - OPM must study sharing technical assessments across examining agencies.
  - EMST's relevance: its task profiles and linkage data are inputs to those job analyses. Natural Resources Management is in OPM's Tier 2 of the transition, with no Tier 2 date; FY2028 is the government-wide full-implementation target [E: V1-17].
- **5 CFR 300.103** still requires each employment practice to rest on a job analysis, a rational relationship to performance, and nondiscrimination. OPM's interim final rule (91 FR 48236, Jul 31 2026) removed its references to the Uniform Guidelines from the federal hiring rules [E: V4 IO-022].
- **Merit Hiring Plan** (May 29 2025): since Sep 30 2025, self-assessment questionnaires may be used only for minimum qualifications or eligibility, not rating and ranking (with exceptions) [E: V1-09].

### 2.3 Title VII disparate impact (defensibility)

- The statutory disparate-impact provision, 42 U.S.C. 2000e-2(k) (Civil Rights Act of 1991), remains law [E: V4 IO-029]. Its structure [E-u: statute text, not re-read this session]:
  1. a complaining party shows that a particular employment practice causes a disparate impact on a protected basis;
  2. the employer must then demonstrate that the practice is **job related for the position in question and consistent with business necessity**;
  3. liability can still follow if a less discriminatory **alternative** practice exists and the employer refuses to adopt it.
- Section 2000e-2(l) prohibits adjusting scores, or using different cut-offs, on the basis of a protected characteristic. **"Alternatives" in this plan never means race-, sex- or other group-norming** [E-u].
- **Federal enforcement posture has changed. The statute has not.**
  - EO 14281 (Apr 2025) directs agencies to deprioritize disparate-impact enforcement [E: V4 IO-026].
  - A DOJ Office of Legal Counsel opinion (Jun 9 2026) concluded that the Uniform Guidelines' disparate-impact framework is unconstitutional as applied; it does not repeal Title VII, and courts have the final word [E: V4 IO-020, IO-029].
  - OFCCP's rescission of 41 CFR 60-3 (with the other EO 11246 regulations) takes effect **Oct 26 2026** [E: V4 IO-028].
  - 29 CFR 1607 remains codified, and its legal status is contested [E: V4 IO-020].
  - The EEOC removed its AI-and-adverse-impact technical assistance on Jan 27 2025 [E: V4 IO-027].
  - Private suits and state anti-discrimination law remain [J].
- **Consequence for EMST [J]:** evidence is written so that an employer can show job-relatedness and business necessity, and a documented search for less discriminatory alternatives, under any of these postures. The four-fifths ratio is used as **one** practical screening heuristic, alongside significance and practical-significance measures (§4.2). It is never presented as a compliance test.

## 3. Content stage: preventing impact in EMST content (EMST owns this)

| Risk built into content | Control in the kit | Where enforced |
|---|---|---|
| Years of experience used as a level or requirement | B04 lint; years only in descriptive `typicalTimeToLevel`; A7 survey item used only for sampling | `indicator_lint_rules.yaml` B04; ADR-0004 §7 |
| Credentials, training completion or degrees as proxies for performance | B04 lint; credentials are gates on tasks and role variants with an essential-function basis (ADR-0003 T-8), and substitution routes are recorded | lint; WP-07 credential register; WP-08 variants |
| Opportunity-gated indicators ("leads national initiatives") that measure access, not competence | OBS-4 O flag; B03 lint; such indicators cannot be `useClass = select` | lint PUB-04; GS-IND double coding |
| Traits and attitudes | B11 lint | lint |
| Access requirements not tied to essential functions (vehicle, availability, unanchored physical demands, citizenship) | B12 lint; physical requirements only on role variants, with an essential-function basis | lint; WP-08 review |
| AI-assisted indicators that disadvantage people without tool access, or that are not accommodated | `augmentationMode` explicit; equal tool access and accommodation required in the assessment specification (D5, D10) | lint PUB-05/08; templates |
| Inflated "needed at entry" flags that shut out trainees and movers | needed-at-entry requires ≥ 50% of incumbents **and** ≥ 50% of supervisors (`instrument_job_analysis_survey.md` §6) | computed view |
| Cultural or linguistic loading; regional jargon; programme labels that only insiders know | bias and sensitivity review (below); cognitive interviews (`protocol_cognitive_interviews.md`); programme labels only as `altLabel` (ADR-0016) | review record |
| Content touching Indigenous knowledge or Tribal programmes | held; never assessed by non-Tribal panels (ADR-0007) | CI hold checks; G-TN |

**Bias and sensitivity review panel** (content stage, per release):
- **Members:** 5–7 reviewers with diverse backgrounds across sector actor, region, career route (incl. ≥ 1 mover and ≥ 1 early-career), disability (≥ 1 accessibility reviewer) and language background.
- **Scope:** every indicator proposed for `develop` or `select`; the survey instrument; kit text.
- **Checklist:**
  - B03, B04, B11 and B12 residues the lint missed;
  - opportunity-gating;
  - cultural, linguistic or regional loading;
  - accessibility of the assessment method implied;
  - gendered or exclusionary language;
  - assumptions about full-time, year-round, single-employer careers.
- **Decision:** each flag is resolved by revision, by retention with a recorded rationale, or by deletion, in the review record (D9 part A).
- **Rule:** TN advises on anything bordering Indigenous-related content. Such content is held, not reviewed for release.

## 4. Assessment stage: monitoring plan template for adopters

### 4.1 Data (employer-held; never in the EMST repo)

- Voluntary applicant self-identification where lawful.
- Applicant flow by hurdle: applied, met MQs, passed assessment 1 … selected.
- Hurdle scores.
- Accommodation requests (counts only in reports).
- Mover and entry-route attributes collected for the programme-fairness metric (§4.6), only with consent.

### 4.2 Analyses at every hurdle and overall [E-u: IO-098; J]

| Measure | Specification |
|---|---|
| Selection rate by group | per hurdle; groups as the employer's applicable law defines; age 40+ |
| Impact ratio (IR) | lower group rate / higher group rate, with a confidence interval [E-u: IO-098] |
| Statistical significance | Fisher's exact test (small samples) or two-proportion z; across cycles or strata, Mantel–Haenszel or similar aggregation |
| Practical significance | shortfall (additional selections from the lower-rate group needed to equalize rates); IR sensitivity to moving one selection between groups |
| Intersectional | where every cell has n ≥ 30 applicants (otherwise pooled across cycles) |
| Score-level | standardized mean differences on each assessment component; differential item functioning where n allows |

### 4.3 Triggers (pre-registered by the adopter; EMST defaults)

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| IR < .80 **and** p < .05 at a hurdle; or IR < .80 in two consecutive cycles; or a practically significant shortfall persisting across cycles | Start an **alternatives review**: (a) check each component's job-relatedness evidence (D2, D4, D8); (b) search for less discriminatory alternatives of comparable validity (§4.4); (c) document what was considered, the evidence, and the decision (D9 part B). The decision is the employer's, with counsel |
| A trigger at a minimum-qualification screen (degree, credential, years) | Review substitution routes first (demonstrated-competency substitution; equivalent experience across sector actors) |
| Any AI-scored component with a trigger | Also apply the jurisdiction's AI rules (§2.4) and SIOP AI recommendations |

### 4.4 Alternatives to consider (none may group-norm; §2.3)

- A different method mix (structured interview with BARS, work sample, knowledge test, SJT), using validity and subgroup-difference evidence for each method [E-u: IO-049, IO-050, IO-052, IO-053, IO-054].
- Component weighting set from job-analysis importance, not by default.
- The cut score re-set by documented standard-setting at minimally acceptable performance (D7), not by rank order.
- Removal of hurdles with weak job-relatedness, especially self-report screens (CTCA: not principally self-assessment).
- Accommodations and alternative formats.
- Never one high-impact hurdle: multi-method assessment with documented consideration of alternatives (ADR-0024, IO dissent 6).

### 4.5 Consortium and transportability studies (EMST supports; employers own data)

- **Why.** Detecting r = .20 at α = .05 (two-tailed) with power .80 needs n ≈ 194 incumbents in one job family. Few single agencies have that [E: IO Phase 2 computation]. Pooled designs are needed.
- **Design.** A concurrent criterion study across participating employers whose positions match an EMST role-variant task profile. Job similarity is shown through the job-analysis survey: the employer's retained critical tasks overlap the profile's with importance-weighted overlap ≥ 0.60 (the same threshold ADR-0006 uses to separate roles) [J].
  - Criteria are collected **for research only**: supervisor BARS ratings by trained raters, blind-scored work products, task-book completion.
  - Transportability to a new employer rests on the documented task-profile match (D8).
- **Pre-registration fields** (in addition to `preregistration_core.yaml`):
  - participating employers (as categories in the repo);
  - job-similarity evidence and threshold;
  - predictor battery and versions;
  - criteria and rater training;
  - target n (≥ 194 for r = .20, two-tailed) and power analysis;
  - corrections (range restriction, criterion unreliability) stated before data;
  - fairness analyses (differential prediction);
  - data-sharing agreement (aggregates only to EMST).
- **Stop rules:**
  - recruitment stops at the target n, or after 18 months;
  - if n < 124 (power .80 for r = .25), the study is reported as underpowered and makes no validity claim;
  - no interim looks at validity coefficients.

### 4.6 Programme-fairness metric: does EMST help movers? (CM lane; not a legal test)

For adopters that consent, compute the same IR and shortfall measures for **cross-sector movers**, **non-traditional entrants** (conservation corps alumni, people with interrupted or seasonal careers, veterans) and **non-degree routes**, at each hurdle that uses EMST-derived content. A tool meant to open mobility that screens out these groups has failed on its own terms (TEAM_BRIEF §6). Results feed CM's gate metrics (ADR-0023). They are reported only as aggregates with n ≥ 5.

## 5. Validity documentation package: templates

Each template is a fill-in structure. **EMST** supplies the structure and EMST-side evidence; **the employer** completes and holds everything about its own positions, people and decisions. Nothing person-level enters the EMST repo.

| ID | Document | Owner | Required contents | Retention [J] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | Validity argument for the intended use | employer, with IO template | intended use and decision; interpretation claims; for each claim: warrant, evidence (by the five *Standards* sources), rebuttals considered; EMST release and use tier relied on | life of use + period set by counsel |
| **D2** | Job analysis | EMST (cluster study, JAS) + employer (position confirmation addendum) | methods, frame, instruments (hashes), response, weights, reliability, retention and criticality decisions; **addendum:** the employer's own confirmation for the position (SMEs, date, tasks confirmed, added or dropped). This is the "based upon a job analysis" record (CTCA; 5 CFR 300.103) | same |
| **D3** | SME documentation | employer | SME qualifications (as categories), conflicts, roles in development (CTCA SME definition); aggregate composition only in shared reports | same |
| **D4** | Linkage | EMST (cluster linkage) + employer | task ↔ competency matrix with link strength and ICC; indicator ↔ task; assessment blueprint coverage of critical tasks | same |
| **D5** | Assessment specification | employer, with IO template | position-specific design; methods per indicator; scoring rules and BARS (with retranslation record); administration standardization; equal tool access in assisted-mode tasks; how the assessment avoids principal reliance on self-assessment | same |
| **D6** | Reliability of scores | employer | scorer agreement (ICC(2,k) ≥ .80 operational, ≥ .70 development); internal consistency for knowledge tests; assessor agreement ≥ .70 for qualification sign-offs | same |
| **D7** | Standard-setting | employer, with IO method | method (modified Angoff for knowledge tests; body-of-work or contrasting groups for work samples); panel; minimally acceptable performance at the required level; resulting cut score; adverse-impact review before adoption | same |
| **D8** | Criterion, transportability or generalization evidence (where content evidence is insufficient) | employer or consortium | design; job-similarity evidence (EMST task-profile match); results with corrections; differential prediction | same |
| **D9** | Fairness | employer (EMST supplies part A for its content) | **Part A:** EMST content-stage review record (§3). **Part B:** hurdle analyses (§4.2), triggers, alternatives considered with evidence, decisions and dates | life of use + period set by counsel; ≥ 4 years where California ADS rules apply [E: V4 IO-036] |
| **D10** | Accommodation | employer | procedures, including for AI-assisted tasks; counts only in reports | same |
| **D11** | AI documentation (only if AI scores or screens) | employer; EMST for its own LLM-coded content | model and version, purpose, evaluation against humans, fairness tests, drift monitoring, notices given, audits (per §2.4 jurisdictions); for EMST content: the LLM-coder gate record (`protocol_llm_coder_gate.md` §10) | same; ≥ 4 years (California ADS) |
| **D12** | EMST version and provenance | EMST (generated) | release ID, delta file, IDs and versions of every element used, their `useTier` and `validationStatus`, evidence labels, agreement results behind any tag | permanent (repo) |

## 6. Pre-registration fields for monitoring (adopter-run; EMST template)

`preregistration_core.yaml#core_required` (adapted: the adopter is the study owner), plus:
- `hurdles_and_procedures` (with versions);
- `groups_and_lawful_basis_for_data`;
- `analyses` (§4.2);
- `triggers` (§4.3);
- `alternatives_review_procedure` (§4.4);
- `programme_fairness_opt_in` (§4.6);
- `reporting_cadence` (each cycle; annual summary);
- `retention` (D9, D11).
Stop rule: monitoring does not stop while the procedure is in use. A procedure that is withdrawn keeps its records for the retention period.

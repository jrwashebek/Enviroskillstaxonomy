# What EMST covers

{% hint style="info" %}
Draft release 2.0.0-rc.1. Drafted by AI agents, not yet reviewed by people. [What this means](../../how-to-read/drafted-by-ai-agents.md)
{% endhint %}

EMST describes part of environmental work, not all of it. This release has role profiles in 10 of the 19 subject modules, plus core work that spans them.

This page shows where EMST has roles, where it has only competencies and tasks, and where it has nothing yet.

## At a glance

EMST's scope map splits environmental work into 44 subsectors, in 9 areas. A subsector is one kind of work, such as drinking water or wildland fire.

Of the 44 subsectors:

- Roles drafted: 28 subsectors

## Kinds of role profile

Each role profile has one of these depths. The Roles page lists every role except the emerging ones.

| Kind of profile | What it means |
|---|---|
| Full profiles: 13 | The roles drafted in most depth. Each lists its tasks, the competencies it needs with draft target levels, and how the work differs by employer. |
| Earlier drafts at lower depth: 18 | The same parts, drafted in less depth. The Roles page calls them starter profiles. |
| Emerging roles: 6 | Ideas about work that may be forming. They appear only in What's changing, without tasks or competencies, because the work is not yet shown to exist. |

## The scope map

Each subsector belongs to a subject module, a group of related subsectors such as air quality. The rest are core practice areas, such as environmental review, which every module shares. There are 6 of them.

{% hint style="info" %}
AI agents drafted the scope map and the tags. No person has reviewed them yet.
{% endhint %}

### How to read the counts

- Roles: every role in the subsector's module. Roles are not tagged by subsector, so a role may not cover every subsector in its module.
- Competencies: those tagged with the subsector, from any module. Core competencies are not tagged by subsector, so each core area shows all of them.
- Tasks: the tasks linked to those competencies.

### What each status means

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| Roles drafted | Its module has at least one role profile. |
| Competencies and tasks only | No roles in its module, but some competencies are tagged with it, and tasks are linked to them. |
| Not started in this release | No roles in its module, and no competencies tagged with it. Related core work may still be in EMST. |

## Where permitting work is

{% hint style="info" %}
Permitting work is in 4 roles: Environmental permit writer, Environmental review specialist, Environmental compliance inspector and Enforcement case manager. They are in the job family “Environmental review, authorization and compliance”. Permitting for energy projects, transmission lines and mines belongs to modules with no roles in this release: “Clean energy, grid and energy management” and “Extractives: mining, reclamation, oil and gas, orphan wells”. Between them, these modules have 1 competency: Administer mineral and energy authorizations and reclamation.
{% endhint %}

## Waves

Wave 1 modules were built first, for this release. Waves 2 and 3 are planned. This release gives no dates for them.

- Wave 1: 9 modules, all 9 with roles.
- Wave 2: 8 modules, 1 with roles (“Working lands, restoration and land conservation”).
- Wave 3: 2 modules, none with roles.

## Not on the scope map

Some environmental work is outside the scope map entirely.

Environmental biotechnology, such as bioremediation or synthetic biology, is one example. It is not one of the 44 subsectors. No task in this release mentions it.

Soil bioengineering appears only inside restoration and erosion-control work, in 1 task:

- Install bioengineered streambank treatments such as live stakes, fascines or brush layers, timing the work to plant dormancy and flow

Environmental DNA (eDNA) appears in 2 tasks, under Conservation genetics and genomics.

## Tell us what's missing

If you know environmental work that EMST does not describe, you can tell us. Describe it in your own words: the job title, the main tasks and the kind of employer.

Suggestions go to the project's GitHub Issues, where the EMST project lead decides on changes. A suggestion does not mean the work will be added.

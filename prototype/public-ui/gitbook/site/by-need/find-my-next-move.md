# Find my next move

{% hint style="info" %}
Draft release 2.0.0-rc.1. Drafted by AI agents, not yet reviewed by people. [What this means](../how-to-read/drafted-by-ai-agents.md)
{% endhint %}

If you are thinking about a change of job, EMST can show which roles share work with yours and what is different. It never tells you which move to make. Sharing work doesn't mean you qualify.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Exploratory, never a recommendation. Destinations are computed from draft role profiles: roles named as next steps in this role's profile, roles with a drafted learning pathway, and other roles that share at least half of their target competencies with this one. Not yet observed in real career moves.
{% endhint %}

## Steps

1. **Find your role, or the one closest to it.** Browse the roles.
2. **Read “Where it can lead” on the role's page.** It lists roles that share work with this one, worked out from the draft profiles.
3. **Compare two roles.** Compare shows the competencies and tasks the two roles share, what is only in each, and what to check with the employer.
4. **Check with the employer.** Sharing competencies doesn't mean you qualify. Each employer decides what its jobs need.

## What a comparison can't tell you

- **Whether you qualify.** Each employer decides what its jobs need. Federal jobs also have qualification standards set by OPM.
- **Whether a move is possible.** If EMST has worked out no path between two roles, that says nothing about whether the move is possible.
- **How people really move.** The links between roles come from draft profiles. None has been observed in real career moves yet.
- **Pay, grade or openings.** EMST has no data on these.

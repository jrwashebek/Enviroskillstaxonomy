# What you can use EMST for

{% hint style="info" %}
Draft release 2.0.0-rc.1. Drafted by AI agents, not yet reviewed by people. [What this means](drafted-by-ai-agents.md)
{% endhint %}

Every record says what it may be used for. There are four uses, from lightest to heaviest: explore only, describe and plan, train and develop, and job analysis input.

In release 2.0.0-rc.1, every record is either “explore only” or “describe and plan”. No record reaches the training or job analysis uses yet.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Never for decisions about people.** EMST describes work. It is not a job analysis, a basis for classification, a crediting plan or an assessment. Do not use it to hire, to set pay or grade, to rate performance, to discipline staff or to choose who to lay off.
{% endhint %}

## Describe and plan

Use for describing work and planning. Not for decisions about people.

You can:

- Describe the work of a role in a workforce plan or grant report.
- Map a course or training program to the tasks a role does.
- Find task wording to start a job or position description. Your HR office must then review and change it.

You can't:

- Screen, score or rank people who apply for a job.
- Set a job's series, grade or pay (classification).
- Rate how well an employee does their job.
- Choose which jobs or people to cut in a reduction in force.

AI agents drafted these records, and no practitioner has checked them yet. That is enough to describe work in broad terms. It is not enough for decisions about people.

## Explore only

Use for exploring ideas only. Not for plans or decisions.

You can:

- Explore what a new course might cover, such as a class on prescribed fire.
- Talk about how a kind of work may change in the next few years.
- Spot new skills to keep an eye on.

You can't:

- State it as fact in a workforce plan or report.
- Build a training plan or task book from it.
- Use it in hiring, or to set a job's grade or pay.
- Use it to rate staff or to decide who to lay off.

The evidence behind these records is thin. Some describe work that may only be starting. Others have skill levels that no expert panel has placed yet.

## Train and develop

{% hint style="info" %}
**Not reached in this release.** No record reaches this use yet. First, practitioners from at least three kinds of employer must review the content. Then an expert panel must agree on the level of each skill statement.
{% endhint %}

When a record reaches this use, you could:

- Build a training plan or a task book.
- Write an individual development plan with a staff member.
- Let people check their own skills against the levels.

Even then, you can't:

- Screen, score or rank people who apply for a job.
- Set a job's series, grade or pay (classification).
- Rate how well an employee does their job.
- Choose which jobs or people to cut in a reduction in force.

Training shapes people's careers. It needs content that practitioners have checked, with skill levels that experts agree on.

## Job analysis input

{% hint style="info" %}
**Not reached in this release.** No record reaches this use yet. A record would need everything the training use needs. It would also need a job analysis that confirms it for that kind of job, a plan to check for unfair effects on groups of applicants, and full records of how it was tested. Even then, it is only one input to an employer's own job analysis.
{% endhint %}

When a record reaches this use, you could:

- Give your experts a first list of tasks to rate in your own job analysis.
- Check your experts' task list against EMST's to find gaps.
- Reuse task wording in a job posting once your own job analysis confirms it.

Even then, you can't:

- Screen, score or rank applicants straight from EMST.
- Set a job's series, grade or pay (classification).
- Rate how well an employee does their job.
- Choose which jobs or people to cut in a reduction in force.

Hiring changes people's lives. It needs evidence about the specific job. OPM's memo of 27 August 2026 on AI in hiring says subject-matter experts must check AI-drafted job analysis against the job's real duties.

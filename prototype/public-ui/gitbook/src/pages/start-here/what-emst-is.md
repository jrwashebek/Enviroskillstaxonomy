EMST stands for Environmental Management Skills Taxonomy. It describes environmental work. It lists the tasks people do and the skills behind them, which EMST calls competencies. It also shows how a role differs from one employer to another, such as a federal agency, a state, a nonprofit or a firm.

## What is in it

- **Roles.** {{roles}} role profiles, such as a forester or an environmental permit writer.
- **Tasks.** {{tasks}} short statements of the work, each linked to the roles that do it.
- **Competencies.** {{comps}} skills and bodies of knowledge behind the tasks.
- **Employer versions.** {{variants}} versions of the roles, one for each kind of employer that has the role.
- **Subject modules.** {{modules}} groups of related work, such as air quality, plus core practice that every module shares.
- **Signals of change.** {{signals}} signs that the work may change, all on watch.
- **Links to other systems.** Links to O\*NET, ESCO, OPM job series and state job classes. AI agents suggested every link, and no person has reviewed them.

[How EMST is put together](page:how-emst-works/README.md) explains how these parts link.

## Three places, one release

- **This guidebook** explains EMST: how to read it, what it covers and how to use it well.
- **The [EMST explorer](explorer:/)** holds every record. It finds roles and tasks, compares two roles, switches between kinds of employer and exports what you see.
- **[GitHub]({{repo}})** is the only place EMST is changed. It holds the data files, the decisions and the issues people report.

All three are built from the same release. [About this guidebook](page:release/about-this-guidebook.md)

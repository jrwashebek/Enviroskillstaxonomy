# ADR-0023: Mobility model, friction register, the operating theory of change and expansion gates

- **Decision point:** DP-22 (new; CA) — similarity basis, frictions, pathway recommendation scope, theory of change, expansion gates
- **Status:** accepted; the **restated theory of change** is adopted as the operating version with the charter sentence as north star — flagged to the project lead (`questions_for_project_lead.md` Q6)
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 · **Decider:** CA
- **Roles consulted:** CM, FH, SL, PS, DB, FC, LC, CP, IO, TN, AS, LM
- **Related:** ADR-0005 (blends describe), ADR-0006 (roles), ADR-0012 (no personal data), ADR-0017 (authority position, sign-off), ADR-0022 (X11 translation)

## Context

CM tested the charter's theory of change ("if those blends are visible and portable, mobility follows") against published evidence: visibility alone rarely moves people (only 37% of firms announcing skills-based hiring followed through; fewer than 1 in 700 hires; Colorado rewrote ~80% of classifications yet non-degree hires stayed at 25%); what moves hiring is embedding skills **in the instruments that gate movement** [E: CM-011, CM-012, CM-014, CM-050]. Portability is mostly a property of institutions on a pathway. In 2025–26 the binding constraint is demand and the destruction of on-ramps. CM's computation on v1 tags found two islands (field/science vs statecraft/human systems) with no above-chance links between them, and three-dimensional craft blends too coarse to separate destinations (31% of family pairs with cosine ≥ .95) [E: CM computed; single-analyst tags].

## Decision

1. **Operating theory of change** (CM `logic_model.md`, P-CM-01): *blends made visible in gate instruments, plus reduced frictions on demand-backed pathways, increase cross-actor movement and knowledge retention, and so protect environmental work where it is co-delivered or devolved.* The charter sentence remains the north star. Pre-register 10–15 pathways and the failure condition before the pilot starts.
2. **Product strategy = visibility in gate instruments** (P-CM-02): exports into PDs, class specs, postings, USA Hire/SME-QA content, IDPs and learning records take priority over browse-only views; an **artifact registry** records which partner artifacts embed which EMST IDs and when (M-08, M-18).
3. **Similarity basis:** importance-weighted, **asymmetric task overlap** between role variants, shown to users as **directional coverage** ("you already hold 42% of this role's core work"); **pathway score = similarity × (1 − friction penalty) × local demand**, every component inspectable (P-CM-04). Competency overlap is used for *explanation*; craft blends for *describing and designing* pathways (ADR-0005); place-boundness separates portable field method from place-bound knowledge as a transfer cost, not a barrier (P-FC-19).
4. **Friction register is first-class** (P-CM-05): `FrictionRecord` on an actor pair and a role-variant pair with `frictionType`, `direction`, `severity ∈ {blocking, costly, minor}`, `basis ∈ {statute, regulation, policy, practice}`, `movableBy`, `mitigation`, evidence label, source and `asOf`. Minimum types (CM §7 Q2): qualification gate; assessment format; licence/certification and reciprocity; appointment type and status; pay system and level mapping (incl. **pay direction** — federal-to-consulting moves are often pay cuts at the median [E: PS-015]); retirement and benefits portability; veterans' preference; security, suitability and citizenship; geography and housing; seasonality and application windows; credential recognition; post-employment and ethics restrictions; bargaining-unit seniority; information and network access; plus SL's pay ratios, list/status gates, residency, hiring time, and missing sign-off authority (ADR-0017). Tribal frictions are not defined by CM; the Tribal Council decides what, if anything, is recorded. **Owner: CM**, with FH, SL, PS and DB contributing.
5. **Recommendation is gated.** No pathway is labelled "recommended" until it passes CM's M-03 (AUC ≥ 0.70 against observed moves and beating naive baselines by ≥ 0.05); until then the pathway view is **exploratory**, and the defaults are translation and gap views (P-CM-03; answers CM open Q4).
6. **Validation data:** the 2025–26 displacement is the first validation dataset — aggregate counts of hires with prior federal service by former series and new class from partner states and the federal-to-state placement partnership; an OPM EHRI research agreement explored via FH (P-CM-12); stored only as aggregates (ADR-0012).
7. **Expansion gates** (P-CM-11): **G1** (NGO pilot → federal/state): "does this help me?" ≥ 70% overall and ≥ 60% in every stratum (panel veto), ≥ 80% task completion, SUS ≥ 68, EMST IDs in a real gate artifact at ≥ 50% of pilot orgs, pathway view validated or labelled exploratory, friction register complete for pre-registered pathways, 508/WCAG and privacy clearance, ≥ 2 federal and ≥ 3 state partner agreements, zero misuse, panel ratification in every stratum — plus AS's G1a–d (cohort retention, willingness to continue without full stipend, governance uptake, 24-month funding horizon). **G2** (→ whole sector): credible contribution story for higher cross-actor hire rates; movers' time-to-productivity and retention at parity; no widening of equity gaps; EMST IDs in live artifacts at ≥ 2 federal agencies and ≥ 5 states with ≥ 80% crosswalk coverage; no entry role on a pilot pathway below the formation-task threshold without mitigation; adopters in ≥ 5 actor types (Tribal participation never a target); ≥ 5 years of stewardship funding; quarterly signal refresh.
8. **Leading indicators from day one:** entry-cohort volume (seasonal, corps, apprentice and trainee starts vs 2024; a fall > 25% triggers a field-pathway warning), posting-language portability, degree-only screens on target pathways (M-20, M-26, M-27).
9. **Mobility without changing employer** is surfaced where verified as current (IPA assignments, co-delivery agreements, NWCG qualification portability, corps and veteran hiring authorities) (P-CM-14).
10. **Families:** v1 family-level similarity is retired as a deliverable and kept only as a baseline prior and tagging diagnostic (P-CM-13); job families survive as navigation groupings of role profiles (ADR-0006; answers CM open Q7).

## Rationale

Task overlap is the grain at which evidence-backed transferability methods (RCA weighting, asymmetry, validation) and O*NET-linked flow data operate; Australia shipped two unvalidated similarity tools that were later retired [E: CM-022, CM-023]. Field craft is the hardest blend to enter (7.6% vs 15.9% Core coverage by direction) and the field → statecraft gap is dominated by one teachable bundle (D5.2 collaboration) [E: CM computed] — hence LC/CM's bridge kits (P-CM-06).

## Dissent and trade-offs

- **CM vs charter wording:** a narrower promise in exchange for credibility with adopters and funders who will ask for outcome evidence.
- **CM vs CA "crafts as profiles":** agreed for description; craft blends are not the mobility engine.
- **Panel veto vs CA authority:** reconciled by the written-override procedure (ADR-0009).
- **Speed vs evidence:** a fast track limited to translation and crediting (ADR-0014); recommendation waits for M-03.
- **PS:** the honest message ("private moves may cost you pay unless you take on sign-off or revenue responsibility") is less attractive for adoption than "mobility creates opportunity"; honesty chosen.

## Introduction

Manage forest stands and landscapes toward stated objectives through measured, prescribed and administered treatments. Professional role that inventories forests, diagnoses forest health, prescribes and implements silvicultural treatments, plans harvests and management, and prepares and administers timber sale, stewardship and partner agreements on public or private forest land.

The Forester is not one of EMST's 13 corridor roles. It is shown because EMST v1 included a Forester position description prototype, which this regenerated version replaces.

## Major duties

### 1. Inventory forest resources and assess forest conditions (___%)

Measure tree diameter, height, defect and age on inventory plots following the cruise or inventory protocol. Estimate stand attributes by combining field plots with airborne lidar, deciding where additional plots are needed to meet precision targets. Check-cruise a crew's inventory plots by blind re-measurement, recording differences outside the protocol tolerance. Survey stands for insect, disease and abiotic damage, recording agent, severity and extent on survey plots. Diagnose the cause of tree decline from field symptoms, signs and site history, sending samples for laboratory confirmation. Project stand development under alternative treatments with a growth model, reporting each projection's assumptions and uncertainty. Classify a stand's development stage from field structure data to anticipate its response to treatment.

### 2. Prescribe and implement silvicultural and fuels treatments (___%)

Write a silvicultural prescription for a stand from inventory data, site conditions and stated management objectives. Mark trees for a thinning or regeneration harvest to the prescription's residual density, species and structure targets. Audit a marked stand against its prescription by sampling residual basal area, spacing and leave-tree selection. Assess fuels in a planning area with standard fuel models and field sampling to set treatment priorities.

### 3. Plan forest management and timber harvests to meet ecological and economic objectives (___%)

Prepare a forest management plan that schedules harvests and treatments to meet ecological, economic and regeneration objectives. Lay out harvest unit boundaries, leave areas and skid trails on the ground to the plan's specifications and best management practices. Inspect active harvest operations for compliance with best management practices for water quality and soil protection.

### 4. Prepare and administer timber sale, stewardship and partner implementation agreements (___%)

Translate a silvicultural or restoration prescription into a sale layout and contract specifications that a purchaser can bid on. Appraise timber value for a sale or stewardship contract from cruise volume, product mix, logging costs and market data. Inspect contract operations against specifications, enforcing contract remedies when the purchaser does not comply.

### 5. Related duties (___%)

Performs related work drawn from neighboring occupational areas:

- **Identify organisms from field characters, specimens, images and recordings.** Identify regional plants and animals to species with dichotomous keys and diagnostic features, collecting evidence for any record not identified with certainty *(formation task)*. Verify species records submitted by crews or volunteers against attached photos, recordings or vouchers before they enter the program database.
- **Ground-truth remote-sensed, modelled and AI-generated outputs in the field.** Decide where field calibration plots are still needed for a lidar or imagery-based inventory, targeting conditions where remote estimates fail. Record reference observations at pre-selected validation points with the reference protocol, without access to the product's predicted value *(formation task)*.
- **Conduct environmental impact review of proposed actions.** Prepare an environmental assessment of a proposed action, analyzing the reasonably foreseeable effects of the action and its alternatives within the procedure's page and time limits. Scope the review of a proposed action with cooperating agencies and the public, setting the purpose, alternatives and effects boundaries the analysis will cover *(formation task)*.
- **Assess effects on protected species and conduct interagency consultation on proposed actions.** Compile species and habitat occurrence information for an action area, documenting the sources and survey dates used for each listed species.
- **Prepare and revise management plans for publicly held lands.** Coordinate an interdisciplinary team drafting a forest, range or refuge management plan, assigning plan components and reconciling conflicting resource objectives. Follow the agency's planning procedure and schedule for a plan revision or amendment, documenting each required step and its completion.
- **Assess tree risk and structural condition.** Verify remote-sensed or analytics-flagged tree risk scores on the ground, recording each override with the field reason.

*Note for the classifier:* several related duties (for example species identification, environmental impact review and protected-species consultation) are typical of other series. Confirm that each belongs in a 0460 position before keeping it.

## Factor 2 — Supervisory controls

*Level: [classifier].*

Plans own approach to non-routine work; review is for policy fit, not method. The supervisor sets objectives, priorities and deadlines; completed prescriptions, cruises and sale packages are reviewed for conformance with plans and policy.

## Factor 3 — Guidelines

*Level: [classifier].*

Adapts methods where guidance is incomplete or conflicting; documents deviations. Guidelines include the land management plan, agency silvicultural and timber-sale manuals and handbooks, inventory protocols, contract specifications and the agency's environmental review procedures. For AI-assisted and remotely sensed products, the incumbent calibrates trust in specific tools across conditions; decides when not to use them; reviews others' AI-assisted products.

## Factor 4 — Complexity

*Level: [classifier].*

Non-routine cases with multiple interacting variables and uncertain or conflicting data. Prescriptions must balance timber, habitat, fire risk, water and carbon objectives on stands whose structure, history and health vary; inventory designs must reconcile plot data with lidar and imagery products.

## Factor 5 — Scope and effect

*Level: [classifier].*

Project or site; persuades and negotiates with external parties on technical matters. The work determines how stands are treated and how sales are laid out and administered, which affects resource conditions for decades and the agency's contract and environmental compliance record.

## Factor 6 — Personal contacts

*Level: [classifier].*

Contacts are with other resource specialists, contract purchasers and operators, partner organizations, landowners and the public in the field and in meetings. They arise mainly in duty 4 and in the related duties.

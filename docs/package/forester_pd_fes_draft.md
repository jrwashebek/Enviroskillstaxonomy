## Introduction

Manage forest stands and landscapes toward stated objectives through measured, prescribed and administered treatments. Professional role that inventories forests, diagnoses forest health, prescribes and implements silvicultural treatments, plans harvests and management, and prepares and administers timber sale, stewardship and partner agreements on public or private forest land.

### 1. Prescribe and implement silvicultural treatments for stand objectives (14%)

Write a silvicultural prescription for a stand from inventory data, site conditions and stated management objectives. Mark trees for a thinning or regeneration harvest to the prescription's residual density, species and structure targets. Audit a marked stand against its prescription by sampling residual basal area, spacing and leave-tree selection.

### 2. Inventory forest resources to stated precision with field plots and remote sensing (13%)

Measure tree diameter, height, defect and age on inventory plots following the cruise or inventory protocol. Estimate stand attributes by combining field plots with airborne lidar, deciding where additional plots are needed to meet precision targets. Check-cruise a crew's inventory plots by blind re-measurement, recording differences outside the protocol tolerance.

Prepare a forest management plan that schedules harvests and treatments to meet ecological, economic and regeneration objectives. Inspect active harvest operations for compliance with best management practices for water quality and soil protection.

### 4. Prepare and administer timber sale, stewardship and partner implementation agreements (11%)

Translate a silvicultural or restoration prescription into a sale layout and contract specifications that a purchaser can bid on. Appraise timber value for a sale or stewardship contract from cruise volume, product mix, logging costs and market data. Inspect contract operations against specifications, enforcing contract remedies when the purchaser does not comply.

## Factor 2 — Supervisory controls

*Level: [classifier].*

Completes standard assignments without step-by-step direction; reviewed for results. At the upper end of the band: plans own approach to non-routine work; review is for policy fit, not method. ## Factor 3 — Guidelines

*Level: [classifier].*

Selects among established methods; interprets guidance for routine cases. At the upper end: adapts methods where guidance is incomplete or conflicting; documents deviations. Guidelines include the land management plan, agency silvicultural and timber-sale manuals and handbooks, inventory protocols, contract specifications and the agency's environmental review procedures. For AI-assisted and remotely sensed products, the incumbent selects tools for routine work; verifies outputs against independent evidence; documents acceptance or rejection.

## Factor 4 — Complexity

*Level: [classifier].*

Varied routine cases; recognizes non-routine cases and escalates. At the upper end: non-routine cases with multiple interacting variables and uncertain or conflicting data. Prescriptions must balance timber, habitat, fire risk, water and carbon objectives on stands whose structure, history and health vary; inventory designs must reconcile plot data with lidar and imagery products.

## Factor 5 — Scope and effect

*Level: [classifier].*

The work determines how stands are treated and how sales are laid out and administered, which affects resource conditions for decades and the agency's contract and environmental compliance record.

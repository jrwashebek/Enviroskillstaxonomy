## Part 1. Why now

### Slide 1. Title

**Headline:** How environmental jobs are changing, and what workforce leaders can do in 2027.

**Body:**
- In 2025–26, personnel and hiring decisions and new rules changed federal environmental work faster and more visibly
  than software did [J]. The entry rungs that form judgment may be what is now at risk (a hypothesis, slide 11)
- EMST v2: an open, task-level description of environmental work for hiring, development and workforce planning
- Built for three mandates: merit and skills-based hiring, mission readiness, knowledge retention
- What follows separates checked outside evidence from EMST's own drafts, which no person has yet reviewed

**Speaker notes:** This briefing has two kinds of content and we keep them apart. Outside facts were checked by AI
agents on 23 October 2026 against primary or traceable sources, and each has a citation. EMST's descriptions of roles
and tasks are drafts written by AI agents; no job analysis or expert panel has reviewed them, and we say so on every
slide that uses them. EMST takes no position on the merits of any law, rule, executive action or budget; it records
their status and suggests workforce practices that hold under every scenario it considered. License: this deck CC BY
4.0; EMST data CC0 1.0.

### Slide 2. Three mandates converge in 2026–27

**Headline:** Skills-based merit hiring, mission readiness after a 12% federal workforce drop, and knowledge walking out
of the door now share one prerequisite: a credible description of the work.

**Body:**
- **Merit hiring:** since 30 September 2025, under OPM's Merit Hiring Plan, self-assessment questionnaires may be used
  only to screen for minimum qualifications, not to rate and rank, except for seasonal positions, positions at GS-4 and
  below and other OPM-authorized cases. In FY2025, 91.23% of delegated-examining announcements relied solely on
  self-report questionnaires [100]
- **Technical assessments:** Natural Resources Management is in Tier 2 of OPM's Chance to Compete Act plan, with no Tier
  2 completion date; the plan's governmentwide target for full implementation is the end of FY2028, which is not a date
  for natural resources specifically [100]
- **Mission readiness:** the federal civilian workforce fell from 2,313,216 to 2,035,344 between September 2024 and
  January 2026, a drop of about 12% [2]
- **Knowledge retention:** the roughly 2,000 EPA employees who left at the end of 2025, mostly through deferred-
  resignation retirements, had a median 30.3 years of service, against 10.8 years for those who stayed [3]

**Speaker notes:** Job analysis is still the legal anchor. 5 CFR 300.103 requires each employment practice to rest on a
job analysis and a rational relationship to performance, even after OPM's 31 July 2026 interim final rule removed
references to the Uniform Guidelines (UGESP) from OPM's hiring regulations [13]; UGESP itself remains codified [52].
EMST never describes itself as meeting UGESP. OPM had published one competency-based classification and qualification
standard (for 2210) as of 23 October 2026 [101]. EMST is designed to feed that work, not compete with it. OPM issued a
memo titled "Final Regulations on Reduction in Force" on 2 September 2026; EMST has read only its title and date, and
EMST profiles must not be used to define competitive levels or make retention decisions [117]. Federal vacancies now
need Strategic Hiring Committee approval, and agencies file annual staffing plans with quarterly updates under EO 14356;
the order sets no hiring ratio [28]. The ~317,000 figure for 2025 departures is the OPM Director's own tally of
departures, not a net change and not a PPS figure; do not combine the two counts [25].

### Slide 3. The federal capacity contraction followed personnel and hiring decisions

**Headline:** The 2025–26 fall in federal environmental staff followed personnel, hiring and grant decisions, not AI.
Appropriations fell far less than staffing, and most of the decline came before the FY2026 spending bill.

**Body:** Chart **F01**: dot plot of percentage change by unit, each row with its own period and base.
- The Forest Service and the National Park Service each lost more than a quarter of their employees, and federal park
  rangers fell 34.1% (September 2024 – January 2026) [2]
- EPA had 12,849 employees at the end of January 2026, 24% fewer than a year earlier [3]
- The sources tie the decline to deferred resignations and retirements [3], hiring controls [28] and grant terminations
  [34]. FY2026 Interior–Environment appropriations, enacted 23 January 2026, were 1.9% below FY2025 [32]

**Speaker notes:** Bases differ by row and must not be read as one series. The Forest Service and Park Service rows are
lower bounds, because the source says "more than a quarter". These are federal figures only: they do not describe
state, local, NGO or private workforces, and they say nothing about Tribal workforces, which EMST does not measure. AI's
employment effect in these occupations has not been measured, and in federal headcount data the contraction would swamp
any AI signal [J]. The distinction matters because the remedies for a capacity contraction (protecting entry cohorts,
mobility, knowledge capture) differ from the remedies for automation.

## Part 2. What the evidence says

### Slide 4. Most registered environmental AI is science work, and most of it is not deployed

**Headline:** In federal environmental units, classical machine learning for science work is the largest type of
registered AI. Of the 347 use cases that report a stage, 61 are deployed, and none of the five in NEPA review or
permitting is.

**Body:** Charts **F02** and **F03**.
- Classical machine learning: 232 of the 329 use cases that report an AI type; 175 report no type [8]
- The leading functions are species and wildlife detection (89 use cases, 11 deployed), remote sensing (63, 11), water
  and hydrology (36, 10) and fire (31, 8) [8]
- NEPA, environmental review and permitting: 5 use cases, 0 deployed [8]
- Governmentwide, the 2025 inventory lists 3,611 use cases, 445 of them high-impact [8]

**Speaker notes:** Source: OMB 2025 Federal Agency AI Use Case Inventory (README as of 13 April 2026). EMST counted 504
use cases in eleven environmental units, nine bureaus and two USDA mission areas: the U.S. Geological Survey (187),
NOAA (157), USDA Natural Resources and Environment (76), EPA (29), USDA Farm Production and Conservation (20), the Fish
and Wildlife Service (11), the Bureau of Reclamation (8), the Bureau of Land Management (7), the Bureau of Safety and
Environmental Enforcement (6), the National Park Service (2) and the Bureau of Indian Affairs (1). "Deployed" means
development stage = deployed only, while OMB's README pools deployed and piloted, so do not mix the two. NOAA's records
report no type and no stage. The function families come from EMST's keyword classification and overlap. Entries are
agency self-reports: registration is not use. The 2026 inventory is expected around the turn of the year [8].

### Slide 5. AI tools are available at scale, and that is not the same as use or automation

**Headline:** Commercial AI tools are now licensed at workforce scale in the federal environmental departments.
Availability is not use, and no EMST change indicator has passed its evidence test.

**Body:** Table **F04**: license bands by department and use category; product names omitted.
- Commercial AI tools are licensed at 10,000 or more users in EPA (drafting, summarizing and search tools), the
  Department of the Interior (a writing tool) and USDA (a search and knowledge-retrieval assistant); the Interior and
  USDA licenses are department-wide, not limited to environmental staff [8]
- EMST moves a change indicator only when two independent families of evidence agree, the change persists or is
  deployed, the match is close, the driver matches and a named person has reviewed it
- **No indicator has moved for any of the 13 corridor roles** (F10)

**Speaker notes:** Language rules we hold ourselves to. The Anthropic Economic Index's "77% of API transcripts show
automation patterns" describes how developers call the model; it is not job or task automation [70]. BLS's new AI
exposure categories are four relative levels built partly from the same Eloundou, Anthropic and Microsoft data; they
are not forecasts and do not separate automation from augmentation [76]. EMST is built with an Anthropic model, the
agents that wrote this deck are that model, and EMST treats Anthropic usage data as one family among several; no
indicator may rest on it alone [24]. Occupation-level exposure index values are kept out of this deck until their
license terms are recorded [67].

### Slide 6. Rules changed substantially in 2025–26 ⟳

**Headline:** In 2025–26, EMST recorded 50 dated policy actions that changed or settled rules for review, authorization,
hiring, disclosure and entry pathways, and 6 more are scheduled for 2027. *Re-checked on: [date before presenting].*

**Body:** Chart **F09**: timeline by domain; recorded, scheduled and pending items marked in text.
- **Review:** CEQ's NEPA regulations removed (final, effective 8 January 2026), so each department follows its own

- **Pending, not law ⟳:** the SPEED Act passed the House and is pending in the Senate Environment and Public Works
  Committee [42, 112]. S. 5653, a separate bipartisan Senate bill, was unveiled 30 September 2026; as of 23 October no
  markup or floor vote had been reported [43]
- **Hiring and selection ⟳:** as of 23 October 2026, OFCCP's rescission of its EO 11246 regulations was scheduled to take
  effect on 26 October 2026 [53]. Colorado's SB 26-189 takes effect 1 January 2027; the Attorney General released draft
  rules on 6 October 2026, with a hearing scheduled for 26 October [54, 55]
- **Fire:** aligned U.S. Wildland Fire Service and Forest Service federal qualification standards take effect in January
  2027; NWCG separately updated PMS 310-1 in January 2026 [58]

**Speaker notes:** The timeline holds no technology-adoption data, so on its own it cannot show that policy moved faster
than technology; the report's statement that rules changed this work more visibly than any technology is a judgment
[J]. Other checked items on the timeline: the ESA "harm" definition rescission took effect 14 September 2026 and is
being litigated [45]. The Section 106 rewrite is under OIRA review and had not been published as of 9 October 2026 ⟳
[48]. The Forest Service project-level objection rule was published 13 October 2026 and is scheduled to take effect
12 November ⟳ [44]. OPM's 24 April 2026 list of series is **proposed** for realignment; never say "realigned" ⟳ [102].
There is no final WOTUS rule ⟳ [47]. FAA Part 108 (drone flights beyond visual line of sight) has been under OIRA review
since 10 July 2026 and was still pending on 23 October ⟳ [115]. California SB 253 first reports are due 10 November 2026
⟳ [14]. The New Jersey Supreme Court granted certification in July 2026 on the state's environmental justice rules,
which remain in effect pending its decision [113]. In July 2025 AmeriCorps told terminated grantees, including those in
states outside the litigation, that they were eligible for reinstatement [111]. Items EMST has not checked are left out.
Re-check every ⟳ item before presenting.

### Slide 7. Plans and projections: what they show and what they do not

**Headline:** Official projections show environmental technician and analyst occupations growing faster than average.
Newer forest plans appear to call for more monitoring, but that signal is weak, and a plan requirement is not a funded
job.

**Body:** Charts **F07** and **F05**.
- BLS 2025–35: environmental science and protection technicians +7%, environmental engineers +6.3%, environmental
  scientists +6%, against +3.5% for all occupations [61]
- Slower or declining: foresters +2.2%, forest and conservation workers −2%, water and wastewater operators −6% [61]
- In western national-forest documents, components typed as monitoring average 30.7% per 2012-rule document against
  2.7% per 1982-rule plan. Most of that gap comes from monitoring reports and stand-alone monitoring plans: among plan
  documents alone the shares are about 10% and 3%, and the intervals overlap [86]
- Whether agencies fund and staff the work plans call for is the open question [J]

**Speaker notes:** The plan data compare different documents written under different rules; they are not a time series.
About 95% of components come from Forest Service plans in western states, so do not generalize beyond western national
forests. The comparison uses 120 documents (84 under the 1982 rule, 36 under the 2012 rule); 17 of the 36 newer
documents are monitoring evaluation reports and 5 are stand-alone monitoring plans. Only 24 of the 84 older plans have
any component typed as monitoring, although the 1982 rule required monitoring, so the older baseline probably reflects
how monitoring was documented and extracted. And because the 2012 rule requires a monitoring program, a larger share
would partly restate the rule [86; J]. The claim that coordination "tripled" is a weighting artifact; with each plan
weighted equally only partnership touchpoints rise [86]. The faster/slower groups are EMST's convention (within one
point of the 3.5% average counts as average), not BLS categories. Projections are context, not evidence of skill
change, and BLS does not attribute these occupation projections to AI. Wildland firefighters are not counted separately
in the +4% firefighter projection [61].

## Part 3. How roles are changing

### Slide 8. Thirteen corridor roles

**Headline:** EMST v2's first 13 role profiles sit on three corridors. Each starts in field or NGO work, is designed to
cross field craft, data craft and statecraft, and ends in state and federal roles.

**Body:** Chart **F10**: three swimlanes; emerging roles outlined dashed; footer "change indicator: none active".
- **Field and data corridor:** corps member → field technician → monitoring and data technician, geospatial analyst,
  ground-truth and validation steward (emerging)
- **Fire corridor:** fire operations → fuels and prescribed fire specialist, fire intelligence analyst (emerging)
- **Stewardship and regulation corridor:** restoration project lead → environmental review, permit writing, compliance
  inspection → program manager
- Each role has a short brief. The explorer also holds earlier, lower-depth drafts of 18 more roles (among them forester,
  hydrologist and air-quality, drinking-water, wastewater, environmental-health and solid-waste roles) and six emerging
  roles [88]

**Speaker notes:** EMST's architect role (an AI agent) chose the corridors because they are designed to cross all three
crafts and end in recognizable state and federal positions, so the pilot can test whether pathways carry across
employers [88]. Role content is an agent draft: no person has reviewed the tasks or the draft targets. Tribal content is
held and not shown. No craft-blend or exposure ratings are charted, because all are agent drafts awaiting assessment by
several independent human raters. Air, water, wastewater, drinking-water, waste and environmental-health roles have no
briefs yet.

### Slide 9. Hypothesis: the new work is checking

**Headline:** EMST's draft role profiles, written by AI agents, expect the same new work in every corridor: checking AI
and automated outputs. The pilot will test whether practitioners see it.

**Body (agent drafts; every row "assessment pending"):**

| Role | Could get quicker (agent draft) | Expected new work (agent draft) |
|---|---|---|
| Field resource technician | Detection effort moves to stations and classifiers | Accept, correct or reject AI-suggested identifications; field-check AI condition maps |
| Geospatial analyst | Classification production | Transfer-test pretrained models locally; catch circular validation; review AI-written code line by line |
| Environmental review specialist | Drafting of analysis sections | Verify AI-drafted sections against the record; batch-review automated exclusion screening |
| Permit writer | First drafts of permits and fact sheets | Review AI-drafted permits; confirm or override automated completeness checks |
| Compliance inspector | Inspection report drafting | Confirm or dismiss automated anomaly flags before referral |
| Fire intelligence analyst | Hand-worked spread predictions (models now run continuously) | Adjudicate automated detections before resources are ordered |

**Speaker notes:** Source: EMST's draft role-change views [85]. They are agent drafts, hypotheses for the pilot and a
job-analysis survey to test, not findings. The drafts were written by agents that had read the same evidence, so their
agreement with it is not independent confirmation. What the outside evidence does show is availability without
deployment: drafting tools are licensed at scale (F04), while no review or permitting AI is registered as deployed (F03)
[8]. The briefs describe each task and the drivers behind each expected change; they show no time shares.

### Slide 10. What stays with people is accountable judgment

**Headline:** In the draft role views, what stays with people is the judgment someone answers for, on the ground and on
the record.

**Body (descriptive, from the agent-drafted role views; not a claim that this cannot change):**
- **On the ground:** reading a restoration site's response; the go/no-go decision from a test fire; findings of fact in
  an inspection; confirming an invasive detection
- **On the record:** deriving permit limits from standards; the significance determination; the reasoned explanation
  linking evidence to each choice; frank written advice
- **Over the tools:** deciding when a model is fit for a use, and when to restrict or retire it

**Speaker notes:** These are the tasks the agent-drafted views keep with people, and the reasons the drafts give; the
description is not normative. The fit with law matters for HR. OPM's 27 August 2026 memo treats AI drafting of position
descriptions as generally not high-impact when the responsible official independently reviews, revises and adopts the
draft; adopting a draft without substantive, documented review is to be treated as high-impact, and rating and ranking
are presumed high-impact [74]. Deference doctrine after *Seven County* rewards documented agency reasoning [12].

### Slide 11. The entry-path problem (a hypothesis)

**Headline:** Hypothesis: the tasks through which juniors form judgment are being cut by personnel and hiring decisions
and could be compressed by tools at the same time. If so, tomorrow's seniors may not be able to tell when AI is wrong.

**Body:** Table **F08**: one row per indicator, each with its own unit and scope.
- The Forest Service hired no non-fire seasonal staff in 2025 and committed to 2,000 seasonals for 2026, per a
  congressional letter relaying agency statements [4]
- On one job board, paid conservation internship postings fell 43.8% and AmeriCorps postings 42% (March–September
  2025) [33]
- Under-30 share of the federal civilian workforce: 8.9% → 7.9% (September 2024 – January 2026) [2]
- **Mechanism:** the same entry tasks (identification, plot work, first drafts, comment coding) are where tools could
  compress repetition. **Evidence status:** hypothesis; untested in environmental work. Evidenced so far only for entry
  jobs in federal land agencies and conservation corps

**Speaker notes:** This is the career-ladder hypothesis, shown with its mechanism and evidence status, not a result. The
closest outside benchmark is descriptive and not environmental. In ADP payroll data, employment of 22–25-year-olds in the
most AI-exposed occupations was about 19% below the trend for less-exposed occupations as of June 2026, in the authors'
August 2026 update; the original 2025 paper reported 13%, and the update revises that vintage to 15%. The gap works
through reduced hiring; it shrinks when education is controlled for, some of the differing trends began before
generative AI, and ADP under-covers government [77]. Do not cite it as causal. For corps, grant status matters: an
11 September 2026 AmeriCorps settlement with 23 state attorneys general and two governors covers FY2026 in those
jurisdictions only, and programs elsewhere have no equivalent protection [34, 111]. EMST's design response is the
formation provisions in its exploratory pathways: unaided work before AI assistance is credited, such as 50
identifications checked by an experienced observer, two effects sections drafted unaided, and one comment set of 200+
coded by hand. The quotas are EMST's curriculum draft, for the pilot to calibrate [93; J]. The pilot pre-registers the
test.

## Part 4. Four futures and the moves that hold in all of them

### Slide 12. Four plausible futures (agent draft)

**Headline:** EMST's foresight scenarios cross two uncertainties: whether public stewardship capacity is rebuilt, and
whether AI earns trust in environmental decisions. The four results call for different bets but share one set of moves.

**Body (2×2; cell text as in the flagship report):**

| | AI trusted and assured | AI brittle and contested |
|---|---|---|
| **Capacity rebuilt and coordinated** | **Assured Augmentation.** Headcount near 2024 levels, different mix: fewer people producing documents, more specifying and reviewing AI-assisted analysis, collecting ground truth and running continuous intelligence. Field craft becomes the bottleneck. Quiet risk: validation without formation. | **Human Premium.** After failures or court remands, AI is confined to search, summary and quality checks. Experienced reviewers, field ecologists and fire leaders command a premium; throughput is slow. |
| **Capacity thin and fragmented** | **Platform Stewardship.** A small, senior public core procures, oversees and signs; AI-leveraged consultancies and platforms deliver. Risk: a hollow state that cannot judge vendor output. | **Patchwork Retreat.** Junior roles are cut on the assumption that AI will carry the load; AI under-delivers; fire absorbs what capacity remains. Highest risk of irreversible loss: fire leadership, place-based judgment, taxonomic skills, long-term monitoring records. |

**Speaker notes:** EMST's foresight agent drafted these scenarios for 2026–2035; no human foresight panel has reviewed
them, and they are not forecasts [15; J]. Treated as given in all four: more fire and climate-hazard workload, expert
retirements, regulatory swings between administrations, and continued private demand for greenhouse gas accounting. One
checked workload marker: as of 11 October 2026 the season had reached 63,884 fires and 9.0 million acres, 133% and 145%
of the 2016–2025 average [57]. EMST's foresight review found no court ruling on an AI-assisted environmental record as of
October 2026 (a not-found result, not a systematic docket search), so the Human Premium trigger events remain
speculative [S].

### Slide 13. Eight no-regret moves (desk analysis)

**Headline:** In EMST's desk analysis, eight moves hold up in all four scenarios. No practitioner workshop has reviewed
them yet.

**Body:**
1. Protect a minimum entry cohort and its learning tasks, with entry work redesigned so juniors still produce,
   field-check and defend analyses with AI
2. Run a tacit-knowledge capture and succession sprint for retirement-eligible experts in high-tacit, place-bound
   functions
3. Make AI validation a cross-cutting requirement for anyone who uses AI output, with a small model-risk function
4. Treat ground truth (reference plots, labeled datasets, calibrated field assessors) as infrastructure
5. Build skills-based mobility channels that translate federal experience into state, local, NGO and private terms; use
   details and intergovernmental personnel assignments
6. Strengthen informed-buyer capacity: public data rights, model auditability and exit clauses in AI and data contracts
7. Invest in intergovernmental agreement craft, and fund partner capacity alongside any devolution
8. Stand up horizon scanning and a sentinel network linked to workforce plans

**Speaker notes:** From EMST's foresight analysis, a desk exercise by an AI agent [15; J]. Contingent moves wait for
their triggers. Examples: scale AI-augmented review teams only after two or more appellate decisions uphold AI-assisted
records *and* environmental-series headcount is stable for two quarters; train drone program leads for flights beyond
visual line of sight only once FAA Part 108 is published; fund fire-intelligence cohorts once agencies ingest satellite
fire-detection data on known access terms. Acting too early has stated risks. The analysis judged cutting entry roles
first with AI the most fragile strategy, but that verdict partly follows from how the scenarios are defined, since
Patchwork Retreat assumes exactly that cut followed by AI under-delivering. No person has checked that adopters can act
on every move, so present them as options [J].

## Part 5. What EMST v2 is and how to use it

### Slide 14. What EMST v2 is

**Headline:** EMST v2 is an open, versioned description of environmental work at task level. It is built to go into
the documents that gate hiring and development, not to sit on a shelf.

**Body:**
- **Roles and variants** by employer type (federal, state, local, NGO, private, academic), with tasks and competencies,
  all agent drafts until people review them. Fields for time share, criticality and entry needs stay empty in public
  views until a job analysis supplies them
- **Generated HR views:** a Federal Series Profile per OPM series and a State Class View per mapped class, each labeled
  "not a classification basis / not a job analysis / not for RIF or rating decisions"
- **Change and pathway views:** what is expected to change, with evidence status, and exploratory pathways with
  formation provisions
- **Open and private by design:** CC0 data, a static explorer that stores no personal data, an accessibility target of
  WCAG 2.2 AA

**Speaker notes:** North star (charter): "If those blends are visible and portable, mobility follows." Product strategy:
exports into position descriptions, class specifications, postings, assessments, development plans and learning records
take priority over browse-only views. The evidence behind that strategy is that announcing skills-based policy does not
by itself change who is hired. In a Burning Glass Institute and Harvard Business School study of private firms that
removed degree requirements, about 37% of those firms accounted for nearly all of the change in actual hiring, and the
change reached fewer than 1 in 700 hires in 2023 [97]. Colorado officials say about 80% of state job classifications now
emphasize skills over degrees, yet about 25% of 2024 hires in those classifications were people without degrees,
roughly the same share as in 2023; 2023 is the first year of data, after the reforms began, so this cannot show whether
reclassification had an effect [98]. WCAG 2.2 AA covers the Section 508 (WCAG 2.0 AA) and ADA Title II (WCAG 2.1 AA)
standards; Title II compliance dates are 26 April 2027 and 26 April 2028 [D1, D2].

### Slide 15. Federal HR: tomorrow morning

**Headline:** Federal HR teams can start job analysis and position description work from EMST task lists instead of a
blank page, without using EMST to select anyone.

**Body:**
- **0400 Kit (0401 and 0404 first):** Federal Series Profiles, competency models, position description language by grade
  band, and task-level crosswalks from series **proposed** for realignment [102]
- **Job-Analysis Starter Pack:** a task inventory and a matrix-sampled expert survey for one Tier 2 natural-resources
  series, run by your talent team on 3–5 vacancies
- **Forester PD in FES format:** an example regenerated from EMST data, labeled "DRAFT — SUBJECT TO CLASSIFICATION
  REVIEW", with percentages of time, factor levels and grade left to the supervisor and the classifier
- **Guardrails:** no selection use without your own job analysis [13]; never "UGESP compliance"; series shown under
  current codes until OPM decides; no EMST profile used for competitive levels or retention in a reduction in force

**Speaker notes:** These are planned packages with potential design partners; none is committed. The Forester PD gives
no percentages of time, criticality or needed-at-entry flags; the supervisor and the job analysis set those. AI-assisted
drafting of a PD is generally not high-impact under OPM's 27 August 2026 memo when the official reviews, revises and
adopts it; adoption without substantive, documented review is to be treated as high-impact [74]. Since 30 September
2025, self-assessment questionnaires may be used only to screen for minimum qualifications, not to rate and rank,
except for seasonal positions, positions at GS-4 and below and other OPM-authorized cases [100]; where the rule applies,
agencies need job-analysis-based technical assessments, and EMST supplies the task frame for those analyses. Check with
your labor-relations office before changing PDs on the basis of EMST drafts. OPM's 2 September 2026 RIF memo is cited by
title and date only [117].

### Slide 16. States, NGOs and educators: the same task language

**Headline:** States, NGOs and educators can crosswalk, credit and train against the same task language that federal
teams use, so experience can be recognized when people change employers.

**Body:**
- **States:** State Class Views for classes in the sample of state class plans EMST read (no agency has been involved);
  a federal-to-state crosswalk EMST proposes to test with a state partner. Pennsylvania's Office of Administration, for
  example, has identified federal job titles whose experience may count as equivalent for certain Commonwealth titles,
  and reports 1,350 hires with federal work experience since March 2025 [37]
- **Not yet covered:** air, water, wastewater, drinking-water, waste and environmental-health roles have only
  lower-depth drafts and no briefs; check the status of any EPA-provided training your staff rely on [J]
- **NGOs:** cite EMST role and competency identifiers in grant reports and in a corps member's exit portfolio held on
  the member's own device
- **Educators and trainers:** map course outcomes to EMST tasks and competencies; keep field ground-truth work and
  unaided work before AI-assisted credit in data- and AI-heavy programs. Workforce Pell can fund 150–599 clock-hour
  programs; governors, with state workforce boards, identify the in-demand occupations [103]

**Speaker notes:** The gap check runs in the browser: ratings stay in a file the member holds, and nothing about an
individual is sent to EMST. EMST's AI-literacy content maps to the Department of Labor framework's five content areas:
Understand AI Principles, Explore AI Uses, Direct AI Effectively, Evaluate AI Outputs and Use AI Responsibly (TEN
07-25, 13 February 2026) [94]. WIOA has not been reauthorized; H.R. 8210 was ordered reported 19–14 with no floor vote
found [103]. EMST claims no alignment with O*NET, ESCO, OPM, state or accreditation frameworks until a reviewed mapping
set exists.

## Part 6. The offer, the limits and the ask

### Slide 17. The offer, and what EMST cannot yet claim

**Headline:** EMST plans a 2027 NGO pilot and a federal–state design-partner track in parallel. It is explicit about what
EMST v2 cannot yet claim and how it would earn each claim.

**Body:**
- **NGO pilot (March 2027 – February 2028):** about 12 organizations (range 10–14) across the three corridors, for
  describing and developing work only; a go/no-go decision on wider rollout in March 2028. **Not yet funded:** pilot
  funding is being sought through January 2027, and participation is unfunded unless it is secured
- **What participants would give and get (current design):** a named lead with about 0.1 FTE of time (the design asks
  for ten months of it within the pilot year); in return, a kickoff workshop, monthly cohort calls, weekly office hours,
  peer pairing, a route to request roles EMST has not yet profiled, and a tiered stipend if funding is secured.
  A three-person panel would score applicants against the criteria in the pilot design, through an open call planned
  for November 2026 – January 2027
- **Design-partner track (January – December 2027):** potential federal and state partners would co-produce the 0400
  Kit, the Job-Analysis Starter Pack, a federal-to-state crosswalk and a fire crosswalk. None is committed
- **Not yet claimed:** every task value is an agent draft; every change is "assessment pending"; no change indicator has
  moved; pathways stay exploratory until they predict real career moves clearly better than simple guesses; values that
  raters do not agree on are never shown
- **Not used:** EMST v1 single-analyst statistics (methodology history only); occupation exposure index values (license
  pending); Tribal content (held until a Tribal Advisory Council constituted by Tribal governments exists and approves)

**Speaker notes:** No organization has committed. EMST's commitment register is the only source of committed status,
and it is empty; an organization is a partner only when a signed record is entered there [104]. The expansion decision
in March 2028 uses pre-set usefulness thresholds, with a veto on usefulness for a practitioner panel that is still to be
recruited, and requires
EMST identifiers to appear in real hiring or training documents at half or more of the pilot organizations. Reliability
studies, a modified Delphi panel and a matrix-sampled job-analysis survey are designed now and would run with people
from 2027. No October 2026 posting baseline exists, so EMST makes no claim about posting volumes or content beyond the
one-board figures cited. The Tribal outreach gate stays closed until reviewer compensation is funded and a Council exists;
EMST does not contact or speak for any Tribal nation.

### Slide 18. The ask

**Headline:** Three kinds of commitment in 2027 would let EMST test whether a shared description of the work helps people
move and helps agencies keep what they know.

**Body:**
- **Federal agencies:** a design-partner letter (a talent team runs the Job-Analysis Starter Pack on 3–5 vacancies;
  classification staff review the 0400 Kit)
- **States and workforce boards:** test a federal-to-state or class-specification crosswalk, and share aggregate
  placement counts (five or more people per cell)
- **NGOs, corps and educators:** a pilot agreement with a named lead (about 0.1 FTE); map one curriculum or season plan
- **Everyone:** correct the drafts. Practitioners are needed for expert panels, reliability studies and the Delphi

**Speaker notes:** EMST's own targets, not commitments: a federal design-partner letter by February 2027; three state
forestry or environmental agencies plus one workforce board by April 2027; an anchor partner plus 11 pilot agreements by
March 2027 [104]. EMST collects no personal data. Pilot outputs may not be used to rank, select or remove anyone, and any
such use is a misuse incident that pauses expansion. To comment, correct an error or ask about the pilot, open an issue
at [github.com/jrwashebek/Enviroskillstaxonomy/issues](https://github.com/jrwashebek/Enviroskillstaxonomy/issues).

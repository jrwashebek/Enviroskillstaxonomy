## Who does this work

Corps members work in supervised crews on public and private land. They build and maintain trails, install restoration and erosion-control treatments and control invasive species, all under safety protocols. For most people this is the first formation step in field craft, and it feeds agency technician roles.

## The work today (agent draft)

| Task | Share of time | Flags |
|---|---|---|
| Install native plantings and seedings to the treatment specification, selecting microsites | 8.9% | E F |
| Treat invasive plants by mechanical, chemical or biological methods, protecting non-target vegetation and water | 8.5% | E F |
| Construct trail tread to design grade, outslope and width, reading soils and drainage | 8.5% | E F |
| Maintain existing trails: clear drainage, brush corridors, repair tread | 8.5% | E |
| Use hand tools (grubbing hoes, axes, crosscut saws) with safe spacing and body position | 8.5% | C E F |
| Build low-tech process-based structures such as beaver dam analogues, placed from field reading of flow | 5.7% | F |
| Install check dams, wattles or silt fences, keyed and anchored | 5.7% | E F |

Safety tasks take little time but are all flagged critical. They include a job hazard analysis on the actual site, stop-work, recognising heat and smoke illness, remote first aid and emergency radio calls.

## What stays human, and why

This is a description of where the work sits today, not a claim that it cannot change. The draft codes almost every task in this role as *on-site required*: digging, planting, cutting, setting stone, choosing microsites and reading drainage all happen on the ground. The agent-drafted notes say the same of the tasks that are changing. Confirming and treating an invasive detection stays with the crew. So does checking an AI-drafted hazard analysis against the real site, and judging what is worth recording. Saw work and hazard judgment are learned through supervised repetition under an internal qualification.

## Entry paths and formation

People learn this work by doing it repeatedly under a crew lead. The draft flags plantings, invasive treatment, trail construction, hand tools and chainsaw felling, hazard analysis, navigation and first aid as formation tasks. Three things threaten that learning, and none of them is AI:

- **Unstable funding.** Corps programmes depend on grants, and the AmeriCorps settlement covers FY2026 only [V1-05].
- **Fewer openings.** On one board, AmeriCorps positions fell 42% and paid internships fell 43.8% (Mar–Sep 2025) [V1-14].
- **Thinner destinations.** The U.S. Forest Service hired no non-fire seasonal staff in 2025 and committed to 2,000 seasonals for 2026 [V1-04; WF-04]. The under-30 share of the federal civilian workforce fell from 8.9% to 7.9% (Sep 2024–Jan 2026) [V1-02; WF-02].

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **Corps member → field resource technician (R02; COR-1, new hire).** Five steps. Safety, first aid and navigation come before any data work. Members then collect data to protocol and make **50 unaided identifications checked by an experienced observer** before any AI identification aid is credited. Next come seeded-error drills on AI field products, and the pathway ends with a season capstone signed off on a task book. LC estimates about 240 hours over 12 months, mostly paid placement and mentor time [J].
- **Corps member → wildland fire operations (R06; COR-2, new hire).** Safety culture first. Fireline and saw work are evaluated on qualifying assignments, members make 10 hand calculations of fuel moisture and fire danger, and they ignite under a firing boss. LC estimates about 200 hours over 12 months [J]. USWFS and USFS aligned federal qualification standards take effect in January 2027. NWCG is not a party [V3-13; WF-10].
- Prior learning the pathways credit: wilderness first aid; wildland fire position qualifications.

Neither pathway has been tested against observed moves (ADR-0023 §5). Source: `docs/build/WP-07/staging/pathways/seed-lc-pw-cor1-corps-to-field-technician.yaml`, `…cor2-corps-to-fire-operations.yaml`.

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor or crew lead.** Keep the hand-built formation tasks in every member's season. When a crew uses a template or AI-drafted hazard analysis, have them walk the site and amend it before work starts, and record what changed. Log near misses during the shift in which they happen.
- **Training officer.** Map your season plan to the L1 targets above, including the data and AI floor. Use the corps-to-technician sequence (safety first, unaided identification before AI aids) as a draft to calibrate in your programme. Record wilderness first aid and other recognised prior learning in members' own portfolios.
- **HR specialist at a destination employer.** Use the R02 and R06 State Class Views and Federal Series Profiles to see which corps tasks match your entry work. Treat them as a starting point for your own crediting plan, not as a qualification determination. For federal hiring, self-assessment questionnaires may not rate and rank candidates from September 30, 2025, with exceptions that include seasonal and GS-4-and-below positions [V1-09; FED-02].

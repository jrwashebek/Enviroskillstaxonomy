## Who does this work

Corps members work in supervised crews on public and private land. They build and maintain trails, install restoration
and erosion-control treatments and control invasive species, all under safety protocols. For most people this is the
first step in learning field craft, and it feeds agency technician roles.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Employer types** | **NGO:** nonprofit corps funded by grants and fees, delivering under contract. **State:** state-run or state-sponsored corps. **Local:** local and youth corps working for counties and municipalities. **Federal:** crews working on federal land under cooperative agreements; members are not federal employees. In every case the corps is a service provider. |
| **Typical titles** | Corps crew member; conservation crew member |
| **Federal series** | No matching series: corps members are program participants, not federal employees. EMST lists 0462 Forestry Technician, 0404 Biological Science Technician, 0458 Soil Conservation Technician and 0455 Range Technician only as series former members may move into, including through corps hiring authorities (EMST has not yet checked those authorities' current terms). 0455 is **proposed** for realignment to 0404; the proposal is not final [1]. |
| **State class examples** | Texas Fish and Wildlife Technician I, as a destination class (partial fit). No local class is mapped in the jurisdictions whose public class plans EMST read. |

## The work today (agent draft)

Main tasks, in rough work order. This is a selection; EMST shows no time shares.

| Task | Formation task |
|---|---|
| Use hand tools (grubbing hoes, axes, crosscut saws) with safe spacing and body position | yes |
| Fell trees with a chainsaw within certification limits | yes |
| Construct trail tread to design grade, outslope and width, reading soils and drainage | yes |
| Maintain existing trails: clear drainage, brush corridors, repair tread | |
| Install check dams, wattles or silt fences, keyed and anchored | yes |
| Build low-tech process-based structures such as beaver dam analogues, placed from field reading of flow | yes |
| Install native plantings and seedings to the treatment specification, selecting microsites | yes |
| Treat invasive plants by mechanical, chemical or biological methods, protecting non-target vegetation and water | yes |

Safety work runs through all of it: a job hazard analysis on the actual site, stop-work, recognizing heat and smoke
illness, remote first aid and emergency radio calls.

The outside evidence about this role concerns grant and hiring decisions, not AI. Context only: BLS projects
employment of forest and conservation workers to fall 2% over 2025–35 [3].

## What stays with people, and why

This describes where the work sits today, not a claim that it cannot change. The work happens on the ground: digging,
planting, cutting, setting stone, choosing microsites and reading drainage. In the agent-drafted notes, confirming and
treating an invasive detection stays with the crew. So does checking an AI-drafted hazard analysis against the real
site, and judging what is worth recording. Saw work and hazard judgment are learned through supervised repetition under
an internal qualification. The role draws mainly on field craft.

## Entry paths and formation

People learn this work by doing it repeatedly under a crew lead. The draft marks plantings, invasive treatment, trail
construction, hand tools and chainsaw felling, hazard analysis, navigation and first aid as formation tasks. Three
things threatened that learning in 2025–26, and none of them is AI:

- **Grant decisions.** In April 2025 AmeriCorps terminated grants that funded many conservation corps. A federal court
  enjoined the terminations in the 24 plaintiff states and DC on 5 June 2025, and programs there were reinstated. In
  July 2025 AmeriCorps also told grantees whose grants had been terminated, including programs in states that had not
  joined the litigation, that they were eligible for reinstatement; how many took it up is not documented. An
  11 September 2026 settlement with 23 state attorneys general and two governors covers FY2026 in those jurisdictions
  only; it does not cover programs elsewhere. The litigation is stayed until 1 February 2027 [4, 5].
  **Corps leads should check whether their state is in the coalition.**
- **Fewer openings.** On one job board, AmeriCorps positions fell 42% and paid internships 43.8% (March–September
  2025) [2].
- **Thinner destinations.** A congressional letter relaying Forest Service statements reports that the agency hired no
  non-fire seasonal staff in 2025 and in February 2026 committed to hiring 2,000 seasonals for 2026 [6]. The under-30
  share of the federal civilian workforce fell from 8.9% to 7.9% between September 2024 and January 2026 [7].

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **Corps member → field resource technician (new hire).** Five steps. Safety, first aid and navigation come before any
  data work. Members then collect data to protocol and make **50 unaided identifications checked by an experienced
  observer** before any AI identification aid is credited. Seeded-error drills on AI field products follow, and the
  pathway ends with a season capstone signed off in a task book. EMST's curriculum draft estimates about 240 hours over
  12 months, mostly paid placement and mentor time [J].
- **Corps member → wildland fire operations (new hire).** Safety culture first. Fireline and saw work are evaluated on
  qualifying assignments, members make **10 hand calculations** of fuel moisture and fire danger, and they ignite under
  a firing boss. About 200 hours over 12 months [J]. Aligned U.S. Wildland Fire Service and Forest Service federal
  qualification standards take effect in January 2027; NWCG separately updated its position-qualification standard
  (PMS 310-1) in January 2026 [8].
- Prior learning the pathways credit: wilderness first aid; wildland fire position qualifications.

Neither pathway has been tested against observed career moves.

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor or crew lead.** Keep the hand-built formation tasks in every member's season. When a crew uses a template
  or AI-drafted hazard analysis, have them walk the site and amend it before work starts, and record what changed. Log
  near misses during the shift in which they happen.
- **Training officer.** Compare your season plan with the draft competencies above, including the basic data and AI
  skills, and note where they are wrong for your crews. Use the corps-to-technician sequence (safety first, unaided
  identification before AI aids) as a draft to calibrate. Record wilderness first aid and other recognized prior
  learning in members' own portfolios.
- **NGO program lead.** Cite EMST role and competency identifiers in your next grant report and in each member's exit
  portfolio, so a state or federal hiring office can read what members did. Check whether your state is in the
  AmeriCorps settlement coalition, and remember that the settlement covers FY2026 only.
- **HR specialist at a destination employer.** Use the explorer's 0456 Wildland Fire Management Federal Series Profile
  (built from the wildland fire operations role) and its Texas Fish and Wildlife Technician I–III State Class Views
  (built from the field resource technician role) to see which corps tasks match your entry work; these are the only
  close-fit views for the destination roles in this release. Treat them as a starting point for your own crediting
  plan, not a qualification determination. For federal hiring: since 30 September 2025, under OPM's Merit
  Hiring Plan, self-assessment questionnaires may be used only to screen for minimum qualifications, not to rate and
  rank, except for seasonal positions, positions at GS-4 and below and other OPM-authorized cases. OPM's plan targets
  full governmentwide use of technical assessments by the end of FY2028; natural resources management is in a later
  tier with no date of its own [9].
- **Educator.** Use the corps-to-technician sequence as field-practicum design: safety and navigation first, a set
  number of unaided identifications checked by an experienced observer before any AI aid counts, and a season capstone
  signed off against a task book.

## Who does this work

Restoration project leads plan, permit, deliver and monitor ecological restoration and land stewardship projects. The
work includes reading site history and reference conditions, adapting treatments to site response, designing
monitoring, working with landowners and partners, and managing contracts, budgets and authorizations. The role combines
field craft (implementation), data craft (monitoring design) and statecraft (agreements and permits).

## The work today (agent draft)

Main tasks, in rough work order. This is a selection; EMST shows no time shares. The bundle is broad, which is typical of
a project role.

- Interpret fire scars, stumps, soil horizons and legacy structures to reconstruct site history
- Determine whether restoration to historical conditions is feasible, documenting the limits (senior, lead)
- Define monitoring questions, indicators and decision triggers
- Break a large project into phased work packages with decision points
- Write a contractor or partner scope of work in verifiable terms
- Conduct a joint field visit with a landowner or permittee
- Read site response after treatment (sediment capture, wetting, establishment, browse) at fixed photo points
- Adapt restoration techniques when monitoring shows the site is not responding as planned

## What stays with people, and why

This describes where the work sits today. The judgments that matter most are made on the ground: reading how a site
responded, deciding to change a technique, and judging whether a site has moved beyond recovery to historical
conditions. They depend on knowing the particular place, and people learn them by walking the same sites with an
experienced lead. Working with landowners and volunteers is relational and happens in person. Imagery adds evidence
between visits, but in the draft it does not replace the visit.

## Entry paths and formation

People reach this role from corps and field technician work. They carry implementation skill and learn site reading
through repeated, mentored visits to the same sites over seasons. When federal capacity and seasonal hiring contract
[2, 5], fewer experienced leads are available to mentor and fewer juniors walk sites with them. EMST's foresight
analysis (a desk exercise by an AI agent, not yet reviewed by a human panel) judges that a tacit-knowledge capture and
succession sprint for place-bound functions, with paired fieldwork and recorded field walks, holds up in all four of its
scenarios [J].

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **Out, reskill → environmental review specialist (the field-to-statecraft bridge):** five steps. The learner maps
  authorizations and pathways; drafts **two effects sections unaided** from the project record before reviewing
  AI-drafted sections is credited; runs two scoping meetings under a senior reviewer; codes **one comment set of at
  least 200 comments by hand** before machine-assisted coding is credited; and closes with a moot defense of a
  level-of-review decision before mock counsel. EMST's curriculum draft estimates about 160 hours over 9 months plus one
  live review [J]. Shadow-drafting on live reviews replaces the formation once supplied by drafting whole documents.
- **Out (role link, no pathway drafted yet):** to natural resource and environmental program manager.
- **In:** from conservation corps member and field resource technician.

None of these has been tested against observed career moves.

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor.** Pair junior staff with experienced leads on repeat visits to the same sites, and record field walks
  while the people who know the sites are still there. Ask for written predictions before treatment ("where will legacy
  conditions show up?") and compare them afterward.
- **Training officer.** Use the restoration-to-review bridge to grow review capacity from restoration staff, keeping the
  by-hand drafting quota. Add contract and agreement administration training, since delivery may move to partners.
- **NGO program lead.** Describe your crews' restoration, monitoring-design and partner work in grant reports and
  staff portfolios using EMST role and competency identifiers, so public employers can read what your staff can do.
- **HR specialist.** Read the explorer's 0401 Federal Series Profile for duty language, subject to classification
  review. For partner-delivered work, describe in position descriptions the contract-oversight duties that remain
  in-house, and keep acceptance-criteria authority with a named role.
- **Educator.** Use the field-to-review bridge as a capstone design: two effects sections drafted unaided from a project
  record, and a comment set coded by hand, before any machine-assisted work counts.

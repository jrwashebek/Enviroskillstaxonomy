## Who does this work

Restoration project leads plan, permit, deliver and monitor ecological restoration and land stewardship projects. The work includes reading site history and reference conditions, adapting treatments to site response, designing monitoring, working with landowners and partners, and managing contracts, budgets and authorizations. The role combines field craft (implementation), data craft (monitoring design) and statecraft (agreements and permits).

## What stays human, and why

This describes where the work sits today. The tasks the draft marks critical are judgments made on the ground: reading how a site responded, deciding to change a technique, and judging whether a site has moved beyond recovery to historical conditions. They are coded *on-site required* or *on-site preferred*, *site-specific*, and highly dependent on mentoring. Working with landowners and volunteers is relational and happens in person. Imagery adds evidence between visits, but in the draft it does not replace the visit.

## Entry paths and formation

People reach this role from corps and field technician work (R01, R02). They carry implementation skill and learn site reading through repeated, mentored visits to the same sites over seasons. The draft flags site reading and treatment adaptation as highly mentorship-dependent. When federal capacity and seasonal hiring contract [V1-02; V1-04], fewer experienced leads are available to mentor and fewer juniors walk sites with them. FS's no-regret move NR-2 (a tacit-knowledge capture and succession sprint for place-bound functions, with paired fieldwork and recorded field walks) addresses this directly (`scenarios.md` §8.3, [J]).

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **Out, reskill → environmental review specialist (R10), the field-to-statecraft bridge:** five steps. The learner maps authorizations and pathways; drafts **two effects sections unaided** from the project record before reviewing AI-drafted sections is credited; runs two scoping meetings under a senior reviewer; codes **one comment set of at least 200 comments by hand** before machine-assisted coding is credited; and closes with a moot defence of a level-of-review decision before mock counsel. About 160 hours over 9 months plus one live review [J]. Shadow-drafting on live reviews replaces the formation once supplied by drafting whole documents.
- **Out (role link, no pathway authored yet):** to resource programme manager (R13).
- **In:** from corps member (R01) and field resource technician (R02).

None of these has been tested against observed moves (ADR-0023 §5).

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor.** Pair junior staff with experienced leads on repeat visits to the same sites, and record field walks while the people who know the sites are still there. Ask for written predictions before treatment ("where will legacy conditions show up?") and compare them after.
- **Training officer.** Use the R09-to-R10 bridge to grow review capacity from restoration staff, keeping the by-hand drafting quota. Add contract and agreement administration training, since delivery is moving to partners.
- **HR specialist.** Read the 0401 Federal Series Profile for duty language, subject to classification review. For partner-delivered work, describe in position descriptions the contract-oversight duties that remain in-house, and keep acceptance-criteria authority with a named role.

## Who does this work

Programme managers plan and justify budgets, brief decision-makers, manage land or regulatory programme plans and agreements, and track instrument and policy change. The draft also gives them a duty that is easy to miss: protecting the programme's capacity to form and keep skilled staff, including how AI tools are governed. This is where the corridor's statecraft, budgeting, workforce planning and formation of others come together.

## What stays human, and why

This describes where the work sits today. The draft's critical tasks are acts of accountability: a budget request answered before appropriators, a decision memo signed by an official, frank written advice when the evidence does not support a preferred option, and contingency plans that rank activities by legal requirement. Managers also decide **what juniors keep doing by hand**. The bundle includes analysing which entry-level tasks form judgment and which AI now performs, and advising leadership on what adopting a tool will do to formation *before* it is deployed.

## Entry paths and formation

People reach this role from senior specialist work in restoration, review, permitting or inspection (R09–R12). The formation of a manager used to come from long apprenticeship to another manager. The exploratory pathway substitutes **an acting assignment or rotation** for that apprenticeship [J]. As part of it, the learner writes a **supervisor formation plan for one entry role** in the unit. The threat is the same one the role is asked to manage: fewer experienced managers to learn from, and departures of very long-serving staff [V1-03]. FS's no-regret moves NR-1 (protect a minimum entry cohort) and NR-2 (a tacit-knowledge capture and succession sprint) are programme-manager decisions (`scenarios.md` §8.3, [J]).

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **In, senior specialist → this role (upskill):** six steps, covering public budgets and appropriations; formation of others; knowledge continuity before departures; delivering and funding work with partners; systems trade-offs, durable choices and briefing; and programme-level formation and safety-culture design. About 120 hours over 18 months plus a rotation or acting assignment [J]. The R10 route is shown, and R11 and R12 follow it with recognition of prior learning.
- **Out (watch only):** a knowledge-continuity lead role (R35) appears only in the explorer's "What's coming" view.

None of these has been tested against observed moves (ADR-0023 §5).

## What you can do now

- **Programme manager (for your own unit).** List the knowledge held by one or two people, rated by criticality and risk of loss, and schedule handovers and recorded field walks before departures. Before adopting an AI tool, write down which entry tasks it takes over and the alternative way juniors will learn that judgment. Keep a dated change notice for each new rule, guidance or court decision.
- **Training officer.** Set up acting assignments and rotations as the formation route for managers. Require each new manager to write a formation plan for one entry role.
- **HR specialist.** Use the 0340 Federal Series Profile, subject to classification review. In staffing plans submitted under EO 14356, name single-incumbent functions and entry positions that protect formation [V1-08]. Knowledge retention is a stated purpose of this package, so record what was captured, not who left.

## Who does this work

Program managers plan and justify budgets, brief decision-makers, manage land or regulatory program plans and
agreements, and track instrument and policy change. The draft also gives them a duty that is easy to miss: protecting
the program's capacity to form and keep skilled staff, including how AI tools are governed. This is where the
corridor's statecraft, budgeting, workforce planning and formation of others come together.

## The work today (agent draft)

Main tasks, in rough work order. This is a selection; EMST shows no time shares.

- Identify knowledge held by one or a few people, rating how critical it is and the risk of losing it
- Prepare a program budget request linking each increment to verifiable outputs and outcomes
- Reconcile obligations and expenditures against appropriations, agreements and fee accounts monthly
- Prepare continuing-resolution and funding-lapse contingency plans
- Coordinate an interdisciplinary team drafting a land management plan
- Write a two-page decision memo for an accountable official
- Brief a decision-maker orally under time pressure, stating where evidence is uncertain

## What stays with people, and why

This describes where the work sits today. The draft's central tasks are acts of accountability: a budget request
answered before appropriators, a decision memo signed by an official, frank written advice when the evidence does not
support a preferred option, and contingency plans that rank activities by legal requirement. Managers also decide
**what juniors keep doing by hand**. The bundle includes analyzing which entry-level tasks form judgment and which AI
tools can now perform, and advising leadership on what adopting a tool will do to formation *before* it is deployed.
The role draws mainly on statecraft.

## Entry paths and formation

People reach this role from senior specialist work in restoration, review, permitting or inspection. The formation of a
manager used to come from a long apprenticeship to another manager. The exploratory pathway substitutes **an acting
assignment or rotation** for that apprenticeship [J]. As part of it, the learner writes a **supervisor formation plan
for one entry role** in the unit. The threat is the same one the role is asked to manage: fewer experienced managers to
learn from, and departures of very long-serving staff [2]. Two of the moves EMST's foresight analysis (a desk exercise by
an AI agent, not yet reviewed by a human panel) judges robust in all four of its scenarios, protecting a minimum entry
cohort and running a tacit-knowledge capture and succession sprint, are program-manager decisions [J].

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **In, senior specialist → this role (upskill):** six steps, covering public budgets and appropriations; formation of
  others; knowledge continuity before departures; delivering and funding work with partners; systems trade-offs,
  durable choices and briefing; and program-level formation and safety-culture design. EMST's curriculum draft estimates
  about 120 hours over 18 months plus a rotation or acting assignment [J]. The route is drafted from environmental
  review; permit writers and inspectors follow it with recognition of prior learning.
- **Out (watch only):** a knowledge-continuity lead role appears only in the explorer's "What's coming" view.

None of these has been tested against observed career moves.

## What you can do now

- **Program manager (for your own unit).** List the knowledge held by one or two people, rated by how critical it is
  and the risk of losing it, and schedule handovers and recorded field walks before departures. Before adopting an AI
  tool, write down which entry tasks it would take over and the other way juniors will learn that judgment. Keep a
  dated change notice for each new rule, guidance or court decision.
- **Training officer.** Set up acting assignments and rotations as the formation route for managers. Require each new
  manager to write a formation plan for one entry role.
- **HR specialist.** Use the explorer's 0340 Federal Series Profile, subject to classification review. In staffing plans
  submitted under EO 14356, name single-incumbent functions and the entry positions that protect formation [4]. Record
  the knowledge captured, not personal details about who left. Do not use EMST profiles to define competitive levels or
  make retention decisions in a reduction in force; OPM issued a memo titled "Final Regulations on Reduction in Force"
  on 2 September 2026, of which EMST has read only the title and date [7].
- **Educator.** In program-management and policy courses, have students write a formation plan for one entry role and a
  knowledge-continuity plan for one single-incumbent function.

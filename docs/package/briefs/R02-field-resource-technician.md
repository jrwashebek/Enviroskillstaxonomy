## Who does this work

Field resource technicians collect vegetation, wildlife, water and soil data to protocol. They identify organisms, keep sample custody and records intact, and check remote and AI-generated products against the ground. It is the largest field entry role and the main place where people learn identification and measurement judgment.

## What stays human, and why

This describes where the work sits today. The draft codes almost every task *on-site required*. What the role adds is a defensible observation: the right plot, the right sample, a documented identification and an intact chain of custody. The agent-drafted notes put the new AI-related work on top of field skill rather than in place of it. Only someone who can identify a species can judge an AI's suggestion, and only someone at the site can say why a map and the ground disagree (timing, mixed pixels, hidden understory, location error).

## Entry paths and formation

People learn this work through seasons of supervised fieldwork: plots, surveys, identification, water sampling and navigation. The WP-07 provisions protect that learning by holding AI aids back until unaided skill is shown. In the corps-to-technician pathway, **50 unaided identifications** are checked by an experienced observer and **10 plots** are established under supervision before AI identification aids are credited. The threat is that seasonal and internship openings are scarce at the same time as automated detection compresses the repetitions people learn from [V1-04; V1-14]. FS's no-regret move NR-1 describes the same risk: protect a minimum entry cohort and redesign entry work so juniors still produce and field-check their own work (`scenarios.md` §8.3, [J]).

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **In:** from conservation corps member (R01; five steps, about 240 hours over 12 months [J]).
- **Out, upskill → monitoring and data technician (R03):** observed sensor installations; manual meter readings at every service visit as the reference for sensors; one month of automated flags reviewed by hand before automated flagging is credited. About 160 hours over 6 months [J].
- **Out, reskill → geospatial and remote sensing analyst (R04):** teaches only the gap between the two profiles and keeps a field calibration practicum at 20–30% of pathway time. Two reference tiles are interpreted by hand before automated classification is credited. About 320 hours over 9 months. A community-college certificate may fit Workforce Pell where a governor-approved programme exists [J; V3-03; SKL-02]. The pathway notes it "must not be used to stop hiring field entrants".
- **Out, upskill → ground-truth and validation steward (R05, emerging):** reference-data design, observer calibration, fitness-for-use decisions. About 200 hours over 12 months [J].

None of these has been tested against observed moves (ADR-0023 §5).

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor.** Keep unaided identification and plot work in every new technician's season before you introduce classifiers. When a crew accepts or rejects an AI identification, have them record the diagnostic reason. Use blind re-measurement of a crew sample as a routine check.
- **Training officer.** Build seeded-error drills (an identification set or a field product with planted errors) into the season. Tag modules on specific tools with an expiry date. Use the corps-to-technician and technician-to-monitoring sequences as drafts to calibrate.
- **HR specialist.** Compare your class specification or position description with the R02 State Class View or Federal Series Profile, and treat it as a starting point that is subject to classification review. For 0455 positions, plan around the series as it stands: realignment to 0404 is proposed, not decided [V1-10]. Any selection use needs your own job analysis [SEL-02].

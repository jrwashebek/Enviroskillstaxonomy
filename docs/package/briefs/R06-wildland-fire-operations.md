## Who does this work

The role runs from crew member to crew supervisor. It covers constructing and holding fireline, firing and mop-up, saw and hand-tool work, observing fire weather and behaviour, crew safety and emergency response, and, at senior levels, size-up, tactical direction and crew development. Position qualifications are credential gates, not EMST levels. EMST links to the fire qualification systems and does not compete with them.

## What stays human, and why

This describes where the work sits today. Almost everything in the bundle is coded *on-site required* and flagged as formation. Fireline, saw work, firing, mop-up, hazard trees and crew safety are learned through graduated exposure on qualifying assignments and judged by evaluators in the field. The agent-drafted notes keep the *ground* judgments with people: sizing up fuels and values at risk, adjusting tactics when observed behaviour departs from the forecast, declining to fell a tree beyond one's qualification, and stopping work when hazards change. Detection and indices arrive faster. Deciding what to do with them on the line does not move.

## Entry paths and formation

People learn this work through supervised seasons and task books signed off on qualifying assignments. The WP-07 provisions keep **10 hand calculations of fuel moisture and danger indices** in the entry sequence, checked against a reference calculation, even where stations supply the values. Sand-table and scenario simulation is used only for rare events such as entrapment or an escaped prescribed fire, "never in place of graduated exposure on assignments". The pressure on formation is institutional: qualification standards are being realigned and federal land agencies have lost staff. The USFS and the NPS each lost more than a quarter of their employees between September 2024 and January 2026 [V1-02; WF-02], and the fire-intelligence pathway notes that detail slots are scarce as a result.

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **In, corps member (R01) → this role (new hire):** safety culture first; fireline, mop-up and saw work evaluated on qualifying incidents; fire weather by hand; ignition under a firing boss; then briefings and directing a small crew. About 200 hours over 12 months plus one to two seasons [J]. The role profile also links entry from field resource technician (R02).
- **Out, upskill → fuels and prescribed fire specialist (R07):** six steps, including two fuels maps verified in the field and go/no-go decisions as a trainee under a qualified burn boss. About 240 hours over 24 months, because burn windows set the calendar [J].
- **Out, reskill → fire intelligence analyst (R08):** five hand-worked spread predictions checked against observed behaviour before modelled outputs are credited; seeded-error detection sets; field checks on three incidents or burns. About 300 hours over 18 months [J].

None of these has been tested against observed moves (ADR-0023 §5). COR-2 pathways that touch these roles sit in the forest module (`docs/build/WP-08/WORKLOG.md`).

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor.** Keep hand calculation of indices and hand-worked behaviour predictions in trainees' seasons. Require that any generated hazard briefing is checked against conditions on site before delivery. Use after-action reviews to record where modelled and observed behaviour differed.
- **Training officer.** Map your programme to the January 2027 USWFS–USFS standards when they apply, and keep EMST competencies as the description of the underlying work, not a replacement qualification. Plan for scarce detail slots by pairing duty-officer shadowing with simulation for rare events.
- **HR specialist.** Use the 0456 Federal Series Profile to draft duties, labelled subject to classification review. Do not present credential gates or sign-off authority from EMST as fact; check them against the current qualification standard. For state positions, crosswalk combined land-and-fire classes against the role's targets.

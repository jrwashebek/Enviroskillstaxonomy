## Who does this work

The role runs from crew member to crew supervisor. It covers constructing and holding fireline, firing and mop-up, saw
and hand-tool work, observing fire weather and behavior, crew safety and emergency response, and, at senior levels,
size-up, tactical direction and crew development. Position qualifications are credential gates, not EMST levels: EMST
links to the fire qualification systems and does not compete with them.

|---|---|
| **Employer types** | **Federal, state, local** (special districts): resource manager. **Private:** contract crews and engines dispatched under agreements (service provider). |
| **Typical titles** | Wildland firefighter; fire crew member; engine or hand crew supervisor |

## What stays with people, and why

This describes where the work sits today. Almost all of it happens on the fireline, and most of it is marked as
formation work. Fireline, saw work, firing, mop-up, hazard trees and crew safety are learned through graduated exposure
on qualifying assignments and judged by evaluators in the field. The agent-drafted notes keep the ground judgments with
people: sizing up fuels and values at risk, adjusting tactics when observed behavior departs from the forecast,
declining to fell a tree beyond one's qualification, and stopping work when hazards change. Detection and indices may
arrive faster; deciding what to do with them on the line does not move. The role draws mainly on field craft.

## Entry paths and formation

People learn this work through supervised seasons and task books signed off on qualifying assignments. The formation
provisions in EMST's draft pathways keep **10 hand calculations of fuel moisture and danger indices** in the entry
sequence, checked against a reference calculation, even where stations supply the values. Sand-table and scenario
simulation is used only for rare events such as entrapment or an escaped prescribed fire, "never in place of graduated
exposure on assignments". The pressure on formation is institutional: qualification standards are being aligned and
federal land agencies have lost staff. The Forest Service and the National Park Service each lost more than a quarter of
their employees between September 2024 and January 2026 [6], and the fire-intelligence pathway notes that detail slots
are scarce as a result.

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **In, corps member → this role (new hire):** safety culture first; fireline, mop-up and saw work evaluated on
  qualifying incidents; fire weather by hand; ignition under a firing boss; then briefings and directing a small crew.
  EMST's curriculum draft estimates about 200 hours over 12 months plus one to two seasons [J]. The role profile also
  links entry from field resource technician.
- **Out, upskill → fuels and prescribed fire specialist:** six steps, including two fuels maps verified in the field and
  go/no-go decisions as a trainee under a qualified burn boss. About 240 hours over 24 months, because burn windows set
  the calendar [J].
- **Out, reskill → fire intelligence analyst:** five hand-worked spread predictions checked against observed behavior
  before modeled outputs are credited; seeded-error detection sets; field checks on three incidents or burns. About 300
  hours over 18 months [J].

None of these has been tested against observed career moves.

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor.** Keep hand calculation of indices and hand-worked behavior predictions in trainees' seasons. Require
  that any generated hazard briefing is checked against conditions on site before delivery. Use after-action reviews to
  record where modeled and observed behavior differed.
- **Training officer.** Map your program to the January 2027 aligned federal standards when they apply, and keep EMST
  competencies as a description of the underlying work, not a replacement qualification. Plan for scarce detail slots by
  pairing duty-officer shadowing with simulation for rare events.
- **HR specialist.** Use the explorer's 0456 Federal Series Profile to draft duties, labeled subject to classification
  review. Do not present credential gates or sign-off authority from EMST as fact; check them against the current
  qualification standard. For state positions, compare combined land-and-fire classes with the draft targets.
- **Educator.** Keep hand calculations of fuel moisture and fire danger, checked against a reference calculation, in
  fire courses even where stations supply the values, and use simulation only for rare events.

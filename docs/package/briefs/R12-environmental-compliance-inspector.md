## Who does this work

Compliance inspectors plan and conduct inspections, collect evidence under custody, and write findings of fact. They select the response the enforcement policy sets, support enforcement cases and give compliance assistance. In the draft's summary, monitoring data and automated flags increasingly decide *where* to inspect, but on-site inspection and evidence stay human. Inspection is the state function most often found in state class specifications (SL).

## What stays human, and why

This describes where the work sits today. Inspection produces evidence for possible enforcement, so the draft keeps the on-site inspection, evidence collected under custody, findings of fact written as observations, the choice of enforcement response under policy, and, at senior level, testimony and defence of sampling procedures with the inspector. Analytics can rank facilities and flag anomalies. The draft treats each flag as something to confirm or dismiss against the underlying data before referral, and requires each targeting method to be evaluated after a cycle against what inspectors actually found. Hazard recognition, energized-site clearances and stop-work happen on site.

## Entry paths and formation

New inspectors learn by watching and then doing under observation. The WP-07 onboarding pathway (about 250 hours over 12 months alongside productive work) sets out the sequence. Trainees complete **five observed sampling events with zero custody defects** and **shadow ten inspections, then lead five under observation**, with reports reviewed by a senior inspector. They make **one manual audit of a facility's monitoring reports** before automated screening is credited, and work **a seeded-error AI compliance summary** in which every error that changes a compliance conclusion must be flagged. Evaluator time for observed inspections is the binding cost [J]. Mock inspections at a training facility can stand in for scarce live inspections in the shadowing stage only. Where federal capacity has fallen [V1-03], fewer senior inspectors are available to evaluate trainees.

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **In, new hire → independent inspector (onboarding):** the six-step sequence above, ending with an end-to-end inspection file at month 11 [J]. The role profile links entry from monitoring and data technician (R03).
- **Out, upskill → environmental permit writer (R11, state):** about 180 hours over 12 months, crediting a state inspector credential where one is held [J].
- **Out (role links, no pathway authored yet):** enforcement case manager (R23); facility environmental compliance specialist in the private sector (R37, mirror role). Moves to the private side carry frictions, including post-employment restrictions and pay direction, that the friction register records (ADR-0023 §4).
- **Out, upskill → resource programme manager (R13):** follows the specialist-to-manager pathway with recognition of prior learning [J].

None of these has been tested against observed moves (ADR-0023 §5).

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor.** Require that every automated flag is confirmed against the underlying data before referral, and that inspection reports separate the inspector's observations from conclusions, including when a note was machine-drafted. After each inspection cycle, compare predicted with found violations for any targeting method.
- **Training officer.** Use the onboarding sequence as a draft. Budget evaluator time first. Add a seeded-error compliance summary drill.
- **HR specialist.** Compare your inspector class specification with the R12 State Class View (Texas classes mapped). If you hire former federal staff, Pennsylvania's practice of treating federal experience as equivalent in minimum qualifications is one model [SL-031; SL-080]. Federal: use the 0028 Federal Series Profile, subject to classification review. EMST content is not a selection instrument [SEL-02].

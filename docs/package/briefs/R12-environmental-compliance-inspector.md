## Who does this work

Compliance inspectors plan and conduct inspections, collect evidence under custody, and write findings of fact. They
select the response the enforcement policy sets, support enforcement cases and give compliance assistance. The draft
expects monitoring data and automated flags to decide more often *where* to inspect, while on-site inspection and
evidence stay with people. Inspection is the state function found most often in EMST's sample of state class plans.

## What stays with people, and why

This describes where the work sits today. Inspection produces evidence for possible enforcement, so the draft keeps with
the inspector the on-site inspection, evidence collected under custody, findings of fact written as observations, the
choice of enforcement response under policy and, at senior level, testimony and defense of sampling procedures.
Analytics can rank facilities and flag anomalies. The draft treats each flag as something to confirm or dismiss against
the underlying data before referral, and requires each targeting method to be evaluated after a cycle against what
inspectors actually found. Hazard recognition, energized-site clearances and stop-work happen on site. The role joins
field craft and statecraft.

## Entry paths and formation

New inspectors learn by watching and then doing under observation. EMST's draft onboarding pathway (about 250 hours over
12 months alongside productive work) sets out the sequence. Trainees complete **five observed sampling events with zero
custody defects** and **shadow ten inspections, then lead five under observation**, with reports reviewed by a senior
inspector. They make **one manual audit of a facility's monitoring reports** before automated screening is credited,
and work **a seeded-error AI compliance summary** in which every error that changes a compliance conclusion must be
flagged. Evaluator time for observed inspections is the binding cost [J]. Mock inspections at a training facility can
stand in for scarce live inspections in the shadowing stage only. Where federal capacity has fallen, fewer senior federal
inspectors are available to evaluate trainees [1; J].

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **In, new hire → independent inspector (onboarding):** the six-step sequence above, ending with an end-to-end
  inspection file at month 11 [J]. The role profile also links entry from monitoring and data technician.
- **Out, upskill → environmental permit writer (state):** about 180 hours over 12 months, crediting a state inspector
  credential where one is held [J].
- **Out (role links, no pathway drafted yet):** enforcement case manager; facility environmental compliance specialist in
  the private sector (the mirror role). Moves to the private side carry frictions that EMST records in a friction
  register, including post-employment restrictions and pay: in BLS data for May 2025 the median environmental scientist
  earned $116,110 in the federal government, $80,500 in state government and $79,320 in consulting, a compositional
  comparison because federal staff are older and more senior on average [4].
- **Out, upskill → natural resource and environmental program manager:** follows the specialist-to-manager pathway with
  recognition of prior learning [J].

None of these has been tested against observed career moves.

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor.** Require that every automated flag is confirmed against the underlying data before referral, and that
  inspection reports separate the inspector's observations from conclusions, including when a note was machine-drafted.
  After each inspection cycle, compare predicted with found violations for any targeting method.
- **Training officer.** Use the onboarding sequence as a draft. Budget evaluator time first. Add a seeded-error
  compliance summary drill.
- **HR specialist.** The explorer has no State Class View for this role in this release: the Texas classes above are
  broader-class matches, which the class views leave out, so the class pages list the role only among unreviewed agent
  candidates (not an alignment). Compare your inspector class specification with the draft task list and targets
  instead. If you hire former federal staff, Pennsylvania's Office of Administration, for example, has identified federal
  job titles whose experience may count as equivalent for certain Commonwealth titles [5]. **Federal:** the explorer
  has no Federal Series Profile for this role either (its 0028 row is a broader-series match), so take duty language from
  the role's draft task list in the explorer, subject to classification review. EMST content is not a selection instrument;
  selection needs your own job analysis under 5 CFR 300.103 [6].
- **Educator.** Build observed practice into inspector courses: sampling events checked for custody defects, shadowing
  before leading, and a seeded-error AI compliance summary in which every conclusion-changing error must be found.

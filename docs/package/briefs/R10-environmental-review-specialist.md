## Who does this work

Environmental review specialists decide the level of review a proposed action needs, prepare or direct effects
analyses, coordinate cooperating agencies and consultations, build the administrative record and write decision
documents under the governing agency's procedures. The draft expects AI drafting to compress production, while
verification, record integrity and resilience in litigation grow.

## What is expected to change

**The procedural ground (checked facts, as of 23 October 2026).**

- CEQ's government-wide NEPA regulations were removed, and the removal was adopted as final effective 8 January 2026;
  each department now follows its own procedures. Interior kept a slim 43 CFR part 46 and moved most procedures into its
  NEPA Handbook; USDA consolidated its procedures in 7 CFR part 1b and rescinded the Forest Service's 36 CFR part 220 [1].
- In *Seven County Infrastructure Coalition v. Eagle County* (29 May 2025) the Supreme Court's judgment was 8–0, with a
  five-justice majority opinion holding that courts owe agencies substantial deference on the scope of NEPA review [2].
- The SPEED Act (H.R. 4776) passed the House 221–196 on 18 December 2025; it is pending in the Senate Environment and
  Public Works Committee and is not law [3]. S. 5653, a separate bipartisan Senate permitting bill, was unveiled on
  30 September 2026; no committee markup or floor vote had been reported, and counsel expected floor consideration in
  early November (to be re-checked after 15 November 2026) [4].
- The Forest Service's revised project-level objection rule (36 CFR part 218) was published as final on 13 October 2026
  and is scheduled to take effect on 12 November 2026 (to be re-checked after that date) [5].
- Under NEPA section 112, a project sponsor can pay a fee (125% of the anticipated cost, per counsel) for deadlines on
  environmental assessments and impact statements. No public count of projects using it was found, and it does not speed
  ESA or NHPA reviews [6].
- The Advisory Council on Historic Preservation's rewrite of its Section 106 regulations is under review at OIRA and had
  not been published as of 9 October 2026 (to be re-checked after the Council's 10 November 2026 meeting) [7].

## What stays with people, and why

This describes where the work sits today. Drafting may be compressible; accountability for the record is not. The draft
keeps with people the significance determination, the reasoned explanation linking evidence to each choice, the
independent evaluation of sponsor- or contractor-prepared documents, confirming that every substantive comment has a
reasoned response, and anticipating the claims a decision will face. Each is a judgment for which an official answers in
court, under a deference doctrine that rewards documented agency reasoning [2]. Building a defensible administrative
record now includes marking which content was produced with AI assistance. The role draws mainly on statecraft.

**Legal basis now varies by place.** Analyzing the cumulative burdens and distributional effects of decisions has a
different legal basis in federal and state venues. EO 12898 (Federal Actions to Address Environmental Justice in
Minority Populations and Low-Income Populations), EO 14096 and the relevant parts of EO 14008 were revoked in January
2025; the Congressional Research Service notes that revocation does not change EPA's statutory responsibilities [11].
New Jersey and New York require such analysis in permitting. The rules implementing New Jersey's Environmental Justice
Law remain in effect while an appeal is pending before the New Jersey Supreme Court, which granted certification in July
2026 [12]. New York's Environmental Justice Siting Law applies disproportionate-burden review to applicable permits
[13]. The explorer shows the computed applicability of each such item with a note and excludes nothing.

## Entry paths and formation

People learned review by drafting whole documents under a senior reviewer, and AI drafting threatens exactly that
apprenticeship. The formation provisions in EMST's draft pathways keep **two effects sections drafted unaided** from the
project record, accepted by a senior reviewer without substantive revision, before reviewing AI-drafted sections is
credited. They also keep **one comment set of at least 200 comments coded by hand**, with every substantive comment
recovered, before machine coding is credited. Shadow-drafting on live reviews and a moot defense before counsel stand in
for the formation once supplied by whole-document drafting [J].

## Pathways in and out (exploratory, not recommended)

- **In, restoration project lead → this role (reskill; the field-to-statecraft bridge):** EMST's curriculum draft
  estimates about 160 hours over 9 months plus one live review [J]. The role profile also links entry from compliance
  inspector.
- **Out, upskill → natural resource and environmental program manager:** public budgets, writing a formation plan for one
  entry role, knowledge continuity before departures, partner delivery, durable choices. About 120 hours over 18 months
  with a rotation or acting assignment [J].
- **Out (watch only):** an AI-assisted review lead role is shown only in the explorer's "What's coming" view. EMST's
  foresight analysis (a desk exercise by an AI agent) treats creating it as a contingent move, to wait until two or more
  appellate decisions uphold AI-assisted records and environmental-series headcount is stable for two quarters [J].

None of these has been tested against observed career moves.

## What you can do now

- **Supervisor.** Require that every AI-drafted section is checked against the project record and labeled in the
  record. Keep unaided drafting in each new reviewer's first assignments. Sample machine-grouped comments for unique
  points before responses are drafted.
- **Training officer.** Rebuild review training around your agency's current procedures, not the removed CEQ
  regulations [1]. Add moot defenses and record-gap reviews with counsel. Treat the SPEED Act and S. 5653 as separate
  pending bills, not law, until Congress acts [3, 4].
- **HR specialist.** Use the explorer's 0028 and 0401 Federal Series Profiles for duty language, labeled subject to
  classification review, and check with your labor-relations office before changing position descriptions on the basis
  of EMST drafts. OPM's 27 August 2026 memo treats AI drafting of position descriptions as generally not high-impact
  when the official independently reviews, revises and adopts the draft; adopting a draft without substantive,
  documented review is to be treated as high-impact [14]. Selection uses still need a job analysis under 5 CFR 300.103
  [15].
- **Educator.** Teach review with unaided drafting first: two effects sections from a project record, a comment set
  coded by hand, and a moot defense of a level-of-review decision, before students review AI-drafted text.

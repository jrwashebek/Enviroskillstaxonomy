# Evidence-Based Personas for EMST v2 (provisional)

**Role:** CM, simulated Workforce Voice · **Date:** 2026-10-23

**Read this first.** These personas are **composites built from published evidence**. They are not real people, and they
are not the voice of practitioners. An AI agent drafted them to give the real Workforce Voice panel something concrete to
correct, reject or replace (see `practitioner_panel_design.md` §7). Every situational detail is either cited to a source in
`sources.csv` or labelled [J]/[S]. Where the evidence is thin, the persona says so.

**No Tribal persona is drafted.** A persona for Tribal natural-resource staff would put words in the mouths of sovereign
nations' employees. Persona 10 is a placeholder that sets the requirement; its content must come through TN's protocol and
real consultation (CM-002 §3).

Each persona ends with **"Does this help me?" test questions**. These are the questions the persona would put to the tool,
and they become usability-session tasks (metric M-06/M-07). A question passes only if a real participant from that stratum
completes the task *and* says it helped.

## 1. Dana — federal field technician pushed out in the 2025 cuts

- **Situation.** Three seasons on a national forest trail and recreation crew, then a term appointment. Fired as a probationer in
  February 2025 (CM-035 records ≥3,400 USFS probationary firings, including trail crews and wilderness rangers) [E]. No non-fire
  seasonal hiring followed in 2025 (CM-039) [E]. Dana's forest is one of many that lost staff: White River NF went from 169 to
  111 year-round staff (CM-029) [E]. The county is taking over a popular site the forest can no longer run (CM-029) [E].
- **Crafts held.** Strong field craft (tool use, rock masonry, crew safety, reading terrain). Those are exactly the skills
  CM-040 lists as lost when trail miles maintained fell 22% [E]. Some data craft (GPS, field apps). Little formal statecraft,
  though a lot of public contact [J].
- **Barriers.** Their experience is written in agency jargon. Short federal service means no retirement value to carry [J]. They
  live in an expensive mountain town (CM-007, secondhand) [E-weak]. State and county jobs exist, but their class specs ask for
  different titles [J].
- **What would help.** Seeing which county, state-park or NGO roles want their field craft, in those employers' own words. Knowing
  which of their tasks count as experience under each employer's rules.
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "Show me three non-federal roles near me that use what I did on the trail crew, and say what each calls it."
  2. "Which of my tasks count as qualifying experience for a state park ranger or county open-space technician, and which don't?"
  3. "What is the smallest thing I could learn to qualify for the next step?"

## 2. Rafael — seasonal wildland firefighter deciding whether to stay

- **Situation.** A GS-0462/0456-track seasonal on a USFS engine. Fire staffing ran below target: 10,496 on board against an 11,300
  goal (CM-038) [E]. Interior has stood up the US Wildland Fire Service (CM-048) [E], with qualification standards aligned for
  January 2027 (CM-049) [E]. Collateral-duty red-card holders left in large numbers, ~1,400 to ~1,600 (CM-036, CM-039) [E].
- **Crafts held.** Deep, assessed field craft (NWCG Position Task Books are among the most observable qualification systems in the
  sector; CM-072) [J]. Growing data craft through fire behaviour tools and apps [J].
- **Barriers.** Seasonal appointment, housing, and a pay comparison with state and private fire employers [J; Grassroots Wildland
  Firefighters testimony (CM-060) could not be accessed, so this is not evidenced here]. In v1's tags, Fire Management's similarity to
  the statecraft families is below chance (z = −2.1 vs Environmental Policy; −1.7 vs Land Use Planning; report §3) [E]. A move into planning or policy has no visible bridge.
- **What would help.** Seeing how NWCG qualifications translate into land-management, planning (Community Wildfire Protection
  Plans) and emergency-management roles at state and county level. Knowing whether USFS↔USWFS moves keep their qualifications.
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "If I move from USFS to the new Interior fire service, which of my quals carry over in 2027?"
  2. "What would it take to move from engine captain to a county wildfire planner? Show me the gap."
  3. "Does any of this count toward a permanent job, or does it only describe my job?"

## 3. Priya — mid-career federal NEPA coordinator moving to a state agency

- **Situation.** Twelve years as an interdisciplinary-team leader and NEPA coordinator. Probationary firings and later
  reorganizations hit NEPA coordinators (CM-035) [E]. States courted displaced federal staff in 2025 (CM-044, CM-045) [E], but some
  states froze hiring that summer (CM-012) [E]. Agencies are now leaning on state and community partners for work (CM-034) [E].
- **Crafts held.** Statecraft heavy (federal environmental review, consultation, interagency coordination) plus solid data craft
  [J].
- **Barriers.** State environmental review statutes and procedures differ. Federal-specific statecraft does not translate
  automatically. This is SC's actor-specific-enactment question (CM-071) [J]. Federal-to-state pension portability is a likely
  barrier [J; not evidenced this session]. Applicant pools now hold many degree-holding former federal staff, which pushes degree
  filters back (CM-012) [E].
- **What would help.** Seeing which parts of federal review practice are portable as-is, which need re-learning under state law, and
  which state roles value interagency coordination.
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "Which of my competencies transfer to my state's environmental review office unchanged, and which need state-law re-learning?"
  2. "Show me state, local and consulting roles where federal consultation experience is a plus, and what they call it."
  3. "Can I use this to write a state application that a state HR analyst will actually score?"

## 4. Jordan — conservation corps alum whose term was cut

- **Situation.** One season on an AmeriCorps-funded conservation corps crew. Terms were cut in 2025 ($400M in AmeriCorps cuts;
  Pacific Crest Trail corps hours fell from 19,000 to ~4,000; CM-043) [E]. AmeriCorps' FY2026 status was being litigated into
  2027 (CM-046) [E]. The Corps Network reaches >22,000 young people a year (CM-031) [E]. Its new Service to Career framework
  emphasises pathway mapping, credentials and post-service placement (CM-032) [E]. **No published alumni-outcome data were found
  this session** (CM-031, CM-032), and that is itself a finding [E].
- **Crafts held.** Emerging field craft: chainsaw, restoration planting, invasive species work. Corps members are named
  representative roles in v1's Ecological Restoration family (CM-005) [E].
- **Barriers.** Whether noncompetitive hiring eligibility for corps service is still usable in 2026 is unverified (FC's FC-063 notes
  the same gap) [J]. No degree. Corps experience is hard to express in federal or state qualification terms [J].
- **What would help.** A pathway from corps tasks to entry roles in each actor, with the credential or assessment that gets them
  over the gate.
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "I did a season of restoration crew work. Which entry jobs (federal, state, county, NGO, contractor) does that qualify me for right now?"
  2. "Which certificate or badge would count most, and where do I get it cheaply?"
  3. "Does my corps term give me any hiring advantage anymore?"

## 5. Mei — new wildlife biology graduate with no federal on-ramp

- **Situation.** BS in wildlife biology, 2026. The federal under-30 share is 7.9% and student-trainee occupations shrank (CM-028;
  trainee detail as recorded by FH) [E]. Seasonal technician jobs, the classic first rung, were frozen in 2025 and only partly
  returned in 2026 (CM-039, CM-041) [E]. Unpaid internships are a known barrier in adjacent fields (CM-007, secondhand) [E-weak].
  Early-career workers in AI-exposed occupations are losing ground (CM-069, secondhand; LM's lane) [E-weak].
- **Crafts held.** Field craft from coursework and one field camp. Data craft (R, GIS) [J].
- **Barriers.** Positive-education rules help Mei (a degree meets the 0486 requirement) but cannot create vacancies (CM-007) [J].
  The consulting and NGO alternatives use different titles [J].
- **What would help.** Seeing every sector actor's entry door for the same work (consulting biological technician, state wildlife
  technician, NGO monitoring crew, Tribal program; the last only as Tribes choose to list it). Knowing which first jobs keep the
  formation tasks that build judgement.
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "Where else besides federal agencies can I do wildlife survey work as a first job, and what does each call it?"
  2. "Which of these first jobs will still teach me field judgement, and which are mostly data entry that AI is taking over?"
  3. "What should I learn this year to be hireable in two of these sectors at once?"

## 6. Luis — state water-quality specialist absorbing work the federal government dropped

- **Situation.** A state agency scientist. Federal capacity has fallen (USFS lost ~a quarter of non-fire staff by May 2025 and
  sought help from state and community partners; CM-034) [E]. Federal sites and functions are being handed to counties and state
  agencies (CM-029) [E]. Luis's unit is being asked to take on agreements and co-delivered work [J].
- **Crafts held.** Field and data craft in monitoring. Statecraft in state permitting. Little experience negotiating
  intergovernmental agreements [J].
- **Barriers.** A state hiring freeze (CM-012) [E] means upskilling, not hiring. Training budgets are thin [J].
- **What would help.** A reskilling map from his current role to a "co-delivery coordinator" role, plus the knowledge-continuity
  practices needed to absorb federal work without losing what the departing federal staff knew.
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "My unit is taking over work a federal office did. What competencies did that federal role need that my team lacks?"
  2. "Show me a short training path for intergovernmental agreements that my supervisor will approve."
  3. "How do I capture what the federal staff knew before they're gone?"

## 7. Aisha — corporate sustainability analyst who wants public-sector climate work

- **Situation.** Four years in ESG reporting at a utility. v1 flags the entry task in this field (collecting ESG/GHG data) as
  High-severity CLD (CM-008) [J; single-analyst]. ESG and finance-adjacent roles pay on private-sector norms (CM-007,
  secondhand) [E-weak].
- **Crafts held.** Data and statecraft (disclosure frameworks, stakeholder engagement). Almost no field craft. v1 tags give
  Corporate Sustainability a 4% field-craft share (report §3) [E].
- **Barriers.** Taking a pay cut [J]. State classification may not recognise corporate disclosure work as "environmental program"
  experience [J]. v1 tags show Corporate Sustainability → Climate Science covers only ~18% of the destination's core (report §3)
  [E].
- **What would help.** Seeing which public climate roles value disclosure and data work, and what the 7 missing climate-science
  competencies would take to acquire.
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "Which state or city climate roles would count my ESG reporting experience, and at what level?"
  2. "What exactly am I missing for a climate adaptation planner job, and how long would it take to close?"
  3. "Show me honest pay ranges so I can decide."

## 8. Teresa — state HR classification analyst implementing skills-based hiring

- **Situation.** Works in a state central HR office under a skills-based hiring executive order. Colorado-type programmes rewrote
  ~80% of classifications, but hires without degrees stayed flat at 25% (CM-012) [E]. Maryland and Pennsylvania track applicants,
  not hires (CM-012) [E]. Practitioners say managers still prefer degrees and job descriptions must be rewritten by hand (CM-012) [E].
- **Role for EMST.** Intermediary user. If Teresa can't use it, workers never feel it (CM-014: embedding in hiring operations is
  what moves hiring) [E].
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "Give me competency-based minimum qualifications for an environmental specialist class that I can defend, with assessable indicators."
  2. "Map our class specs to federal series so I can credit federal experience for displaced applicants."
  3. "Tell me which requirements in this class spec are degree proxies I could replace with an assessment."

## 9. Sam — employee-organization representative

- **Situation.** Steward for a federal land-management local. Unions are litigating hiring changes (AFGE v. Kupor stayed one
  Merit Hiring Plan essay question; CM-051) [E]. RIF regulations were finalised in September 2026 and Schedule Policy/Career moved
  ~8,000 senior positions (CM-052; FH records) [E].
- **Concern.** Will skills data be used to rank people for RIF or to justify cutting "redundant" roles? (A11, logic model) [S]
- **Does this help me?**
  1. "Can management use this tool to decide who gets cut? Show me the written rule that says no."
  2. "Does it help my members prove their skills for reassignment or placement elsewhere?"
  3. "Who sees an individual's data, and can they opt out?"

## Cross-persona pattern (for the panel to test)

[J] Seven of the nine drafted personas ask some version of: **"Show me what my work is called in the other sector, and whether it
counts."** That is a *translation-and-crediting* need. It points to role-profile variants and crosswalks to gate instruments, not to
more competency detail. Two personas (Teresa, Sam) are intermediaries who decide whether the tool reaches workers at all. If these
patterns survive contact with real practitioners, they should drive the first three views (CP's question; report §7).

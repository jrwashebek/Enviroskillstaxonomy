## 1. The theory of change as written, and as it needs to be tested

**Charter (CM-001):** "Environmental outcomes depend on professionals who can move between these sector actors. They need a
blend of three crafts… If those blends are visible and portable, mobility follows."

That sentence bundles four causal claims, and each needs testing separately:

1. **Visibility → recognition.** Making craft blends visible changes how workers describe themselves and how employers read them.
2. **Recognition → movement.** Employers who can read the blends hire across sector-actor lines.
3. **Portability.** The rules let the move happen (qualifications, pay, retirement, appointment type, licensure, clearance, geography).
4. **Mobility → environmental outcomes.** Movement improves the work.

The evidence (report §2) supports a narrower, conditional version. I propose this as the **testable theory of change**
[J, built on E in §3 below]:

> **If** craft blends are made visible *inside the instruments that gate movement* (qualification standards, class specifications,
> position descriptions, postings, assessments, credentials and learning records),
> **and if** the main structural frictions on specific, demand-backed pathways are reduced or made navigable,
> **then** cross-actor movement will increase on those pathways, and transferable knowledge will be retained when work changes hands
> between actors;
> **and** environmental outcomes will be protected where work is co-delivered or devolved across actors.

The changes from the charter version are deliberate:
- **"Visible" moves from the taxonomy to the gate.** Publishing a framework rarely changes hiring. Embedding it in hiring operations sometimes does (A3, A3′).
- **"Portable" is reframed as a property of institutions on a pathway**, not of skills in general (A5).
- **A demand condition is added.** In 2025–26 vacancies and co-delivery arrangements, not visibility, are the binding constraint (A6).
- **Knowledge continuity is added as an outcome.** This is the most evidenced mobility need in 2025–26: work is moving between actors faster than people are (A8).

## 3. Assumptions, each with label and the evidence that would confirm or refute it

| ID | Assumption | Label | What the evidence says now | Would confirm | Would refute |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Crafts can be distinguished and rated reliably at task level, so blends are measurable | [S] | v1 craft is a single-analyst classification, collinear with domain (CA audit, CM-071) | IO pilot: inter-rater agreement on craft weights at or above IO's threshold (e.g. Krippendorff α ≥ 0.67 for research use) | α below threshold, or ratings that track domain rather than task content |
| A2 | Lack of a shared language for transferable skills is a binding constraint for the people the tool serves | [J] | Job seekers "must learn to articulate their skills in terms employers understand" (CM-012). Digital badges are "met with confusion" by employers (CM-019) | Panel career-history interviews show moves that failed because skills went unrecognised | Moves fail mainly on rules, pay or vacancies even when skills were recognised |
| A3 | Making skills visible (publishing a framework, removing degree screens) on its own changes hiring | [E] **refuted as stated** | BGI/HBS: 45% of firms that announced skills-based hiring were "in name only"; net effect <1 in 700 hires (CM-010, CM-011). Removing degree requirements alone added ~2 pp non-degree hires (CM-014). Colorado: share of hires without degrees flat at 25% in 2023–24 (CM-012) | — | — |
| A3′ | Embedding skills in hiring operations (mapped credentials, applicant tracking, trained managers, assessed qualifications) increases hiring of non-traditional and cross-sector candidates | [E] partial | Top-decile "credential fluency" firms were 11 pp more likely to hire credentialed workers (CM-014). In Pennsylvania ~60% of new hires lack a degree after 92% of positions were de-degreed (CM-013). Federal hiring is moving to technical assessments: USA Hire 138 → 412 series; 34.3% of delegated-examining announcements used technical assessments in FY26 Q2 (CM-050) | Pilot partners' cross-actor hires rise after EMST-based PD/posting/assessment rewrites, relative to comparison units | No change in applicant or hire mix after embedding |
| A4 | Taxonomy similarity predicts feasible transitions | [S] | No environment-specific test exists. Two national similarity systems were deployed **without validation against observed moves** (CM-022, CM-023). The methods literature validates against flows (CM-057, CM-058; not re-read this session) | Predicted pathways rank observed moves well above chance (e.g. AUC ≥ 0.70) on held-out career histories | Similarity is uncorrelated with observed moves, or is beaten by simple baselines (same agency, same location) |
| A5 | Structural frictions on target pathways can be reduced or navigated | [E] mixed | Positive-education requirements hard-code degrees in 0460, 0482, 0486, 1315, 1340 and 1360 (CM-007, secondhand). Merit Hiring Plan essays are in litigation (CM-051). Schedule Policy/Career changes tenure for ~8,000 senior staff (CM-052). Qualification alignment between DOI and USFS fire is due January 2027 (CM-049). | A6 | Destination actors have demand (vacancies, co-delivery work) | [E] **against at federal level now**; mixed elsewhere | Federal: 386,826 separations vs 122,598 accessions to January 2026; USFS and NPS each lost >25% (CM-028). States: hiring freezes in Colorado and Maryland in summer 2025 (CM-012), yet states actively recruit displaced federal staff (CM-044, CM-045). Work is devolving to counties and state parks (CM-029) and to state/community partners (CM-034) | Pathway-level vacancy and co-delivery data show demand where EMST is deployed | Pilot pathways have no openings, so any mobility gain is unmeasurable |
| A7 | People who move perform well and stay | [E] weak | Skills-hired workers reportedly promoted at near-parity and stay longer (BCG via CM-012; CM-066 not read) | Pilot movers' 12-month retention and supervisor ratings at parity with internal hires | Movers churn faster, or need longer onboarding with no support |
| A8 | Cross-actor mobility protects environmental outcomes | [S] | The *need* is evidenced: Maroon Bells management moves to Pitkin County; White River NF went from 169 to 111 year-round staff (CM-029); trail maintenance fell 22% and specific skills were lost (CM-040). Nothing yet links mobility of people to outcome quality | Case comparisons of devolved/co-delivered work with and without staff who moved with it | No outcome difference, or outcomes driven solely by funding |
| A9 | Entry pathways keep producing people with field craft | [E] **against now** | No non-fire seasonal hiring in 2025 (CM-039). AmeriCorps cuts of $400M; corps hours on the Pacific Crest Trail fell from 19,000 to ~4,000 (CM-043). Under-30 share of federal staff is 7.9% (CM-028). v1 flags CLD on 28% of competencies (CM-003, computed) | Seasonal/corps/apprentice cohorts restored and their formation tasks kept | Continued collapse. The theory of change then fails at its source, because field craft is the hardest blend to enter (report §3.3) |
| A10 | The NGO pilot can tell us whether federal/state rollout will work | [J] against, unless designed for it | NGO HR systems, hiring rules and data differ from federal/state ones | Pilot includes federal/state "shadow users" and HR artifacts (G1-g) | Pilot success relies on NGO-specific flexibility |
| A11 | The tool will not be used against the people it serves | [S] risk | Final RIF regulations (2026-09) and new accountability regimes (CM-052) create incentives to use skills data to rank or remove staff [J] | Misuse policy adopted; no adverse-action use documented | Documented use in RIF or adverse action |
| A12 | Workers trust the tool enough to put their own career data in it | [J] | No evidence. Fear of surveillance is plausible amid RIFs [S] | Opt-in rates and panel trust ratings | Low opt-in; panel withdrawal |

## 5. Contribution-analysis plan

I propose contribution analysis (Mayne's six steps; CM-067, method reference not re-read this session) over an RCT. The
intervention is a shared infrastructure adopted unevenly, under heavy external shocks (X1–X8), so randomisation at
organisation level is impractical. A credible "contribution story" tested against rival explanations is achievable.
Experimental or quasi-experimental components are nested where possible. [J]

| Step | What we do | When | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Set the cause–effect question | "Did EMST contribute to more and better cross-actor moves on targeted pathways, and to knowledge retention where work moved between actors?" Pathways are pre-registered (e.g. federal field technician → state/county field role; field biologist → state permitting; corps alum → federal/state technician; NGO analyst → state climate office) | Before pilot | Pre-registration memo |
| 2. Develop the theory of change and its risks | This document; assumptions A1–A12; rival explanations X1–X8 | Now | logic_model.md (v0.1) |
| 3. Gather existing evidence | Baseline on each pathway: applications, hires, time-to-hire, retention, demographic mix (from partner HR, OPM FWD CM-054, state HR, LEHD J2J CM-068); baseline PD/posting language; panel career histories | Pilot month 0–2 | Baseline dataset (see metrics M-B*) |
| 4. Assemble the contribution story; find weak links | Process tracing at each pilot org: was EMST used, by whom, in which artifact, with what result? Link-by-link ratings (strong/weak/absent) for A2, A3′, A5, A6 | Pilot month 6 | Contribution story v1; weak-link list |
| 5. Seek additional evidence | (a) **Predicted-vs-observed validation** of the pathway graph on held-out career histories (A4). (b) **Comparison units**: matched non-pilot orgs or units, difference-in-differences on applicant and hire mix (A3′). (c) **Dose-response**: depth of embedding (view-only → PD rewrite → assessment) vs outcome change. (d) **Interrupted time series** on posting language at partners. (e) Panel diary studies of real job searches (A2, A12). (f) Rival-explanation checks: freeze-lifting dates, pay changes, state displaced-fed programmes (CM-044) | Pilot month 6–12 | Evidence pack per link |
| 6. Revise and strengthen | External reviewer and panel rate the story (credible / partly / not). The rating feeds expansion gates G1/G2 | Month 12, then annually | Gate decision record |

**Rival-explanation discipline.** A gain on a pathway is attributed to EMST only if (i) the artifact chain is documented,
(ii) the gain exceeds comparison units, (iii) it does not coincide only with X2 or X4 events, and (iv) movers report EMST use
in diaries or interviews. Otherwise it is logged as "co-occurring, not attributable". [J]

**Pre-registered failure condition.** If after 12 months no targeted pathway shows a documented artifact chain *and* the
panel's "does this help me?" pass rate is below threshold, the pathway/mobility features stop at the pilot. EMST continues
only as a role-change and training resource. This keeps the team honest about outcomes. [J]

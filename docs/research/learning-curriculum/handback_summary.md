**What learners and instructors need.** They need one unit EMST doesn't yet publish: one competency at one level, written as 2–5 observable outcomes with conditions and criteria. SAF already accredits at this grain. A pathway is the gap to a target *role*, not a family: a forester lacks 23 of the data-science family's core competencies, but a remote-sensing role needs about 8. v1 is weak for this:
- 63% of its knowledge statements are unobservable ("Understands", "Shapes").
- Only 5.8% of indicators state a standard.
- 82% of competencies with parsed expert indicators include "Publishes…".
- Domain 5's proficiency text is misparsed.

Domain 7.2 (validating AI outputs) is the writing model to copy.

**Template in brief.** Fields cover entry and target profile, the gap, outcomes traced to indicator IDs, modes, assessment, sequence, recognition, credential, and duration and cost. The key rules:
- Teach only the gap.
- Assess in the right context (field, desk, stakeholder, incident).
- "No pixel without plot": AI-heavy pathways keep field ground truth.
- Protect formation: supervised repetitions, and set amounts of unaided work on entry tasks AI is compressing.
- Issue credentials as bundles of 3–8 competency-levels, not single badges.

**Top proposals:**
- P-LC-01: publish level outcomes in the standard.
- P-LC-02: define levels by responsibility, not years.
- P-LC-05: formation rules.
- P-LC-06/07: bundle credentials plus a register that maps existing credentials for prior-learning credit.
- P-LC-08/09: accreditation and program–occupation crosswalks, fixing v1's occupation-code errors.
- P-LC-10/11: fill gaps accreditors require (tree identification, water-treatment operations, environmental-health operations) and add mentor and evaluator competencies.
- P-LC-12: build the AI and data-practice baseline (LC-PW-06) first.

**Biggest trade-off.** AI shortens pathways. The formation rules lengthen them and use mentor time, which is scarce given federal staffing cuts.

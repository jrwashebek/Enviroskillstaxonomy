### 3.2 An operating model for "bringing it all together"

The project lead's ambition is that EMST becomes the asset that connects the many indices, studies, groups and funds shedding light on AI and work in the environmental domain. A list does not do that. A **join key** does. [J]

- **Stable EMST task and competency IDs, crosswalked to O*NET task IDs and DWAs.** Any index anchored to O*NET then becomes joinable to environmental roles: the AEI's task usage, O*NET's own forthcoming AI-impact index [E: AS-005], and Lightcast and BGI skills. LM and IA build this. CA's §7 already anticipates that "if external measures are anchored to O*NET tasks, the task layer must be crosswalk-first." My adoption evidence supports that.
- **Four connection modes**, used consistently in `how_emst_connects`:
  1. *ingest/crosswalk/benchmark:* data comes in and maps.
  2. *partner/validator:* people review and supply evidence.
  3. *adopter:* EMST goes into the adopter's artifacts.
  4. *funder/distribution:* money and reach.

  Every entity has at least one mode and a named integration.
- **An annual "State of Environmental Work" release** with each MINOR version (charter Art. 6.6). It synthesizes the year's signals across the atlas. This is the convening product that gives indices, societies and funders a reason to keep feeding EMST. [J]
- **The Ecosystem Atlas itself** should be a repo dataset with owners and review dates, maintained by the steward's partnerships manager and refreshed each MINOR. Otherwise it decays like any directory. [J]

## 6. Governance (summary of `governance_charter_draft.md`)

**Precedents.** NICE, ESCO, O*NET, Credential Engine, SFIA, ECO Canada, schema.org/W3C/IETF, TDWG, ASC and OpenLMP are compared, yielding five lessons. The draft charter's 11 articles turn them into rules.

**Bodies:**
- a Steward;
- an Editorial Board of 7–9, with no more than one member per organization;
- a Technical Steering Committee;
- an Advisory Council of 17–21 with sector-actor seats and funders as non-voting observers;
- a **Tribal Council with consent authority over Tribal-related content, pending TN review and real consultation**;
- Module Stewards;
- Validation Panels;
- a Sentinel Network.

**Change process:**
- a NICE-style path: proposal → triage (≤30 days) → candidate and comment (30 or 60 days) → adjudication → decision → release;
- change types E, C, S, D, X and T;
- for substantive changes, two independent sources including a demand signal;
- use-tier labels (exploratory, training-grade, selection-grade) set by IO.

**Cadence:**
- PATCH as needed, with crosswalks at least quarterly;
- MINOR every year in a fixed month (April proposed);
- MAJOR no more than every 3 years with 12 months' notice;
- 24-month support windows.

**Other provisions:**
- **Conflicts of interest:** disclosures, recusal, no funder votes, vendor seat caps, and a fee-for-service firewall.
- **Licensing:** as in §0.
- **Marks** are held in trust.
- **Anti-ASC succession clause:** a Host Agreement, a successor list, mirrors and a maintenance floor.

## 10. Answers to CA architecture questions (§6, AS)

### AS-1. Is federated core-plus-modules governance credible? Who are the candidate stewards and the long-term host? (DP-9)

**Credible in stages, not as v2.0's starting state.** [J]

**Supporting evidence:**
- NICE handed its removed work roles to DoD's DCWF, a working core/extension federation in which the extension's steward has its own mandate and staff. [E: AS-027; CA-033]
- ESCO formalizes distributed legitimacy through seats for member-state experts. [E: AS-028]
- SFIA 10 workshop attendees asked for "stable core plus plug-ins," though SFIA has not adopted this. [E: CA-034]

**Against, or missing:**
- No subsector body has agreed to, or is resourced for, stewarding an EMST module. Most candidates could not be verified this session.
- Societies have thin staff and compete for the same work. [E: AS-056]
- Societies are managing their own pressures, e.g., SAF renaming programs to protect accreditation. [E: AS-042] They will not take on unfunded authoring.

**Recommendation** (charter Part B):
- **v2.0–v2.1 (2027–28):** modules are *editorial sections*. They are namespaced and on the shared schema, **authored centrally and paid for by the core**, with a named *module advisory partner* reviewing.
- **v2.2+:** a section becomes a *stewarded module* only after its partner signs a Module Stewardship Agreement and completes two review cycles on time.
- **Orphan clause:** a module returns to the core if its steward misses two cycles.

So CA can design the architecture for Option C (namespaced IDs and module boundaries) while staffing for central authoring through 2028. That costs about one more editorial FTE if federation never matures (§7 of CA's hypotheses).

### AS-2. What change process and release cadence will adopters commit to?

There is **no direct evidence** of what environmental-sector adopters will commit to. I found no studies, and no adopter has yet been asked. This is a gap, and Stage 1b should test it. [J]

**What the evidence does show:**
- **Federal HR artifacts last years.** PDs, classification standards and validated assessments are expensive to change, and changing them can trigger collective bargaining. [E: AS-007]
- **Agencies plan on annual and quarterly cycles.** Staffing plans are annual with quarterly updates. [E: AS-009]
- **Upstream data moves quarterly.** O*NET publishes several releases a year. [E: AS-004]
- **Long-lived precedents limit their own churn.** NICE caps majors at once every three years with a year's notice, and minors at once a year. [E: AS-026]

**Proposal** (charter Art. 6):
- **Stable IDs forever**, with tombstones for deprecated IDs.
- **PATCH** as needed, with crosswalks re-pinned at least quarterly (CM M-30).
- **MINOR** once a year in a fixed month: April, after the sentinel cycle and before federal budget formulation.
- **MAJOR** no more than every 3 years, with ≥12 months' notice and a migration guide.
- **24-month support** for the prior MAJOR. Assessment users may pin a version until their next job-analysis refresh.
- **Use-tier labels** (exploratory, training-grade, selection-grade), so adopters know what each item can be used for.

**Change process.** NICE-style stages with published service levels, the adjudication categories NICE uses [E: AS-025], an evidence standard for substantive changes (two independent sources, including a demand signal), and Tribal-related changes routed to the Tribal Council first.

**Hypothesis to test in the design-partner track [S]:**
- Federal and state adopters will accept an *annual* content cadence only if each release ships a "does this affect me?" change notice and keeps the prior version supported.
- They will reject anything faster than annual for content used in PDs or assessments.
- Confirmed if design partners' artifacts survive a MINOR release without rework; refuted if they demand semiannual content updates or refuse to pin versions.

### AS-3. How do we avoid the Australian Skills Classification outcome?

CA's reading of the ASC 3.0 Release Report says it is no longer actively maintained, with data to Dec 2023. [E: AS-060 via CA-036] I could not establish *why* it stopped.

The failure modes below are general to single-owner public classifications. They are my [J]/[S] diagnosis, not ASC-specific evidence:
- a single owner whose priorities change;
- users with no governance stake and no budget line for upkeep;
- upkeep that depends on a continuous data and ML pipeline;
- no maintenance floor, so the classification decays silently rather than shrinking on purpose.

**Countermeasures built into the AS deliverables:**
1. **No single owner** (charter Art. 2.6). A host operates the program, an independent Consortium governs it, and no organization holds a voting majority.
2. **Adopters become stakeholders.** The commitment register and rung-5 co-governance give adopters seats. Public agreements and service fees mean users fund upkeep instead of only consuming it.
3. **Diversified funding with guardrails.** No funder above 40%, federal share at most about 20%, and reserves.
4. **A published maintenance floor** (3 FTE, ~$0.62M) and a **degradation ladder** with 6 months' notice. Below the floor, succession is triggered, not silent decline (charter Art. 11.4).
5. **Succession by design.** A five-year Host Agreement with 12 months' notice, a pre-qualified successor list including a fiscal sponsor, marks held in trust, mirrors and DOIs, a reproducible build, and credentials held at two organizations.
6. **A pipeline that can run cheaply.** Static delivery (CP Tier 0/1), open demand signals preferred over licensed data, and an annual sentinel cycle that runs through partner societies.
7. **A five-year public review** of host, funding and governance.

## 12. Disagreements and trade-offs

1. **Strict sequence vs. parallel track (with the charter's literal rollout order; aligned with CM A10).**
   - Strict sequence: cleaner learning and less reputational risk with federal partners.
   - Parallel track: catches the 2027 windows and gets validation data from federal incumbents.
   - I recommend parallel, framed as co-producing artifacts rather than deploying a tool. CA decides.
2. **CC BY 4.0 vs. CC0 everywhere (with OpenLMP's CC0 choice).**
   - CC0: fastest embedding into federal standards and vendor systems.
   - CC BY: preserves version-cited provenance (legal defensibility) and visibility (fundability).
   - Splitting by layer gets most of both.
3. **University host vs. society consortium host (with v1's implicit SAF-centered path).**
   - Society host: credential leverage.
   - University host: neutrality across professions that compete for the same work, plus CESU funding.
   - Societies keep strong governance seats either way.
4. **Recruiting Delphi panels through adopters (likely tension with IO).**
   - For: recruiting through adopters builds commitment.
   - Against: it risks bias and loss of independence.
   - Mitigation: IO sets panel composition rules and COI disclosures, and independent panelists make up at least half of each panel. IO should challenge this.
5. **Kits before interactive views (mild tension with CP).**
   - CP already ships PD and IDP exports in the 1.0 MVP, so the gap is small.
   - My point: no Stage 1–2 adopter needs the interactive tool before committing, so kits win when the two compete for the same effort.
6. **Breadth vs. a federal-first first job (tension with DB and the charter's breadth mandate).**
   - The federal windows pull depth toward the 0400 series, 0028 and 0819.
   - I support CA's core-plus-modules design so breadth grows through modules without delaying the federal kits.
7. **Fee-for-service vs. neutrality.**
   - Services fund the core, but paid clients could seek influence.
   - The firewall (charter Art. 7.5) and public fee schedules manage this. A purely philanthropic model avoids the conflict but repeats the ASC risk.
8. **Function-based renaming of DEI/EJ-labeled content.**
   - Some will read renaming as capitulation.
   - Leaving the labels risks federal rejection of whole kits.
   - I recommend keeping the content and naming it by the work performed. This is CA's call, with SC and TN.

**Role:** AS (Pilot Adoption, Coalition & Policy Lead + Stewardship & Sustainability Strategist)
**Date:** 2026-10-23 · **Status:** Phase 2 draft for the Chief Taxonomy Architect (CA)
**Evidence labels:** [E: source ID] evidence I read · [J] expert judgment · [S] speculation. Source IDs resolve to `sources.csv` in this folder.

## 0. The argument in one page

1. **Federal and state adoption will not happen through one big "adopt the EMST" decision.** It happens through small artifacts that agencies are already required to produce: position descriptions, competency models, technical assessments, staffing plans, state class specifications, training plans, grant curricula. EMST wins by being the fastest way to produce those artifacts well. [J]
2. **Three federal windows are open now and close on fixed dates.**
   - **OPM classification.** OPM's series modernization is publishing new classification and qualification standards, with competency models and PD templates, from April 2026 to September 2027. It is realigning several natural-resource series into 0401 and 0404. [E: AS-007]
   - **OPM assessments.** OPM's Chance to Compete plan names Natural Resources Management as a Tier 2 group for technical assessments, with full implementation by FY2028. [E: AS-006]
   - **Wildland fire qualifications.** Aligned USWFS/USFS standards take effect in January 2027. [E: AS-011]
3. **A strictly sequential rollout would miss those windows.** If the NGO pilot runs first and federal work starts in 2028, the windows will already have closed. I therefore propose a **federal and state design-partner track that runs in parallel with the NGO pilot** during 2027. The pilot stays first for deploying the tool. The design-partner track co-produces artifacts. This departs from the literal sequence, and I name the trade-off in §6. [J]
4. **The capacity shock is the adoption opening.**
   - The federal civilian workforce fell 12% between September 2024 and January 2026. [E: AS-039]
   - The Forest Service lost about 16% of its staff, is closing all nine regional offices and is shifting work to states. [E: AS-020, AS-021]
   - Agencies must now file annual staffing plans aimed at critical skills gaps. [E: AS-009]
   - Knowledge retention, reskilling and skills-gap evidence are the needs EMST can meet immediately. [J]
5. **Every commitment is logged with a year-five owner.** See the commitment register, P-AS-15 in `report.md`. A commitment that nobody will maintain is a liability, not an asset. [J]

## 2. Adoption routes, one at a time

For each route: the **mechanism**, the **gatekeeper**, **what EMST must hand over**, **lead time**, the **realistic commitment** to seek, **risks**, and **owner** (role code).

### R1. O*NET: emerging tasks, occupation data and the AI-impact method

- **Mechanism.** New tasks enter O*NET as write-in statements from job incumbents and occupational experts. Reviewers now use NLP-assisted triage. Once a task is developed, it goes into future data collections, where incumbents rate its relevance, importance and frequency. It is then published as an "Emerging Task." [E: AS-003] O*NET has also tested LLM-assisted identification of emerging tasks, for example drone-specific tasks in May 2025. [E: AS-005] There is no documented public channel for associations to propose tasks. The 2018 report on professional-association membership falls outside the reviewed period. [E: AS-005]
- **Gatekeeper.** The National Center for O*NET Development, under DOL ETA.
- **What EMST hands over.**
  - A crosswalk from EMST tasks to O*NET task IDs and DWAs, built on the RDF release. [E: AS-004]
  - A short list of high-confidence new task statements written in O*NET style (e.g., validating AI-generated environmental analyses, from v1 7.2.1) with evidence.
  - Contacts for occupational experts willing to respond to O*NET data collection.
  - An offer to test O*NET's AI-impact recommendations (Jun 2026 report) on environmental tasks. [E: AS-005]
- **Lead time.** 18–36 months from submission to published data. O*NET collects data on a rolling cycle. [J]
- **Commitment to seek.** A technical meeting with the O*NET Center on (a) crosswalk method and (b) whether EMST-sourced task candidates can enter its review. Do not ask O*NET to "adopt EMST"; its mandate is cross-economy. [J]
- **Risk.** DOL budget pressure. [E: AS-016] O*NET's own AI-impact index may diverge from EMST's instrument. LM and IA must align fields to the O*NET task ID so EMST can join either index. [J]
- **Owner.** IA (crosswalk), LM (AI-impact alignment), AS (relationship).

### R2. 2028 SOC

- **Mechanism.** Comments go through Federal Register notices. The SOCPC judges proposals against its classification principles, and comment volume does not count. [E: AS-002] The first notice closed in August 2024. The second, with initial recommendations, is TBD. The final structure is due in 2027. [E: AS-001]
- **What EMST hands over.** A dossier in the SOCPC's requested format: title, nature of work, distinguishing attributes, job titles, employment size and growth ("helpful in evaluating collectability"), employers, education, licensing, tools, associations. [E: AS-002] Candidate detailed occupations to test: environmental data and geospatial analysts in natural resources, and wildland fire intelligence analysts. FH and LM must judge whether any of these meets the collectability bar. [S]
- **Commitment to seek.** Co-signatures from two or three professional societies and at least one employer group on any comment. Society co-signature costs them little and shows the work is distinct. [J]
- **Risk.** Low odds of a new detailed occupation [J]. The cheaper win is better title-to-SOC coding guidance.
- **Owner.** LM and FH (evidence), AS (coalition sign-on).

### R3. OPM classification and qualification standards; agency PDs

- **Mechanism.** OPM develops standards (draft standards are circulated through the CHCO Council site) and agencies classify positions against them. Phase One consolidation ran with agency feedback due May 11, 2026. OPM is now publishing updated standards, guidance, competency models and PD templates through September 2027, and later phases will redesign structure. [E: AS-007] Precedent shows new series arrive when a mandate meets a clear definition of the work (0456, 1560). [E: AS-008]
- **Gatekeepers.** OPM Workforce Policy and Innovation (fedclass@opm.gov) and agency CHCOs and classifiers.
- **What EMST hands over: the "0400 Kit."**
  1. Competency models for destination series 0401 and 0404 (and 0460, 0408, 0486 as a second wave). Each model has EMST competencies at four levels with observable indicators.
  2. PD template language for duties absorbed from retired series (e.g., former 0480 Fish and Wildlife Administration work now in 0401).
  3. A crosswalk from retired to destination series at the task level, so agencies can show duties were preserved.
  4. A note on what changed between versions (an OPM-ready change log).
  FH owns classification correctness. IO owns observability. CP owns accessible formats. [J]
- **Commitment to seek.** One agency classification office (USFS or a DOI bureau) agrees to draft three to five PDs with the kit and send them through normal classification review. OPM Workforce Policy and Innovation agrees to review the kit as an input to its competency models. [J]
- **Lead time.** Must reach OPM by **Q2 2027** to influence standards published by September 2027. [J, from AS-007 dates]
- **Risks.**
  - Collective-bargaining notice for PD changes. [E: AS-007]
  - Language constraints (see §1).
  - A competency model built from v1 content that is still unvalidated. Mitigation: label the kit "provisional; job-analysis validation in progress," which honestly describes where IO's work stands. [J]
- **Owner.** FH (content), AS (sponsor and relationship).

### R4. OPM technical assessments (Chance to Compete)

- **Mechanism.** Agencies must use job-analysis-based technical assessments. USA Hire covers 412 GS series with general batteries on a fee-for-service basis. Agencies can build their own mission-specific assessments or have OPM HR Solutions build them. [E: AS-006]
- **What EMST hands over: a "Job-Analysis Starter Pack"** for one Tier 2 series.
  - Task inventory with criticality and frequency questions.
  - Competency-to-task linkage.
  - Proficiency anchors.
  - An SME survey instrument.
  - Guidance on documenting validity under UGESP (IO leads).
- **Commitment to seek.** One agency assessment team runs the SME survey with its own incumbents and shares de-identified aggregate ratings back to EMST under a data-use agreement. This is also EMST's first defensible validation data. [J]
- **Risk.** Using EMST in **selection** needs a much higher validity bar than using it in training. IO must set the minimum evidence for each use; CA question to IO. [J]
- **Owner.** IO, FH, AS.

### R5. Agency workforce planning, reorganization and fire

- **USFS and DOI staffing plans (EO 14356).** Offer a one-page skills-gap template (series × competency × proficiency gap × single point of failure). It plugs into required quarterly updates. [E: AS-009] This is the lowest-friction federal commitment. [J]
- **USFS knowledge retention.**
  - Map departing staff's critical tasks to EMST competencies before relocations finish in summer 2027. [E: AS-020]
  - Give the 15 state directors and state partners a shared vocabulary for Good Neighbor work. [E: AS-021]
- **USWFS and NWCG.**
  - Crosswalk EMST fire competencies to NWCG positions and the aligned qualifications. [E: AS-011]
  - Offer function-based fire-intelligence competencies (v1 WFIC/F16 work) as input to USWFS workforce design. [E: AS-049]
  - EMST must **link to NWCG**, not compete with it. NWCG's task-book system is a stronger performance standard than most licensure. [E: AS-056]
- **Owner.** FH, FC, AS; TN for any Tribal fire program content.

### R8. Professional societies: credentialing and accreditation

- **What societies control.**
  - Program accreditation: SAF's four standards [E: AS-041]; SRM and ABET [J].
  - Professional certification: SAF Certified Forester [E: AS-040]; TWS CWB, AFS, ESA, GISCI, ASFPM CFM, IHMM CHMM, AICP [E: AS-057 lists; status J].
  - Continuing education: SAF ForestEd. [E: AS-040]
  - State credentialing positions: SAF's six elements. [E: AS-043]
- **Mechanism.** Accreditation standards change on each body's own review cycle. Certifications change through job-task analyses that the credentialing bodies run themselves, often under ISO/IEC 17024 practice. [J]
- **Realistic commitments.**
  - (a) Editorial and advisory seats.
  - (b) Recruiting Delphi panels and sentinel nodes from members.
  - (c) An "alignment review": the society compares its accreditation criteria or exam blueprint with EMST and publishes a crosswalk.
  - (d) Eventually, citing EMST as a reference in its next job-task analysis.
  - Do **not** ask societies to replace their standards. Their legitimacy is the asset. [J]
- **Risk.** Professions compete for the same work: forestry, wildlife, data science and planning claims overlap. [E: AS-056] One society as host or lead would tilt framing (see `sustainability_model.md`).

### R10. Private sector and HR technology (brief; PS and IA lanes)

Vendors (Lightcast, HRIS and talent platforms) adopt when a standard is (a) openly licensed, (b) machine-readable with stable IDs and (c) asked for by their public-sector customers. The adoption ask is a **conformance listing**: vendors map their taxonomy to EMST IDs and are listed as "EMST-mapped." This becomes a value-added service line (see `sustainability_model.md`). [J]

## 4. Change management

**The unit of adoption is one decision, not the whole tool.** Adopters move up a five-step ladder. Each rung is a commitment the register can track. [J]

| Rung | Behavior | Example | What EMST must provide |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Aware | Has seen it | Briefing, demo | Two-page brief per audience |
| 2 Try | Uses it once, informally | Drafts a PD paragraph | Copy-ready language; no login needed |
| 3 Use in one decision | An official artifact cites EMST | A PD, class spec, training plan or grant curriculum | Templates plus version-pinned IDs, so the citation stays stable |
| 4 Embed in a system | Built into a recurring process | Staffing-plan template; assessment; ETPL review | Data files and API; change notices; support |
| 5 Co-govern | Takes a governance role | Advisory seat; module steward; sentinel node | Charter seats; contribution process |

**Change-management practices for public-sector adopters** [J, informed by how the 2025–26 reforms are reaching agencies, E: AS-006, AS-007, AS-009]:

- **Attach to mandates already in force.** These are Chance to Compete, the Merit Hiring Plan, EO 14356 staffing plans, OPM consolidation, Workforce Pell and Good Neighbor Authority. A new mandate from EMST is never the pitch.
- **Make the first use reversible and cheap.**
  - Downloadable files, so no system needs an authority to operate.
  - Section 508-conformant documents (CP).
  - No personal data collected.
- **Find a champion at the right level:** a classifier or training officer who has to deliver the artifact, sponsored by a deputy CHCO or state HR director.
- **Account for labor relations.** PD and assessment changes can trigger bargaining obligations. [E: AS-007] Brief union partners early. Federal and state employee organizations sit on the Workforce Voice panel (CM).
- **Train the trainers.** Each design partner gets a two-hour session and a one-page "how to cite EMST in a PD" guide. Track rung movement quarterly.
- **Reassure on stability.** Adopters fear building on something that changes. Semantic versioning, at least 24 months of support for each major version, and pinned IDs (governance charter Art. 6) are adoption features, not just engineering choices. [J]

**Framing guidance** [J]:
- Describe **work and capability** in the vocabulary of each adopter's current mandate: merit, skills-based hiring, mission readiness, knowledge retention, efficiency, public safety (fire).
- Do not drop substantive competencies to fit a political moment. Instead, ask CA to decide function-based names for content currently labeled DEI or EJ (v1 5.3.4, SA 6.7) so the work stays visible. Example: "engaging overburdened communities in permitting decisions" names a task agencies still perform.
- The career-ladder-destruction (CLD) finding is EMST's most policy-relevant message, per v1's planning [E: AS-052]. It should be released with PPS or NAPA as a co-publisher so it lands as nonpartisan analysis. [J]

**Trade-off I am asking CA to accept.** The charter's rollout runs NGO pilot, then federal and state. I propose running federal and state **design partners** in parallel during the pilot year.
- **Gain:** EMST catches the OPM and fire windows, and the first validation data comes from federal incumbents.
- **Cost:** federal partners see a less mature tool. The design-partner framing ("co-produce artifacts, not deploy a tool") limits reputational risk, and the pilot still decides whether the *tool* expands.
- **Alternative:** a strict sequence, accepting that the September 2027 OPM window is missed and EMST waits for OPM's later redesign phases (dates unknown). [J]

## 7. What adopters will commit to, and what that means for the build

This is the "keep the build tied to commitments" list for CA, CP, FH, IO and SL. Items are ordered by how soon an adopter will use them. [J]

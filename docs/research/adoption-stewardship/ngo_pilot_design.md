## 1. Purpose and the questions the pilot must answer

The pilot is not a demo. It is the first test of the theory of change under real conditions. **If** NGO staff can describe roles, gaps and development in EMST terms, **and** those descriptions end up in real artifacts that point people toward state and federal roles, **then** the tool is ready for federal and state adoption. [J; CM logic model A2, A3′]

It must answer five questions:

1. **Value.** Does the tool help NGO program and training leads (CP persona U1) do something they needed to do? CM measures this with M-06, M-07 and M-28.
2. **Embedding.** Do EMST IDs get into real artifacts within six months, such as postings, IDPs, training plans and grant reports? CM measures this with M-08.
3. **Portability.** Do corridors that start in NGO roles end in recognizable state and federal role variants? This is tested with state and federal "shadow users" (CM A10; M-01 and M-10).
4. **Coverage.** Which roles and competencies does the cohort ask for that EMST lacks? These requests feed CA's backlog (CP "request a role" path).
5. **Adoption economics.** What support does an organization need to keep using EMST after the stipend ends? This feeds `sustainability_model.md`.

Why NGOs first:
- They can act without a federal compliance review. [J; CP strategy §3]
- They sit at the entry point of many environmental careers.
- Corps placement routes, such as the USFS Resource Assistants Program run through The Corps Network, already connect them to federal work. [E: AS-036]

CM warns that NGO success may not predict federal and state success unless the pilot is designed for it (CM A10). The design answers this in two ways:
- Every corridor ends in a state or federal role variant.
- State and federal shadow users from the design-partner track (see `adoption_roadmap.md` §3) review the outputs.

## 2. Corridors

These three corridors are proposed for CP and CA to confirm. They follow v1's pilot framing of permitting capacity, wildfire workforce pipelines, conservation corps to careers, and two or three western states [E: CP-009 per CP], and they match where state design partners are most likely. [J]

| Corridor | NGO entry roles | State / federal destination variants | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Corps to field technician | Corps crew member, crew lead, individual placement | State parks or forestry technician; federal technician (0404 after the 0455 realignment); Resource Assistant | Largest entry pathway; AmeriCorps fragility makes portable competency records urgent [E: AS-018, AS-007, AS-036] |
| C2 Fuels and prescribed fire | NGO burn crew, TREX participant, fire-adapted-community coordinator | State fire or forestry fuels staff; USWFS or USFS fuels positions under the 2027 aligned qualifications | USWFS created; qualification alignment Jan 2027 [E: AS-010, AS-011] |
| C3 Watershed and land stewardship to permitting and monitoring | Land trust stewardship or monitoring staff; watershed council project manager; community air or water monitoring lead | State water-quality or permitting staff; federal 0401 / 0028 | Permitting capacity and devolution to states [E: AS-021]; links to OpenLMP plan monitoring requirements [E: AS-059] |

## 3. Cohort composition

**Size:** 12 organizations, in a range of 10–14. This is enough for strata to show up in CM's "every stratum" tests while staying supportable by one coordinator. [J]

**Geography:** concentrate in two or three states where state design partners are recruited (likely western states with rising Good Neighbor Authority workload [E: AS-021]), plus two national or multi-state organizations. Corridors then end in real state classes that SL can crosswalk. [J]

| Slot | Organization type | Count | Example recruitment channel | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Conservation corps (one state-run, one nonprofit, one multi-program individual-placement corps) | 3 | The Corps Network; Washington Conservation Corps; Conservation Legacy [E: AS-036, AS-019] | Anchor partner: The Corps Network (with JFF's Service to Career Pathways framework if JFF joins) [E: AS-036] |
| B | Land trusts (one accredited mid-size, one small or rural) | 2 | Land Trust Alliance [J] | Easement monitoring, stewardship and transactions roles |
| C | Watershed council or river organization | 2 | River Network; state watershed council networks [J] | Restoration project management, monitoring, grant reporting |
| D | Fire NGO (prescribed fire training or fire-adapted communities) | 1 | TREX / Fire Learning Network partners [J] | Spans NGO, state, federal and Tribal fire practice |
| E | Environmental justice or community environmental health organization | 1 | Regional EJ networks [J] | Community monitoring, permitting participation; tests D5/D6 and Statecraft from the community side |
| F | Tribal-serving NGO | 0–1 | **Only by invitation through TN's protocol** | Not a Tribal government. Participation is never a quota (CM M-25). No Tribal data or knowledge is collected unless the Tribal Council (or TN's interim mechanism) authorizes it. |
| G | Statewide association (e.g., a state association of conservation districts or land trust coalition) | 1 | NACD or state coalitions [J] | Multiplier: tests whether an intermediary can carry EMST to its members |
| H | Data-heavy conservation science or restoration NGO | 1 | WILDLABS, ESIP communities [J] | Tests the Data craft and human-AI practice layers |

Shadow users (not cohort members) give the NGO pilot its state and federal validity. They are recruited through the design-partner track: two or three state HR or training staff and one or two federal classification or training staff. They review NGO-produced artifacts and corridor endpoints. CM M-10 targets at least two federal and three state agencies with signed agreements. [J]

## 4. Selection criteria

Each criterion is scored 1–5 by a three-person panel: AS, CP, and one Workforce Voice panel member. Scores are weighted as shown. [J]

| # | Criterion | Weight | Evidence asked for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real artifact in the next six months**: a hire, reorganization, season plan, grant report or training redesign where EMST could be used | 25% | A named artifact and date |
| 2 | **Corridor fit**: the organization's roles sit on C1, C2 or C3 and it places people toward state or federal roles | 20% | Placement or partnership examples |
| 3 | **Named lead with time**: about 0.1 FTE for 10 months (program or training lead, persona U1) | 15% | Letter from the executive director |
| 4 | **Willingness to share artifacts** under the data agreement (§7) and to take part in research sessions | 15% | Signed intent |
| 5 | **Diversity contribution**: size, rurality, type, state; small organizations favored | 15% | Cohort-level balancing |
| 6 | **Partnership reach**: an intermediary or network role that can spread use | 10% | Membership or network |

**Exclusions:**
- Organizations that sell competing workforce products. This is a conflict of interest.
- Any organization that would need to share personnel records with identifiable information to take part.
- Tribal governments. They are not NGOs, and their participation is governed by TN's protocol, not this pilot.

## 5. What pilots do with the tool: use cases

Each organization picks **at least two** use cases. One must produce an artifact (UC2, UC3 or UC4).

| ID | Use case | Steps (user) | Output | Product dependency (CP) | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UC1 | **"How is this role changing?"** | Pick one or two roles; review task-level change and exposure with uncertainty shown; decide what to train, keep or redesign | Short role-change note | View (a), role change | M-06, M-07 |
| UC2 | **Rewrite a posting or job description with EMST IDs** | Draft from the PD/JD skeleton export; human adopts or edits; publish | Live posting or JD citing EMST IDs and release | 1.0 PD skeleton export | **M-08** |
| UC3 | **Corps member or seasonal transition portfolio** | Member and supervisor map season experience to EMST competencies and levels; gap view against a state or federal destination | Portfolio or IDP the member owns; optional Open Badges | Gap view; IDP export; badges (LC) | M-08, M-09 |
| UC4 | **Season training plan or curriculum** | Map the existing training plan to EMST; find gaps in the corridor's destination competencies | Revised training plan | Kit or data files (works before MVP) | M-08 |
| UC5 | **Grant proposal or funder report** | Describe workforce outcomes in EMST terms (competencies gained, pathways) | Proposal or report section | Data files | Adoption economics (§1, Q5) |
| UC6 | **AI-readiness review of team tasks** | Review which tasks AI may augment or compress; plan human-AI practice training (v1 SA 7.2) | Team note | Role-change view; exposure layer (LM) | M-06 |

**Fallback if the product slips.** UC2, UC4 and UC5 run on data files and kits alone. The pilot can start on time even if the 1.0 MVP lands late. [J]

## 7. Data agreements

There is one pilot agreement per organization (MOU plus data-use terms). The principles come first. They draw on CARE for any Tribal-related matter [E: AS-046] and on CP's privacy positions. [J]

1. **Minimum necessary.** The default exchange is organization-level artifacts (postings, JDs, training plans) with names removed. **No person-level data by default.**
2. **Person-level data only by opt-in.** For example, UC3 portfolios used for research. This requires:
   - informed consent;
   - ethics review by the host institution's IRB or an independent IRB;
   - data held by the person or the organization, with EMST receiving only de-identified aggregates.
3. **Ownership stays with the organization** (and the person for portfolios). EMST receives a non-exclusive license to use de-identified artifacts for validation and to publish aggregate findings and short quotes after the organization reviews them.
4. **No use for ranking or removal.** EMST outputs may not be used to rank, select or remove staff without a written use policy and worker notice. Any such use is a misuse incident that pauses expansion (CM M-11).
5. **Tribal data and knowledge excluded** unless the Tribal Council (or TN's interim mechanism) authorizes it, with Local Contexts labels where it directs.
6. **Right to withdraw** at any time. Withdrawn artifacts are deleted from working sets within 30 days. Aggregates already published remain.
7. **Retention.** Working artifacts are kept for 3 years, then deleted unless re-consented.
8. **Security.** Storage follows CP's controls, with no third-party AI processing of shared artifacts unless the organization opts in.
9. **Federal and state shadow users.** Feedback collection follows Paperwork Reduction Act-aware design (CP CR-12). Agency artifacts are covered by the design-partner DUA, not the NGO MOU.

**DUA template elements:** parties; purpose; data categories (Schedule A); permitted uses; prohibited uses; de-identification standard; consent forms; security; breach notice (72 hours); publication review (10 business days); withdrawal; retention; Tribal carve-out; governing law; contacts.

## 9. Success criteria and expansion gates

**Gate G1 (pilot → federal and state adoption)** uses CM's thresholds as written (`metrics_framework.csv`). I do not restate them differently, to avoid two versions:
- M-01 role coverage: ≥90% of requested roles.
- M-05 accessibility and privacy clearance: pass.
- M-06 "Does this help me?": ≥70% overall and ≥60% in every stratum.
- M-07 core-task completion and usability: ≥80% completion, SUS ≥68.
- M-08 real-artifact use: ≥50% of organizations, ≥30% repeat use.
- M-10 partner commitment: ≥2 federal and ≥3 state agencies with signed agreements.
- M-11 misuse incidents: zero.
- M-28 panel ratification in every stratum.

**AS adds four adoption-and-sustainability conditions to G1** (proposed; for CM to adopt or reject) [J]:

| ID | Condition | Threshold | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| AS-G1a | Cohort retention | ≥10 of 12 organizations complete | Tests whether the support model is enough |
| AS-G1b | Willingness to continue | ≥4 organizations commit to continued use in 2028 with a reduced or no stipend | Revealed value, not stated value |
| AS-G1c | Governance uptake | ≥2 pilot organizations take a sentinel-network or advisory role | Seeds co-governance (adoption ladder rung 5) |
| AS-G1d | Funding horizon | Stage 2 funding secured or committed for ≥24 months | Feeds CM M-29 (G2 requires ≥5 years) |

**Stop or redesign triggers:**
- Any misuse incident (M-11).
- M-06 fails in any stratum after a redesign round.
- Fewer than three organizations produce an artifact by month 6 (M-08 trajectory).
- The anchor partner withdraws.

A stop means redesign, not abandonment. The data and kits remain useful to federal and state design partners. [J]

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| AmeriCorps funding disruption at corps sites (stay ends Feb 1, 2027) [E: AS-018] | Medium | Recruit one state-run corps; UC3 portfolios are member-owned and survive program loss |
| Product slips | Medium | UC2/UC4/UC5 run on kits and data files; shift research rounds |
| NGO needs pull the product away from federal and state needs (CP risk) | Medium | Corridors end in state and federal variants; shadow-user reviews; FH/SL screen review |
| Small organizations lack capacity | High | Tiered stipends; peer pairs; office hours; a light minimum (two use cases) |
| Sensitive data shared by mistake (e.g., site locations) [E: TN v1_assessment, NHPA §304 note] | Low–Medium | Artifact intake checklist; Tribal carve-out; no third-party AI processing by default |
| Pilot read as endorsement of a product rather than a test | Medium | Pre-registered evaluation (CM); publish negative findings |
| Political or framing risk in federal shadow reviews (EJ/DEI-labeled content) | Medium | CA decision on function-based naming before shadow reviews ([E: AS-008] banner) |

# EMST Tribal Engagement Protocol (DRAFT 0.1)

> **AI-drafted. Requires review and revision by Tribal representatives before use.**
>
> An AI agent playing the Tribal Nations Workforce & Sovereignty Advisor (TN) wrote this draft from published sources. **No Tribal nation, inter-Tribal organization or Tribal professional has reviewed it.** It does not speak for any Tribe. Nothing in it authorizes outreach. It becomes usable only after (1) the project lead adopts it as a working draft, (2) Tribal reviewers chosen through the channels in §4 revise it, and (3) the Tribal Advisory Council it describes adopts its own version. Tribal reviewers may discard any part of it.

## 1. What this protocol is for

The Environmental Management Skills Taxonomy (EMST) describes environmental work across federal, state, local, Tribal, NGO, private and academic employers. This protocol sets out how the project engages Tribal nations and Tribal professionals **as partners and sovereign governments, not as stakeholders or data sources**. It covers four kinds of activity:

| Activity | Example | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| A. Content **about working with** Tribal nations | Competencies on government-to-government consultation, treaty rights and Indigenous data governance, written for non-Tribal professionals (candidates TN-C01 to TN-C12; v1 6.5.1 and 7.1.1; the L2 ethical lens) | Tribal Advisory Council (§5), which has binding authority over inclusion and wording |
| B. **Tribal-employer role variants** | How a Tribal environmental director's or Tribal forester's role differs in authority and accountability from a federal counterpart (TN-C13) | Opt-in only. Validation by Tribal professionals, then Tribal Advisory Council approval |
| C. **Tribally stewarded content** | A module that Tribes or an inter-Tribal body choose to author, for example competencies they define for their own workforce | The Tribal steward. The project hosts or links only on the steward's terms |
| D. **Research involving Tribal people** | Delphi panels, surveys, interviews or workshops that include Tribal employees or citizens | Each Tribe's research review process (§10) and the participant |

**Things this protocol is not:**

- **It is not government-to-government consultation.** EMST is not a federal agency and holds no trust responsibility. The project must never call its outreach "consultation." It must never imply that participating in EMST satisfies any agency's consultation duty under EO 13175, NHPA Section 106 or agency policy. [J; consultation duties described in TN-014, TN-016, TN-017]
- **It is not a way to acquire Indigenous knowledge.** The project does not collect Indigenous knowledge (see `indigenous_data_governance_policy.md` §4). Engagement is about governing EMST content and design. It is not about extracting knowledge.

## 2. Principles

1. **Sovereignty first.** Tribal nations are governments and employers. Where a Tribe has its own law, policy or protocol on research, data or knowledge, that law governs the Tribe's participation. Where this protocol and a Tribe's requirements differ, the Tribe's requirements prevail for that Tribe. [J; Tribes already set their own on-reservation qualification standards via compacts, TN-013]
2. **Partners, not stakeholders.** Tribal reviewers hold decision authority over Tribal-related content (§5). The project does not merely "seek their input."
3. **Nothing about Tribes without Tribes.** The project does not publish content that characterizes Tribal nations, Tribal programs or Indigenous knowledge without approval under this protocol. Until approval exists, the default is **exclusion**.
4. **Free, prior and informed consent, specific to purpose.** Consent is sought before any contribution. It covers a stated purpose and can be withdrawn (§7, §9). DOI policy applies the same standard to the federal receipt of Indigenous knowledge (301 DM 7 requires FPIC before receiving IK). The project should do no less. [E: TN-018]
5. **Opt-in, and silence is not consent.** A Tribe that does not respond has declined. Non-participation is never interpreted, reported or used against anyone. (§8)
6. **Reciprocity.** Participation must bring the Tribes something they want (§11). Participation is compensated (§6). (Collective benefit, CARE. [J; TN-060 not re-read this session])
7. **Tribal time, not project time.** Release schedules adjust to Tribal governance cycles, not the reverse (§10).
8. **One voice is not a nation's voice.** An individual Tribal professional speaks for themself. A Tribe's position comes only from the Tribe's government or its designee. The project records which is which.
9. **AI does not stand in for Tribes.** AI agents on this project do not contact Tribes. They do not produce material presented as a Tribal perspective. Everything they draft is labeled as AI-drafted (§12).

## 3. Who is a Tribal partner here (draft definitions for reviewers to correct)

- **Federally recognized Tribes (574)** [E: TN-028] are sovereign governments with a government-to-government relationship with the United States.
- **Inter-Tribal organizations and consortia** are bodies that Tribes form and govern: for example the Intertribal Timber Council, NATHPO, NAFWS, inter-Tribal fisheries commissions, NTAA, ITEP (university-hosted, Tribally oriented), RTOCs and NCAI. They speak for themselves and, where their governance provides for it, for their members. They do not speak for any individual Tribe unless that Tribe says so. [E for ITC/NATHPO: TN-004, TN-036; J for others]
- **Alaska Native Tribes, Tribal consortia and ANCSA corporations.** ANCSA corporations are a distinct legal category with separate DOI consultation policy (512 DM 6/7). [E: TN-016] They need their own track and reviewers.
- **Native Hawaiian organizations** have a distinct legal status and are not federally recognized Tribes. They need a separate track. [J]
- **State-recognized Tribes** are outside the federal government-to-government relationship, but they may run environmental programs and hold knowledge rights. Reviewers should decide how they participate. [J]
- **Tribal professionals as individuals:** Tribal employees, Tribal citizens working for any employer, and TCU faculty and students.

## 4. Outreach: appropriate channels

**Do:**
- Approach **Tribal governments** through a letter to the Tribal leader (chair, president, governor) that states who the project is, what it is asking, what it offers and what it will never do. Copy the Tribe's natural resources or environmental director only if the Tribe's own published contact guidance says so. [J]
- Approach **inter-Tribal organizations** first. Ask each one how it wants to be engaged, and whether and how it is willing to help reach its members. Accept "no." [J]
- Ask the first inter-Tribal reviewers to help design the Tribal Advisory Council's selection process (§5) **before** any wider outreach.
- Attend Tribal or inter-Tribal conferences **only at the host's invitation** and on the host's terms.
- Put a budget for compensation and travel in place **before** first contact (§6).

**Do not:**
- Scrape staff directories or job boards to build contact lists.
- Cold-email individual Tribal employees about their employer's programs.
- Treat a federal liaison or a non-Tribal intermediary as a substitute for the Tribe.
- Present an AI-drafted document to Tribes without the AI-drafted label.
- Use a Tribe's name, seal or logo, or list it as a "partner," without written permission.

## 5. Tribal Advisory Council (TAC)

**Composition (draft).** Seven to eleven members. They are nominated by Tribal governments and inter-Tribal organizations, not selected by the project. The project proposes the following and reviewers decide:
- regional spread, including Alaska;
- at least one Tribal environmental program professional;
- at least one Tribal forestry, fire or fisheries professional;
- at least one person with Indigenous data governance expertise;
- at least one Tribal college or university representative.

A separate Native Hawaiian participation track. Members choose their own chair.

**Decision authority: binding, not advisory.** Nothing in the following areas is published or released without a TAC decision:

1. inclusion, exclusion and wording of any Tribal-related content (category A in §1), including the L2 ethical lens definition and every L2 tag;
2. any Tribal-employer role variant (category B);
3. the Indigenous data governance policy and any change to it;
4. any use of Tribal workforce data, including Tribal employer job postings and change signals that touch Tribal programs;
5. the release gate: the TAC signs off on the Tribal-related content of each release.

**The TAC's no is final.** For Tribal-related content, the Chief Taxonomy Architect's general authority to resolve disputes does not override the TAC. If the TAC and the project disagree, the content is withheld. **This needs an explicit amendment to the team charter** (flagged to CA and the project lead). [J]

**Advisory role.** On everything else (architecture, product, adoption), the TAC advises and the project responds in writing.

**Working method.**
- The TAC sets its own quorum and decision rule. The draft suggestion is consensus-seeking with recorded minority views.
- The TAC approves its own meeting records.
- Deliberations are confidential unless the TAC decides otherwise.
- The project provides staff support but no voting member.

**Terms and conflicts.**
- Terms are two years, staggered.
- Members disclose conflicts.
- A member may speak in a personal capacity and the record says so.

## 6. Compensation

- **Pay for expertise.** TAC members and Tribal reviewers receive honoraria at professional consulting rates for preparation and meeting time, plus travel costs. Unpaid review is not an acceptable default. [J]
- **Pay the right party.** Where a Tribal employee takes part as part of their job, ask the Tribe whether payment should go to the Tribal government (through an agreement) rather than to the individual. Follow the Tribe's answer.
- **No strings.** Payment never depends on agreeing with the project, and it does not transfer ownership of anything shared. DOI's IK policy states the same rule: compensation does not transfer ownership of Indigenous knowledge. [E: TN-018]
- **Budget before outreach.** If AS cannot secure a budget line, outreach does not begin. [J]

## 7. Consent

- **Written and specific.** Consent covers the purpose, what will be published, where and in what form, the license, attribution, retention, whether AI tools may process the material (default: no), and how to withdraw.
- **From the right authority.**
  - A Tribal position needs the Tribal government's or its designee's consent.
  - An individual's professional views need the individual's consent. Also ask whether their employer requires authorization.
  - An inter-Tribal organization consents through its own process.
- **Renewed at major changes.** If the project's purpose, license or host changes (for example, transfer to a federal agency, which could bring records-disclosure law into play), consent is sought again. [J; FOIA limits on confidentiality noted in TN-018]
- **Recorded.** A consent record ID is attached to every contribution (see the policy, §7).

## 8. Opt-in participation

- No Tribe, Tribal program or Tribal employer appears by name in EMST content, datasets, dashboards or communications unless it has opted in.
- The project does not claim or imply that "Tribes were engaged," "Tribal input was incorporated" or that content is "Tribally reviewed" beyond what the TAC authorizes to be said.
- Tribes that decline are not listed as having been asked.

## 9. Right of refusal and withdrawal

- **Refusal at any stage.** A Tribe, organization or individual can decline to take part, decline a specific use, or decline publication. No reason is needed.
- **Withdrawal at any time.**
  1. The withdrawn content is removed from working branches and from the next release.
  2. It is marked "withdrawn at the contributor's request" in the changelog, without reproducing it.
  3. Known downstream users are notified.
- **Be honest about open releases.** Copies of an openly licensed release cannot be recalled. For that reason Tribal-contributed content does **not** go into an openly licensed release unless the contributor understands this and chooses it (see the policy, §9 and §12).
- **TAC withdrawal.** The TAC can withdraw approval of Tribal-related content. The content is then removed at the next release, or sooner if the TAC asks.

## 10. Timelines that respect Tribal governance

- **Council cycles.** Decisions by Tribal governments may need council action or a resolution. Councils meet on their own schedules.
  - Draft minimum windows: 90–120 days for any request that needs a Tribal government decision, and 30–45 days for staff- or professional-level review.
  - Extensions are granted on request, without justification.
- **Seasons.** Fire season, fishing and harvest seasons, ceremonial periods and fiscal-year deadlines (federal grant workplans are due early in the calendar year [E: TN-003]) are periods to avoid.
  - Each Tribe or reviewer names its own periods.
  - The project does not assume them.
- **No project deadline justifies a shorter window.** If Tribal review is not complete, Tribal-related content stays out of the release and the rest of the release proceeds.
- **Rapid policy change is not a reason to rush Tribes.** In 2025–26, consultation-related policy moved quickly (EO 14112 rescinded; OMB M-25-36; USWFS; Section 106 rulemaking). [E: TN-026, TN-014, TN-007, TN-041] Tribes criticized short-notice, webinar-style "consultation" on the fire reorganization. [E: TN-009] The project should not repeat that pattern.

## 11. Reciprocity: what Tribes should get

The TAC and Tribal partners decide what would be useful. Candidates to offer:

- training-ready competencies for **non-Tribal** staff on consultation, treaty rights, agreements and data governance (TN-C01 to TN-C12). Tribes in 2025 asked for federal staff with real authority and better practice. [E: TN-009]
- workforce analyses that Tribes control. For example, aggregate pathway and pay comparisons for Tribal natural resource roles, released only as the Tribes direct. IFMAT IV documents pay and staffing gaps. [E: TN-004]
- curriculum materials co-developed with Tribal colleges, led by the TCUs. [J]
- free access to the tool, and the option to host Tribal-specific views under Tribal control.
- evidence for Tribal advocacy, where Tribes want it: for example, documentation of reimbursement delays as a workforce barrier. [E: TN-009]

## 12. Rules for project team members, including AI agents

1. AI agents do not contact Tribes, Tribal organizations or Tribal professionals.
2. AI agents do not produce content presented as a Tribal, Indigenous or community perspective. AI-drafted text shown to Tribal reviewers carries the AI-drafted label.
3. No material provided by a Tribe or Tribal professional enters an AI system unless the provider has explicitly allowed it. This includes the project's own LLM-based NLP pipeline, any third-party AI tool, and use as prompts, fine-tuning data or evaluation sets. [J; consistent with TN-017, TN-018]
4. Team members do not summarize or paraphrase Indigenous knowledge in project documents, even when it is publicly available elsewhere.
5. Team members disclose any personal or institutional relationship with a Tribe they engage.

## 13. Records and accountability

- **Engagement log.** It records who was approached, through which channel, when, what was asked, the answer, the consent record and any compensation. The Tribe can see its own entries and correct them.
- **Annual report.** The project reports to the TAC each year on Tribal-related content, engagement, withdrawals and incidents.
- **Complaints.** Complaints go to the TAC chair, who can require corrective action.

## 14. Phasing (draft)

| Phase | Activity | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (now) | No Tribal-related content is published. This draft and the draft policy are shared with no one outside the team until the project lead adopts them as working drafts | Project lead decision; compensation budget |
| 1 | Ask a small number of inter-Tribal organizations whether they will advise on TAC design. Revise this protocol with them | Their agreement |
| 2 | TAC constituted and adopts its own protocol and data policy | TAC charter adopted |
| 3 | TAC reviews category A content (competencies for non-Tribal professionals; L2; 7.1.1 rewrite) | TAC approval per item |
| 4 | Opt-in validation of Tribal-employer role variants (category B) with Tribal professionals, under Tribal research approval where required | TAC approval; Tribal research approvals |
| 5 | Any Tribally stewarded module (category C), only if Tribes propose it | Steward's terms |

## 15. Questions for Tribal reviewers

1. Should the project engage at all at this stage, given 2025–26 strains on Tribal staff time and on federal-Tribal relationships? If yes, through whom first?
2. Is a single TAC right, or should regional or subsector bodies (forestry, fire, fisheries, environmental programs, data governance) hold authority?
3. What compensation rates and structures are appropriate, and should payment go to Tribes or to individuals?
4. Should Tribal-employer role variants exist in EMST at all, or should EMST describe only how non-Tribal professionals work with Tribes?
5. Under what conditions, if any, may Tribal employers' public job postings be analyzed?
6. What should EMST give back?

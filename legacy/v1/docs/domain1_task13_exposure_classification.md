## Section 1 — Domain Character Summary

Domain 1 (Ecological & Scientific Knowledge) presents a fundamentally different automation exposure profile than any other domain classified to date. Across 120 tasks covering 40 competencies in 8 skill areas, the domain is overwhelmingly Augmentation (A), with 104 tasks (87%) classified as A, 9 as Human-Essential (H), 5 as Compressive Augmentation (Ac), and zero Substitution or Supervision calls. The vector distribution reflects the domain's character equally clearly: all 111 non-H tasks carry an AI vector, and zero tasks show Ro (physical robotics) or Cv (convergent AI-plus-robotics) exposure. Physical robotics simply does not appear in ecological science knowledge work — that layer of the profession lives in Domain 4 (operations). Domain 1 is protected not by physical complexity but by tacit knowledge density: 75 of 120 tasks (63%) are Hi-tacit, concentrated in the field science cohort (skill areas 1.1–1.4) and the interpretive synthesis functions throughout 1.6–1.8. The domain's central protection mechanism is the inseparability of analytical output from accumulated ecological judgment.

The 9 Human-Essential calls are the domain's structural signature. Every single H call falls on an FC Primary (Fieldcraft Primary) competency, and every single DC Primary competency produces zero H calls. This is the FC/DC structural pattern, confirmed without exception across all 8 skill areas and 120 tasks. The mechanism is consistent: H calls arise from irreducibly embodied, place-based judgment — soil horizon identification, wildlife behavioral observation, field state-transition recognition — that depends on sensory engagement with specific ecological conditions developed through repeated exposure to variable conditions in specific system types. Datacraft Primary competencies involve computational pipelines, modeling frameworks, and bioinformatics workflows where AI enters as an augmentation tool for analytical throughput, not as a substitute for embodied presence.

Career ladder destruction signals are minimal: one confirmed signal at 1.3.2 T3 (electronic data recording displacing manual field inventory data entry at P-level), and four S-trajectory Delphi flags concentrated in 1.6 (Biotechnology & Genetics) where bioinformatics pipeline automation has compressed the analytical execution layer to review-and-certify functions. Critically, all five Ac calls are C-position — the disruption is hitting the mid-career analytical execution layer rather than entry-level learning positions (with the one exception at 1.3.2 T3). Compared to Domain 4, which contains multiple P-position career ladder destruction signals from data entry and standardized monitoring task displacement, Domain 1's disruption is more selective and sits higher on the career ladder. Compared to Domain 6 (Policy, Economics, Governance), Domain 1 has more H calls by mechanism (embodied ecological judgment vs. relational institutional authority) but fewer governance-intensive Statecraft protection dynamics. The cross-domain implication is that workforce resilience planning for Domain 1 practitioners should center on the interpretation layer — the capacity to translate ecological analytical outputs into management-relevant judgments — rather than the technical execution layer, which is the compression target.

## Section 5 — New Instrument Findings

The following findings arose during Domain 1 classification and should be carried forward to Domains 3, 5, and 2.

**Finding 5.1 — FC/DC Structural Pattern (confirmed)**
H calls fall exclusively on FC Primary competencies across all 8 skill areas and 120 tasks, with zero exceptions. This is confirmed as a structural pattern rather than a correlation. For subsequent domains: any H call on a DC Primary competency is anomalous and must be flagged and documented with explicit reasoning. The analytical shortcut "FC Primary → watch for H; DC Primary → A expected" is now a standing rule with full Domain 1 evidentiary support.

**Finding 5.2 — T3 Horizon Pattern (refined)**
T3 tasks with synthesis/recommendation/planning function → Horizon 2. T3 tasks with monitoring/assessment/verification function → Horizon 1. The rule is functional, not positional: a T3 task that is structured as planning synthesis (1.7.5 T3: "build flexibility into long-term plans") classifies H2 even though T3 is syntactically the third task. Conversely, T1 and T2 monitoring tasks classify H1 regardless of position. Standing rule confirmed; no revision needed.

**Finding 5.3 — Technology-Integrated FC Rule (established at 1.4, generalized)**
Where technology (GPS, acoustic sensors, AI species identification tools, remote sensing platforms) is present as a standard deployment condition across organizational contexts — i.e., not a mode-split between technology-using and non-technology-using deployments — the task classifies A with field-H character documented, rather than H. This distinguishes "technology as universal standard tool" (→A) from "technology vs. no-technology as organizational mode split" (→modal A or H with mode-split flag). The rule has been applied consistently in 1.4 (wildlife survey), 1.5 (phenology monitoring), 1.7 (refugia mapping), and 1.8 (regenerative agriculture monitoring).

**Finding 5.4 — A/V Task-Description Framing Observation (methodological concern)**
The absence of V calls in Domain 1 is partly a methodological artifact: the source text writes all tasks at the active-practitioner register ("analyze," "interpret," "design"), which maps to A under the instrument criterion. V calls require explicit supervisor framing ("oversee," "review outputs from," "validate results of automated system"). This means the instrument is sensitive to how tasks are written, not just what the work involves. For subsequent domains: if practitioners in expert review would describe their relationship to an automated system as supervisory rather than as primary executors, the V classification should override the active-verb framing of the task description. This is a Delphi validation instrument design question.

**Finding 5.5 — C-position Ac Pattern in 1.6 (new)**
All five Ac calls in Domain 1 are C-position. The disruption in the analytical science skill areas is hitting the mid-career pipeline execution layer, not entry-level positions. This differs from the P-position Ac pattern in Domain 4 (data entry, standardized monitoring). For subsequent domains: when classifying Ac calls, note the career ladder position explicitly and distinguish between P-position CLD signals (workforce access disruption) and C-position Ac (analytical execution compression). The distinction has different implications for career pathway design and professional education.

**Finding 5.6 — Source-Text Register Effect on X-Position (new)**
Zero X-position calls across 120 tasks, despite 1.7 covering genuinely frontier scientific content. The X-position absence reflects the source text's consistent management-application register rather than a finding that all Domain 1 competencies are accessible at the C-level. For subsequent domains: if source text consistently writes tasks at the applied practitioner register for content that expert practitioners would describe as research-specialist territory, the X classification may need to be assigned based on practitioner reality rather than source text framing. Flag all such cases for Delphi.

# Fieldcraft / Datacraft / Statecraft Assignment Criteria
## Environmental Management Skills Taxonomy — Task 8 Output
### Status: Complete — Stage 2, February 2026
### Dependencies: None (informs Task 11)

---

## Purpose

This document formalizes the criteria for assigning primary and secondary craft tags to all 215 competencies in the Environmental Management Skills Taxonomy. It replaces over-inclusive auto-detected tags with deliberate assignments grounded in a single governing test: *what is the irreducible contribution of this competency — the element whose removal would cause the competency to collapse as a professional capacity?*

---

## Craft Definitions

**Fieldcraft:** Competencies grounded in embodied, place-based practice — direct ecosystem interaction, physical skills, sensory observation, field judgment. The irreducible value is tacit knowledge from being in the landscape: pattern recognition, anomaly detection, and contextual judgment that cannot be acquired from data or process alone.

**Datacraft:** Competencies centered on information management, analysis, modeling, and technology — collecting, structuring, analyzing, interpreting data. The irreducible value is making patterns visible and projecting futures: revealing structure in complexity at spatial and temporal scales that physical presence alone cannot achieve.

**Statecraft:** Competencies focused on institutional navigation, policy, stakeholder engagement, and governance — the social and political infrastructure enabling collective action. The irreducible value is enabling decisions across competing interests: the relational, procedural, and institutional work that translates technical capacity into authorized action.

---

## The Governing Test

Before applying the decision tree, ask the irreducibility question: **"If you removed [craft X]'s core contribution from this competency, would the competency still exist as a meaningful professional capacity?"**

- If removing place-based embodiment causes the competency to collapse — if it could not be performed remotely, from data, or through institutional process — it is Fieldcraft primary.
- If removing information synthesis causes the collapse — if the practitioner's value is fundamentally in making patterns visible through analysis — it is Datacraft primary.
- If removing institutional navigation causes the collapse — if the same technical actions without the relational and procedural work would fail to produce outcomes — it is Statecraft primary.

Note: Many competencies involve all three crafts in execution. The governing test resolves this by anchoring the assignment to the irreducible contribution, not the full range of what the competency involves.

---

## Decision Tree for Primary Assignment

**Step 1:** Identify the competency's defining deliverable or outcome. What does a practitioner who performs this competency well produce, do, or enable that a practitioner without this competency cannot? Name it specifically.

**Step 2:** Apply the irreducibility test to each craft. For each of the three crafts, ask whether the defining deliverable depends on that craft's irreducible contribution.

**Step 3:** Assign primary based on which craft is most essential to the defining deliverable. If a genuine tie exists between two crafts (the defining deliverable depends equally on both and would be substantially diminished by removal of either), flag as an integration point.

**Craft-specific indicators:**

Assign **Fieldcraft primary** when the competency's defining deliverable:
- Requires direct sensory engagement with ecological or physical systems conducted in the field
- Depends on tacit landscape knowledge — contextual judgment about conditions, patterns, or anomalies that develops through physical presence and cannot be transferred through data or protocol alone
- Involves physical manipulation of ecological systems at the site level (planting, harvesting, prescribed burning, sampling, capture-and-release)
- Produces ground truth that validates or calibrates downstream data products

Assign **Datacraft primary** when the defining deliverable:
- Makes patterns visible across spatial, temporal, or taxonomic scales beyond what direct observation can achieve
- Requires integration, transformation, or analysis of information from multiple sources or time points
- Projects futures — models, forecasts, scenario analyses, population viability assessments
- Produces an analytical product (map, model, dataset, analysis) that is itself the deliverable, not merely a tool enabling another action

Assign **Statecraft primary** when the defining deliverable:
- Requires navigating institutional relationships, regulatory processes, or political dynamics as core competency (not just as enabling condition)
- Produces authorization, agreement, or coordinated action across institutional boundaries
- Depends fundamentally on understanding competing interests, stakeholder positions, and governance structures
- Builds or maintains the institutional infrastructure — coalitions, regulatory relationships, governance frameworks — through which technical work becomes consequential

## Rules for Secondary Assignment

**Threshold question:** "Could a practitioner with strong primary craft skills but minimal secondary craft skills still perform this competency at intermediate proficiency?" If yes: no secondary tag. If no — if the absence of secondary craft would substantially impair performance even when primary craft is strong — assign secondary.

Secondary tags are warranted when:
- The competency requires methods, tools, or judgment from a second craft that cannot be learned incidentally while developing primary craft skills
- The competency's failure modes are as often attributable to secondary craft deficiency as to primary craft deficiency
- The secondary craft's contribution appears across most or all of the proficiency indicators, not just at advanced levels

Secondary tags are not warranted when:
- A craft provides context or background that enriches performance but is not required to achieve intermediate competency
- The secondary craft contribution is concentrated at the expert proficiency level only — note in rationale rather than adding a tag
- The craft appears only in specific sector applications rather than across the competency's general scope

**Maximum two secondary tags per competency.** If a competency appears to require all three crafts to perform at any level, the primary assignment is wrong — return to Step 1.

**Note on secondary Fieldcraft assignment for Datacraft-primary competencies:** Assign secondary Fieldcraft more liberally than the standard threshold might suggest. Many analytical and modeling competencies nominally require no field skills, but practitioners without field grounding make systematic errors — in model parameterization, in assessing whether results are ecologically plausible, in recognizing when field conditions violate model assumptions. The impairment is often invisible until practitioners encounter novel conditions where models and protocols fail.

## Standing Rules for Task 11

**Rule 1 — Complexity-dependent primary assignments:** When simple and complex applications of a competency have different primary assignments, tag based on modal application, document the exception in rationale, and flag for Task 14 review. Do not split competencies during Task 11.

**Rule 2 — Adaptive management system design:** Designing AM systems = Statecraft primary. Operating monitoring/analysis within them = Datacraft primary. Evaluating field results = Datacraft primary with Fieldcraft secondary. This propagates into all OBAM-adjacent competencies in Domain 3.

**Rule 3 — Asymmetric error caution:** Over-assignment of Datacraft is the most likely systematic error given the taxonomy's construction history. Apply the irreducibility test rather than defaulting to Datacraft when uncertain.

## Relationship to the Five Functional Positions

The three crafts and five functional positions are orthogonal lenses that partially overlap but cannot be mapped one-to-one.

| Functional Position | Primary Craft Tendency | Secondary | Key Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensing & Generating | Fieldcraft | Datacraft | — |
| Structuring & Curating | Datacraft | Fieldcraft | — |
| Analyzing & Interpreting | Datacraft | Statecraft (where analysis directly serves governance) | — |
| Integrating & Presenting | Datacraft + Statecraft (co-equal) | — | Only position where two crafts are consistently co-equal |
| Deciding & Directing | Statecraft | Datacraft | See key mismatch below |

**Key structural mismatches:**

1. The Five Functional Positions framework places Deciding & Directing at the governance-interface end of information flow, implying Statecraft dominance. But substantial Fieldcraft competencies also operate at the Deciding position — firefighters making real-time suppression decisions, wildlife biologists calling whether to abort a capture, restoration practitioners making site-level triage decisions. These are decisions made in the landscape, with tacit knowledge as the primary input. The craft framework handles these more accurately by distinguishing Fieldcraft decisions from Statecraft decisions by irreducible contribution, regardless of position in the information flow.

2. Fieldcraft appears only at the Sensing & Generating position in the Five Functional Positions framework. In the craft framework, Fieldcraft competencies appear at every workflow stage — including Planning (field-informed assessment), Environmental Review (ground-truthing), and Adaptive Management (field-based evaluation of whether interventions worked). The craft framework corrects a systematic underrepresentation of field judgment at stages after data collection.

3. The Integrating & Presenting position is the only functional position where two crafts are regularly co-equal, explaining why Stages 1 and 2 (Planning and Environmental Review) are the most craft-mixed stages in the workflow stage analysis.

## Edge Cases: Ten Integration Points

These competencies have the most difficult primary assignments and are documented for Task 11 reviewers. They represent structural integration points in the taxonomy, not tagging failures.

| # | Competency | Assignment | Rationale | Flag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remote sensing image interpretation | Datacraft primary, Fieldcraft secondary | Defining deliverable is pattern recognition in data products — making landscape-scale spatial patterns visible. Field grounding reduces error rates in novel ecosystems but is not required for intermediate performance. | Delphi question: where does intermediate proficiency begin without field experience? |
| 2 | Traditional Ecological Knowledge integration | Statecraft primary, Fieldcraft secondary | Without the relational and institutional navigation, TEK cannot be accessed or appropriately used regardless of ecological knowledge. | Most important integration point in taxonomy; sits at intersection of all three crafts and Lenses 2 and 4. |
| 3 | Prescribed fire planning | Fieldcraft primary, Statecraft secondary | The plan's core content is ecological and operational; authorization and coordination are enabling conditions, not the defining deliverable. | Defensible 50/50; expect practitioner disagreement given permitting burden. |
| 4 | Stakeholder power and interest mapping | Statecraft primary, Datacraft secondary where quantitative network analysis methods are employed | Analysis serves the purpose of revealing political landscape structure — Statecraft is the purpose, Datacraft is the method. | — |
| 5 | Species status assessment for regulatory listing | Datacraft primary, Statecraft secondary | The practitioner's core value is in the quality of the evidence synthesis. The regulatory framing defines the question but not the irreducible contribution. | Agency practitioners may experience this the other way around. |
| 6 | Adaptive management framework design | Statecraft primary, Datacraft secondary | The institutional learning architecture — monitoring endpoints, decision triggers, management response protocols — is the irreducible contribution. Analytical specification of what evidence triggers what response is essential content but can be supplied by collaborators. | **CONFIRMED by OBAM rule. Propagates: designing AM systems = Statecraft; operating within them = Datacraft.** |
| 7 | Conservation genetics — genetic rescue design | Datacraft primary, Fieldcraft secondary | The defining deliverable is the analytical judgment about source populations and exchange rates for population viability improvement. | Primary Intervention Ethics trigger in taxonomy. |
| 8 | Environmental justice screening / cumulative impact analysis | Co-equal integration point | Analytical content (Datacraft) and regulatory input function (Statecraft) are genuinely co-equal in regulatory contexts. | If coded as distinct competency, primary note should explain the judgment. |
| 9 | Riparian buffer design | Fieldcraft primary, Datacraft secondary for competencies specifying quantitative hydrologic or regulatory dimensions | Simple applications = Fieldcraft dominant; complex applications = co-equal. Tag based on modal application; document exception; flag for Task 14. | **Complexity-dependent standing rule applies here.** |
| 10 | Mitigation banking and conservation easement valuation | Statecraft primary, Datacraft secondary | The value of a mitigation credit is defined by the regulatory system, not by ecological quality alone. Understanding the regulatory credit accounting framework is the irreducible contribution. | Counterintuitive to practitioners who experience this as primarily technical. |

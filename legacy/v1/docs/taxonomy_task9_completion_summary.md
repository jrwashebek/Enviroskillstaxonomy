## Governing Principles Applied

**Core tag criterion:** A credentialed practitioner in this profession family would regard not having this competency as a meaningful gap in their professional preparation.

**Supporting tag criterion:** Practitioners in this family regularly apply this competency in their work, but the competency is not distinctive to their professional identity.

**Boundary test for ambiguous cases:** Reason from the *content* of the competency, not from who tends to occupy associated roles. The former is correct; the latter produces scope creep.

**Special rules applied:**
- Conservation Science (F5) / Wildlife & Fisheries (F2) co-Core rule: applied where competency involves generating population knowledge AND informing management decisions (1.4.1, 1.6.5, 5.4.4)
- Quantitative Ecologist product-vs-tool test: applied in Domain 2 throughout
- F3 trajectory criterion: held consistently — restoration boundary criteria requires knowledge of trajectory toward a target state; competencies organized around risk reduction or reference-state-independent management were assigned Supporting

## Key Structural Findings

### Finding 1: Domain-Family Correspondence Is Architecturally Sound
Every profession family's Core tag concentration aligns with the domain(s) their boundary criteria names as primary: F5 in D1, F7 in D2, F14 in D3, F12/F3/F10/F1 in D4, F11/F13 in D5, F8/F15/F9 in D6. No family's Core tags are concentrated outside their predicted domain territory. This confirms that the boundary criteria are internally consistent across all 215 competencies.

### Finding 2: Three Families, Three Relationships to the Field (Architectural Story)
The Core tag distribution reveals three structurally distinct professional identities and their relationships to environmental management:
- **F5 (34 Core tags, spanning 5 domains):** Knowledge production. Conservation Science is the knowledge-production infrastructure that nearly every environmental management domain draws on — broad because ecological knowledge underlies all practice.
- **F7 (32 Core tags, concentrated in D2):** Technical systems. Environmental Data Science is extremely deep but almost perfectly domain-bound — the technical infrastructure professionals who build the systems others use.
- **F8 (25 Core tags, concentrated in D5–D6):** Governance infrastructure. Environmental Policy appears concentrated in two registers — stakeholder engagement as process governance (D5) and regulatory/legal framework production (D6) — because governance operates at the interface between knowledge, action, and accountability.

This comparison should anchor the Delphi packet introduction and the GitBook structural overview.

### Finding 3: The Foundational Professional Preparation Stratum
Approximately 20 competencies across the taxonomy produce zero Core tags from any profession family. These are not tagging failures but a structural finding: the taxonomy contains competencies that all practitioners need without any family claiming them as identity-constituting. Three distinct categories:

| Category | Location | Character | Curriculum Design Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Administrative management infrastructure | 3.1–3.3, 3.5 | Career-stage dependent (increases with seniority) | Include in professional development tracks, not entry-level disciplinary curricula |
| Operational safety | 4.4 | Career-stage independent (required from day one) | Include as universal foundational training prerequisite across all programs |
| Public-facing service delivery | 5.5 | Service-context dependent | Include in public land management and interpretation program curricula specifically |
| General professional communication | 5.1.1, 5.1.2, 5.1.4 | Universal | Include in foundational professional preparation regardless of family |

**Recommendation for Delphi packet:** Name this stratum explicitly as "Foundational Professional Preparation" — not a new tagging tier, but a structural observation that these competencies are invisible in profession-family filtered GitBook views and need explicit treatment in curriculum design guidance. The filtered views are correct for professional identity purposes; the invisibility is a display feature, not a gap.

### Finding 4: F13's Defining Conjunction Is Confirmed
F13 (Environmental Health & Justice) produces 5 Core tags in Domain 5 (equity analysis: 5.3.5, 5.6.2, 5.6.3, 5.6.5, 5.4.4 partial) and 6 Core tags in Domain 6 (technical rigor: all of 6.7 plus co-Core on 6.7.2). Zero Core tags elsewhere. The distribution confirms the boundary criteria's defining conjunction — technical assessment capacity AND equity analysis held simultaneously — is structurally legible in the taxonomy. A practitioner filtering by F13 will find competencies distributed across two distinct domains for two distinct professional reasons.

### Finding 5: Stakeholder Engagement Is Governance Infrastructure
F8 produces 7 Core tags in Domain 5 (stakeholder engagement and cultural resources) and 18 Core tags in Domain 6 — 25 total. The concentration confirms that engagement processes in environmental management are regulatory and governance functions first, not educational or communicative ones. This has direct implications for where engagement training sits in workforce development programming: it belongs in regulatory process and governance curricula alongside policy professionals, not exclusively in environmental education programs.

## Coverage Question for Delphi Panel

**F4 (Water Resources & Watershed Science) at 8 total Core tags** is the lowest Core tag count among the eight field-oriented families. Given the centrality of water resources practice to environmental management, this may reflect:
- Taxonomy source document coverage gaps (Domain 1 soil/water science and Domain 4 watershed treatment have strong F4 presence, but Domain 6 water law and water markets may be underrepresented in the source text)
- A genuinely narrower professional identity footprint than neighboring families like F1 (12) or F2 (10)
- Appropriate scoping — F4 Core tags appear exactly where the boundary criteria predicts (hydrology, water quality, watershed treatment, green infrastructure)

Recommend Delphi reviewers with water resources backgrounds assess whether F4's Core tag distribution reflects their professional preparation expectations.

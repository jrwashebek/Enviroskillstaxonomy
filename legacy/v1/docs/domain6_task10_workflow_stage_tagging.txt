# Task 10: Workflow Stage Tagging — Domain 6: Policy, Planning, Law & Economics
## Environmental Management Skills Taxonomy
## Status: Complete — February 2026

---

## Stage Key
S1 = Planning & Strategy
S2 = Environmental Review & Compliance
S3 = Permitting & Authorization
S4 = Implementation & Field Operations
S5 = Monitoring & Assessment
S6 = Adaptive Management & Learning

## Tagging Cap
2 Primary maximum + 2 Secondary maximum per competency.
Cap exceptions flagged explicitly with justification.

## Reform Sensitivity Controlled Vocabulary
NEPA-timeline | CatEx-scope | S2-S3-boundary | digital-permitting | consultation-reform | one-federal-decision

## SKILL AREA 6.1: ENVIRONMENTAL LAW & REGULATION

Code  | Title                          | Primary | Secondary | Reform Sensitivity                                              | Notes
------|--------------------------------|---------|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|-------
6.1.1 | Environmental policy analysis  | S2      | S1        | NEPA-timeline, S2-S3-boundary                                   | S2 Primary: analyzing environmental policy — interpreting statutory requirements, regulatory history, agency guidance, and case law to characterize the compliance landscape for a proposed action — produces the legal and regulatory characterization that is the foundational analytical layer of environmental review documentation. Policy analysis answers "what legal framework applies and what does it require?" before any project-specific analysis can proceed. S1 Secondary: environmental policy analysis is also a planning function — understanding the regulatory environment shapes which management approaches are feasible and how programs must be designed to maintain compliance. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (NEPA policy analysis must account for a regulatory framework that is actively changing; policy analysts must track CEQ rulemakings, agency guidance updates, and court decisions that alter NEPA requirements in real time, creating a moving-target problem that timeline compression exacerbates); S2-S3-boundary (the S2/S3 boundary is itself a policy analysis question — determining when review ends and authorization begins requires ongoing policy interpretation, particularly as concurrent review models blur that sequence).
6.1.2 | Regulatory compliance assessment | S2    | S3        | S2-S3-boundary, NEPA-timeline                                   | S2 Primary: assessing regulatory compliance — determining whether a proposed project or existing program meets the requirements of applicable environmental statutes, regulations, and permit conditions — is the definitional environmental review function. Compliance assessment characterizes the gap between what is proposed and what is required, which is the analytical core of the review stage. S3 Secondary: regulatory compliance assessment informs permitting directly — compliance findings determine which authorizations are required, what conditions must be met before permits can issue, and whether existing authorizations remain valid. Reform sensitivity: S2-S3-boundary (compliance assessment exists at the precise boundary between review and permitting; the competency that characterizes compliance simultaneously determines whether authorization can proceed, making it structurally co-located with the S2/S3 boundary that reform proposals most directly contest); NEPA-timeline (regulatory compliance assessment under NEPA is subject to timeline reform; compressed review schedules constrain the thoroughness of compliance characterization, which is a litigation risk rather than merely a quality concern).
6.1.3 | Permitting procedures          | S3      | S2        | NEPA-timeline, S2-S3-boundary, one-federal-decision             | S3 Primary: understanding and navigating permitting procedures — the specific procedural requirements, submission standards, agency review timelines, and decision criteria for permit applications across relevant regulatory programs — is the definitional Stage 3 competency. Permitting procedures knowledge is what practitioners use to move from review completion to formal authorization. S2 Secondary: permitting procedures knowledge informs how environmental review documentation must be structured to support permit applications; understanding what the permitting stage will require shapes what review-stage documentation must produce. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (NEPA completion is a statutory prerequisite for many federal permits; NEPA timeline reform directly affects when the permitting stage can begin and how much time is available for preparation); S2-S3-boundary (permitting procedures knowledge is the most explicit S2/S3 boundary competency in the taxonomy — it governs the handoff itself); one-federal-decision (OFD's concurrent review model restructures permitting procedures for major federal actions, collapsing the sequential S2→S3 pathway into a parallel process that requires different procedural knowledge).
6.1.4 | NEPA process implementation    | S2      | S3        | NEPA-timeline, CatEx-scope, S2-S3-boundary, one-federal-decision | S2 Primary: implementing NEPA processes — scoping, alternatives analysis, impact characterization, public involvement, document preparation, and agency decision-making — is the definitional Stage 2 function. NEPA process implementation produces the environmental review documentation that is Stage 2's primary professional output. S3 Secondary: NEPA completion is the legal trigger for most federal permit issuance; NEPA implementation directly enables and shapes the permitting stage. Reform sensitivity: this competency carries four simultaneous reform codes, the highest reform exposure of any competency in the taxonomy. NEPA-timeline (NEPA timeline compression is the central federal permitting reform target; implementing NEPA under page limits, time constraints, and abbreviated scoping requirements requires practitioners to navigate a fundamentally different process than the one for which existing practice norms were developed); CatEx-scope (CatEx expansion reduces the volume of actions requiring NEPA analysis, directly affecting the demand for NEPA process implementation competency and the conditions under which it is exercised); S2-S3-boundary (NEPA completion simultaneously closes Stage 2 and unlocks Stage 3, making NEPA process implementation the quintessential S2/S3 boundary competency); one-federal-decision (OFD explicitly restructures NEPA implementation for major infrastructure actions, requiring NEPA practitioners to coordinate multiple cooperating agencies, manage parallel review tracks, and produce integrated documentation on compressed timelines).
6.1.5 | Enforcement procedures         | S2      | S5        | S2-S3-boundary                                                  | S2 Primary: understanding and applying environmental enforcement procedures — compliance inspection protocols, notice of violation issuance, enforcement order administration, penalty assessment, and referral pathways — is an environmental review and compliance function. Enforcement is the mechanism through which compliance requirements are made operative; it is the consequence side of the regulatory review framework. S5 Secondary: enforcement procedures intersect with monitoring — compliance monitoring generates the observational data that triggers enforcement action, and enforcement outcomes are tracked as indicators of regulatory program effectiveness. Reform sensitivity: S2-S3-boundary (enforcement authority is itself subject to the S2/S3 boundary question; determining when a compliance failure requires enforcement action versus permit modification or re-review is a live area of regulatory interpretation that reform proposals affecting the boundary directly implicate).

## SKILL AREA 6.2: LAND USE PLANNING

Code  | Title                              | Primary | Secondary | Reform Sensitivity              | Notes
------|------------------------------------|---------|-----------|---------------------------------|-------
6.2.1 | Comprehensive land use planning    | S1      | S2        | NEPA-timeline                   | S1 Primary: comprehensive land use planning — producing general plans, land use frameworks, and long-range planning documents that establish the spatial and policy framework for resource management decisions — is a definitional Stage 1 output. Comprehensive planning precedes and enables all subsequent project-level review and authorization. S2 Secondary: comprehensive land use plans are subject to environmental review and establish the programmatic framework within which subsequent NEPA analyses tier; plan-level NEPA analysis is a legally significant Stage 2 function that practitioners of this competency must navigate. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (programmatic and plan-level NEPA processes are among the longest and most complex; timeline reform that compresses individual project NEPA timelines will affect whether adequate tiering from plan-level NEPA remains feasible, with downstream implications for project-level review burden).
6.2.2 | Zoning and land classification     | S1      | S3        | —                               | S1 Primary: developing and applying zoning and land classification systems — defining land use categories, establishing overlay zones, classifying lands for management intensity — is a planning output that precedes and structures authorization decisions. Zoning and classification answers "what uses and activities are permitted in this location?" S3 Secondary: zoning designations are themselves authorization instruments in many jurisdictions — zoning variances, conditional use permits, and rezoning approvals are Stage 3 functions that depend on prior zoning classification work. The S3 Secondary reflects that classification (S1) enables the permitting system (S3) that administers departures from it.
6.2.3 | Public land management planning   | S1      | S2        | NEPA-timeline, CatEx-scope      | S1 Primary: producing public land management plans — forest plans, resource management plans, land and resource management plans, unit management plans — is the large-scale planning function of public land management agencies. These documents are the Stage 1 outputs that govern subsequent project-level activity across millions of acres. S2 Secondary: land management planning on federal lands is conducted through NEPA processes — programmatic EISs that establish the plan-level environmental review foundation for tiering. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (plan-level NEPA processes are the largest and most complex in the federal system; proposals to streamline plan revision processes and impose page and timeline limits on programmatic EISs directly affect this competency's primary professional output); CatEx-scope (the eligibility of plan amendments for CatEx treatment is an active reform question, as many plan-level changes are proposed under categorical exclusions, bypassing the programmatic NEPA process that these large planning documents normally require).
6.2.4 | Community planning integration    | S1      | S2        | consultation-reform             | S1 Primary: integrating community perspectives, needs, and priorities into land use and resource management planning — bridging local knowledge and community values with formal planning processes — is a planning function. Community planning integration shapes plan content before review and authorization proceed. S2 Secondary: community integration is a required component of NEPA environmental justice analysis and public involvement procedures; formal NEPA processes require documentation of community perspectives on environmental impacts and alternatives. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (community integration in planning depends on sustained, relationship-based engagement; consultation reform proposals that compress public participation timelines directly affect the quality of community integration, reducing it toward extractive data collection rather than genuine co-production of planning knowledge).
6.2.5 | Scenario development and analysis | S1      | S6        | —                               | S1 Primary: developing and analyzing planning scenarios — projecting outcomes under alternative management approaches, modeling ecosystem responses to different policy and investment choices, characterizing tradeoffs — is a planning function that structures decision-making about alternatives. Scenario analysis is the analytical method by which planning considers futures before committing to a course of action. S6 Secondary: scenario frameworks developed in planning are revisited during adaptive management when monitoring evidence reveals that initial assumptions were incorrect or that conditions have shifted; updating scenario models based on observed outcomes is an adaptive management function. The S1/S6 pairing reflects the iterative character of well-designed adaptive management: scenarios structure planning and are revised through learning.

## SKILL AREA 6.3: RESOURCE ECONOMICS

Code  | Title                                  | Primary | Secondary | Reform Sensitivity                   | Notes
------|----------------------------------------|---------|-----------|--------------------------------------|-------
6.3.1 | Natural resource valuation             | S1      | S2        | NEPA-timeline                        | S1 Primary: valuing natural resources — applying contingent valuation, hedonic pricing, replacement cost, and other valuation methods to characterize the economic worth of ecosystem assets — is an analytical planning function. Resource valuation produces the economic baseline that informs conservation investment decisions, mitigation design, and resource management tradeoff analysis. S2 Secondary: natural resource valuation is a required input to NEPA economic analysis — characterizing the economic value of resources that would be affected by a proposed action is a standard component of EAs and EISs for resource-affecting projects. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (natural resource valuation for NEPA purposes requires data collection and analysis that timeline compression constrains; valuation studies that take months to complete are vulnerable in compressed review windows).
6.3.2 | Ecosystem services assessment          | S1      | S2        | NEPA-timeline                        | S1 Primary: assessing ecosystem services — quantifying the provisioning, regulating, cultural, and supporting services that ecosystems deliver to human communities — is a planning function that characterizes what is at stake in management decisions and provides the ecological economic basis for conservation investment. Ecosystem services assessment produces the valuation framework that shapes planning priorities. S2 Secondary: ecosystem services assessment is increasingly required in NEPA analyses as a method for characterizing the full range of impacts on human wellbeing from proposed actions; CEQ guidance encourages ecosystem services characterization as part of effects analysis. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (ecosystem services assessment is methodologically intensive; incorporating credible ecosystem services analysis into NEPA documents requires data and expertise that timeline compression makes difficult to procure for specific project analyses).
6.3.3 | Market-based conservation mechanisms  | S1      | S3        | digital-permitting                   | S1 Primary: designing and implementing market-based conservation mechanisms — payment for ecosystem services programs, conservation easement markets, biodiversity offset programs, voluntary carbon markets — is a planning function. Market design is a Stage 1 output that enables subsequent authorization and transaction execution. S3 Secondary: market-based mechanisms typically require regulatory authorization to function — conservation easement deeds, carbon credit registration, and mitigation bank instruments are Stage 3 authorization products. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (conservation markets are increasingly transacted through digital platforms; blockchain-based credit registries, digital conservation easement tracking systems, and automated payment for ecosystem services platforms are live areas of conservation market digitization directly affecting this competency).
6.3.4 | Economic impact analysis              | S2      | S1        | NEPA-timeline, S2-S3-boundary        | S2 Primary: analyzing economic impacts of proposed actions — characterizing effects on employment, income, community tax base, regional economies, and affected industries — is a required component of NEPA environmental review for major federal actions. Economic impact analysis is the socioeconomic dimension of the environmental effects characterization that NEPA mandates. S1 Secondary: economic impact analysis also informs planning — understanding the economic consequences of alternative management approaches shapes which alternatives are viable and how tradeoffs are characterized. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (economic impact analysis for major EISs is methodologically intensive, requiring regional economic models, baseline data, and scenario projections that timeline compression constrains); S2-S3-boundary (economic impact characterization in NEPA shapes the conditions under which permits can be issued; the economic findings in an EA or EIS directly influence permit conditions, making economic analysis a structural bridge between S2 and S3).
6.3.5 | Carbon credit and offset programs     | S3      | S1        | digital-permitting, S2-S3-boundary   | S3 Primary: administering carbon credit and offset programs — verifying offset project eligibility, issuing credits, managing registry accounts, executing transactions, maintaining offset integrity — is an authorization function. Carbon credits are regulatory instruments — their issuance and transfer constitute a form of environmental authorization that quantifies and validates offset claims. S1 Secondary: carbon offset program design — identifying eligible project types, structuring additionality requirements, designing monitoring and verification protocols — is a planning function that precedes authorization. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (carbon offset registries are digital-native systems; blockchain verification, automated monitoring and reporting, and digital credit issuance are the infrastructure through which this competency operates); S2-S3-boundary (carbon offsets sit at the S2/S3 boundary — they are authorized as a compliance mechanism within environmental review frameworks and executed through registry instruments that function as permits; the growing use of carbon credits as NEPA mitigation measures makes this boundary-straddling particularly analytically significant).

## SKILL AREA 6.4: POLICY DEVELOPMENT

Code  | Title                           | Primary | Secondary | Reform Sensitivity                                         | Notes
------|---------------------------------|---------|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------|-------
6.4.1 | Policy research and analysis    | S1      | S2        | NEPA-timeline                                              | S1 Primary: researching and analyzing policy options — reviewing existing policy frameworks, synthesizing evidence about policy effectiveness, characterizing policy alternatives and their tradeoffs — is a planning function. Policy research and analysis produces the analytical foundation on which policy proposals are built. S2 Secondary: policy analysis is applied in environmental review — analyzing the regulatory policy context for proposed actions, assessing policy consistency, and characterizing regulatory requirements are standard NEPA analytical functions. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (policy research for NEPA purposes must characterize a rapidly changing regulatory environment; permitting reform itself generates new policy to be analyzed, creating a recursive demand on this competency).
6.4.2 | Policy drafting and review      | S1      | S2        | NEPA-timeline, CatEx-scope, S2-S3-boundary                 | S1 Primary: drafting policy documents — regulations, guidance documents, agency orders, programmatic policies — is a planning function. Policy drafting produces the instruments that structure what review, permitting, and implementation must accomplish. S2 Secondary: policy drafting in environmental contexts frequently produces review-related documents — guidance on NEPA implementation, categorical exclusion determinations, agency policy on consultation procedures — that constitute Stage 2 documentation. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (permitting reform is primarily achieved through policy drafting — CEQ rulemaking, agency NEPA guidance revision, and programmatic policy changes that alter timeline requirements are produced by practitioners of this competency, placing it at the origin of the reform causal chain); CatEx-scope (CatEx expansion is implemented through policy documents — new CatEx determinations, agency extraordinary circumstance guidance revisions — drafted by practitioners of this competency); S2-S3-boundary (policy documents that define the S2/S3 boundary are produced by practitioners of this competency; the boundary is itself a policy artifact).
6.4.3 | Regulatory impact assessment    | S2      | S1        | NEPA-timeline, one-federal-decision                        | S2 Primary: assessing the regulatory impacts of proposed rules, guidance changes, and policy decisions — characterizing economic costs and benefits, distributional effects, and compliance burden — is an environmental review function in the regulatory development context. Regulatory impact assessment is the NEPA-analog for rulemaking: it characterizes the effects of proposed regulatory actions before they are finalized. S1 Secondary: regulatory impact assessment also informs planning — understanding the regulatory burden of alternative policy approaches shapes which options are feasible and how they should be sequenced. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (regulatory impact assessments of NEPA reform proposals must characterize the effects of changing NEPA itself — a reflexive application of this competency that is analytically and politically significant); one-federal-decision (OFD requires regulatory impact assessment for the interagency coordination requirements it imposes; assessing the compliance burden of OFD-mandated concurrent review is a live application of this competency).
6.4.4 | Public comment management       | S2      | S3        | NEPA-timeline, digital-permitting, consultation-reform     | S2 Primary: managing public comment processes — receiving, organizing, analyzing, and formally responding to public comments on draft environmental review documents — is a legally required Stage 2 function. NEPA requires that agencies respond to substantive public comments in final EIS documents; comment management is a core procedural competency for environmental review. S3 Secondary: public comment processes are also required for many permit applications — NPDES permit public notice, Section 404 individual permit comment periods, and state environmental permit public review processes all require comment management competency at Stage 3. Reform sensitivity: NEPA-timeline (compressed comment periods reduce the quality of public input and increase legal challenge risk for final documents; NEPA timeline reform consistently targets comment periods as efficiency opportunities, creating tradeoffs between speed and participation quality); digital-permitting (electronic comment management systems — regulations.gov, agency-specific portals, e-docket systems — are central infrastructure of modern NEPA implementation; managing digital comment volumes and AI-assisted comment analysis tools are live areas of digital government development); consultation-reform (public comment management is distinct from but overlapping with tribal consultation; reform proposals that restructure participation timelines affect comment processes and consultation simultaneously).
6.4.5 | Legislative process navigation  | S1      | S3        | one-federal-decision                                       | S1 Primary: navigating legislative processes — understanding how environmental legislation is developed, amended, and implemented; engaging in the legislative process on behalf of conservation interests; tracking legislative developments that affect program viability — is a planning function. Legislative navigation informs strategic planning by characterizing the policy environment and identifying opportunities to advance conservation through statutory change. S3 Secondary: legislative authorizations are a category of Stage 3 authorization — congressional project authorizations, appropriations riders that affect permitting, and statutory CatEx provisions are legislative instruments that function as permits. Reform sensitivity: one-federal-decision (OFD is a policy framework with significant legislative dimensions; navigating the legislative environment in which OFD operates, and potential statutory codification of permitting reform, directly engages this competency's legislative process expertise).

## SKILL AREA 6.5: INTERGOVERNMENTAL RELATIONS

Code  | Title                                    | Primary | Secondary | Reform Sensitivity                              | Notes
------|------------------------------------------|---------|-----------|-------------------------------------------------|-------
6.5.1 | Tribal consultation and coordination     | S3      | S2        | consultation-reform, one-federal-decision       | S3 Primary: conducting tribal consultation — the government-to-government engagement process required by NHPA Section 106, NEPA, and agency-specific consultation policies — is an authorization function. Tribal consultation is a prerequisite for federal authorizations affecting tribal interests; completing consultation in good faith is a legal condition of permit and authorization validity. S2 Secondary: tribal consultation is simultaneously a component of environmental review — characterizing tribal interests, documenting consultation process, and addressing concerns raised by tribal governments are Stage 2 analytical and procedural requirements. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (tribal consultation reform is the most politically and legally contested dimension of permitting reform; proposals to streamline consultation timelines risk violating trust responsibilities and generating litigation that defeats the efficiency objectives reform seeks); one-federal-decision (OFD's interagency coordination requirements create new complexity for tribal consultation, as multiple agencies with different consultation relationships to the same tribes must coordinate their government-to-government engagement).
6.5.2 | Federal-state-local coordination        | S3      | S1        | one-federal-decision, consultation-reform       | S3 Primary: coordinating federal, state, and local governmental interests in environmental management — negotiating intergovernmental agreements, managing cooperative relationships, resolving jurisdictional conflicts — is an authorization function. Intergovernmental coordination produces the agreements that authorize cross-jurisdictional environmental programs. S1 Secondary: federal-state-local coordination shapes planning — understanding intergovernmental dynamics and existing agreements frames what planning approaches are feasible within multi-jurisdictional landscapes. Reform sensitivity: one-federal-decision (OFD is explicitly a federal-state coordination framework for major infrastructure; its implementation requires federal-state coordination at an intensity and formality that this competency must support); consultation-reform (state environmental review processes are increasingly integrated with federal consultation requirements; coordination between state and federal consultation obligations is an active area of intergovernmental complexity).
6.5.3 | Interagency agreements development      | S3      | S1        | one-federal-decision, S2-S3-boundary            | S3 Primary: developing interagency agreements — memoranda of understanding, programmatic agreements, interagency mitigation commitments, joint management agreements — is an authorization function. Interagency agreements are the formal instruments through which multiple agencies authorize and commit to shared environmental management responsibilities. S1 Secondary: interagency agreement development also has planning dimensions — identifying the need for agreement, scoping agreement content, and negotiating framework provisions are planning activities that precede formal agreement execution. Reform sensitivity: one-federal-decision (OFD explicitly requires interagency agreements as coordination instruments for concurrent review; developing the MOU frameworks through which multiple agencies implement OFD is a primary professional application of this competency); S2-S3-boundary (programmatic agreements under Section 106 are the paradigm case of an instrument that simultaneously closes environmental review and opens authorization — these agreements are developed by practitioners of this competency and define the S2/S3 boundary in practice).
6.5.4 | Cross-jurisdictional planning           | S1      | S3        | —                                               | S1 Primary: planning across jurisdictional boundaries — designing conservation strategies, habitat connectivity corridors, and watershed management programs that span multiple administrative units — is a planning function. Cross-jurisdictional planning produces the strategic frameworks that align management across administrative boundaries. S3 Secondary: cross-jurisdictional programs require multi-party authorization — conservation easements across multiple ownerships, joint management agreements, multi-agency permit instruments — that must be negotiated and executed as Stage 3 products. The S3 Secondary reflects that cross-jurisdictional strategies must be authorized to be implemented, and those authorizations often require novel or complex permitting arrangements.
6.5.5 | International conservation agreements   | S3      | S1        | consultation-reform                             | S3 Primary: negotiating and implementing international conservation agreements — species treaties, transboundary habitat agreements, international conservation financing instruments — is an authorization function at the international level. International agreements are the authorization instruments that enable cross-border conservation programs. S1 Secondary: international agreement engagement also informs planning — understanding international frameworks, identifying treaty opportunities, and designing programs compatible with international obligations are planning functions that precede negotiation. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (indigenous and local community consultation requirements in international conservation agreements — FPIC obligations under international law, Convention on Biological Diversity community protocols — parallel domestic consultation reform debates; how consultation obligations are interpreted in international contexts shapes the field norms that domestic reform proposals must account for).

## SKILL AREA 6.6: ENVIRONMENTAL MARKETS

Code  | Title                                  | Primary | Secondary | Reform Sensitivity                   | Notes
------|----------------------------------------|---------|-----------|--------------------------------------|-------
6.6.1 | Carbon accounting and quantification   | S5      | S3        | digital-permitting, S2-S3-boundary   | S5 Primary: measuring, reporting, and verifying carbon stocks and fluxes — applying quantification methodologies, conducting field-based carbon inventories, processing remote sensing data for carbon accounting, generating third-party verifiable carbon datasets — is a systematic monitoring function. Carbon accounting is outcome monitoring for carbon markets and climate programs; it answers "are the carbon outcomes we committed to actually materializing?" S3 Secondary: carbon accounting produces the verified data required for carbon credit issuance — the authorization instrument of carbon markets. Carbon accounting at the verification stage is a prerequisite for credit issuance, placing it in Stage 3 territory. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (digital MRV — monitoring, reporting, and verification — systems are the technical infrastructure of carbon markets; automated carbon accounting, satellite-based stock verification, and blockchain-secured reporting are restructuring how carbon quantification connects to credit authorization); S2-S3-boundary (carbon accounting sits precisely at the S2/S3 boundary — it produces the verified evidence (S5) that enables credit issuance (S3), with the accounting methodology itself subject to review requirements that straddle both stages).
6.6.2 | Biodiversity credits and habitat banking | S3    | S1        | digital-permitting, S2-S3-boundary   | S3 Primary: developing and administering biodiversity credits and habitat banks — establishing mitigation bank instruments, managing habitat bank service areas, processing credit transactions, maintaining bank ledgers — is an authorization function. Biodiversity credits and habitat bank instruments are regulatory authorizations; they constitute the permit instruments through which compensatory mitigation obligations under Section 404 and state equivalents are satisfied. S1 Secondary: biodiversity credit program design — identifying banking opportunities, designing habitat credit algorithms, developing service area delineation frameworks — is a planning function that precedes banking instrument development. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (habitat mitigation banking is increasingly administered through digital platforms; RIBITS — the national mitigation banking tracking system — is the live digital permitting infrastructure for this competency); S2-S3-boundary (habitat banks are established through NEPA review and mitigation instruments simultaneously, with the mitigation banking instrument serving both as NEPA mitigation commitment documentation and as a permit authorizing future credit transactions).
6.6.3 | Environmental market transactions      | S3      | S1        | digital-permitting                   | S3 Primary: executing environmental market transactions — purchasing carbon credits, trading renewable energy certificates, transacting biodiversity offsets, administering conservation easement transactions — is an authorization function. Environmental market transactions are the execution of regulatory instruments; each transaction consummated is an authorization event that fulfills or creates compliance obligations. S1 Secondary: environmental market transaction competency informs strategic planning — understanding market conditions, transaction costs, and credit availability shapes conservation finance planning and organizational investment strategy. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (environmental markets are digital-native systems; all major carbon, biodiversity, and renewable energy markets operate through digital platforms with regulatory compliance implications for transaction documentation, audit trail maintenance, and reporting to regulatory authorities).
6.6.4 | Conservation finance mechanisms        | S1      | S3        | digital-permitting                   | S1 Primary: designing and deploying conservation finance mechanisms — structuring payment for ecosystem services programs, designing conservation investment vehicles, developing blended finance arrangements, identifying conservation finance opportunities — is a planning function. Conservation finance design is a Stage 1 output that creates the financial architecture within which conservation programs are sustained. S3 Secondary: conservation finance mechanisms typically require regulatory authorization to deploy — easement conveyances, banking instruments, and PES program agreements are Stage 3 authorization products. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (conservation finance platforms — including digital PES payment systems, tokenized conservation credits, and integrated monitoring-to-payment infrastructure — are restructuring how conservation finance mechanisms are structured and administered).
6.6.5 | Green bonds and sustainable investment | S1     | S3        | digital-permitting                   | S1 Primary: designing green bond frameworks and sustainable investment strategies — developing green bond eligibility criteria, structuring ESG investment mandates, designing sustainability-linked finance instruments — is a planning function. Green bond and sustainable investment strategy is a Stage 1 output. S3 Secondary: green bonds require regulatory authorization and market registration — SEC disclosure requirements for green bond issuers, stock exchange green bond listing standards, and regulatory verification of green bond use of proceeds are Stage 3 functions that practitioners of this competency must navigate. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (green bond verification, ESG data reporting, and sustainable investment disclosure are increasingly digitized; the EU taxonomy for sustainable activities, SFDR reporting requirements, and digital ESG data platforms are restructuring the regulatory infrastructure through which green investment instruments are authorized and monitored).

## SKILL AREA 6.7: ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH & JUSTICE

Code  | Title                                          | Primary | Secondary | Reform Sensitivity                              | Notes
------|------------------------------------------------|---------|-----------|-------------------------------------------------|-------
6.7.1 | Environmental health risk assessment           | S2      | S5        | consultation-reform                             | S2 Primary: assessing environmental health risks — characterizing exposure pathways, evaluating toxicological evidence, quantifying health risk from contaminant exposure, and producing health risk characterizations — is an environmental review function. Health risk assessment produces the human health effects characterization that is a required component of NEPA review and Superfund risk-based cleanup decision-making. S5 Secondary: health risk assessment also operates at the monitoring stage — tracking community health outcomes against baseline, evaluating whether remediation or mitigation measures are reducing exposure, and monitoring biomarkers are systematic assessment functions. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (environmental health risk assessment has significant environmental justice dimensions; community engagement in defining exposure pathways, selecting risk characterization methods, and interpreting health risk results is a form of consultation with affected communities; consultation reform that compresses engagement timelines affects whether health risk assessment can incorporate community knowledge and address community concerns adequately).
6.7.2 | Environmental justice screening and assessment | S2    | S1        | consultation-reform, NEPA-timeline              | S2 Primary: screening and assessing environmental justice implications — identifying disproportionately burdened communities, characterizing cumulative exposure and impact, analyzing distributional effects across demographic groups — is an environmental review function. EJ screening and assessment is a required NEPA analytical component under Executive Order 12898 and subsequent CEQ guidance; it produces the distributional effects characterization that makes environmental review responsive to equity concerns. S1 Secondary: EJ assessment also informs planning — incorporating EJ screening into program design, siting decisions, and investment priorities before projects reach environmental review is the upstream planning application of this competency. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (substantive EJ assessment requires genuine engagement with affected communities; consultation reform that compresses participation timelines reduces the quality of community input into EJ characterization, converting EJ analysis from community-responsive assessment into technocratic impact mapping); NEPA-timeline (EJ assessment is methodologically intensive — characterizing cumulative burden across demographic groups requires data that is not always readily available and analysis that timeline compression constrains).
6.7.3 | Community-based participatory research        | S1      | S2        | consultation-reform                             | S1 Primary: designing community-based participatory research processes for environmental health and justice — developing co-research partnerships, designing participatory exposure assessment protocols, structuring community-driven data collection, building knowledge co-production frameworks — is a planning function. CBPR design is a Stage 1 output that shapes what data will be collected, by whom, and for whose benefit. S2 Secondary: community-based participatory research increasingly informs environmental review — producing baseline community condition data, characterizing local ecological and health knowledge, and documenting community-defined impacts that agency analysis would otherwise miss. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (CBPR is the most intensive and relationship-demanding form of community engagement; consultation reform that compresses timelines is directly incompatible with the iterative, trust-building character of genuine participatory research, making CBPR a sentinel competency for the quality implications of participation reform).
6.7.4 | Toxicology and exposure pathways              | S2      | S5        | consultation-reform                             | S2 Primary: applying toxicological knowledge to characterize exposure pathways — identifying how contaminants move through environmental media, assessing bioavailability, evaluating dose-response relationships, and characterizing aggregate and cumulative exposure — is an environmental review function. Toxicological exposure pathway analysis is the scientific foundation for environmental health review of contaminated sites, chemical-releasing projects, and cumulative exposure evaluations. S5 Secondary: toxicology and exposure pathway knowledge is applied in monitoring — tracking contaminant concentrations in environmental media and human biomarkers, evaluating whether remediation is reducing exposure along characterized pathways, and updating exposure models as monitoring data accumulates. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (toxicological exposure characterization has significant EJ implications; communities with prior cumulative burden face different exposure risks from incremental contamination than baseline populations, requiring community-specific exposure analysis that adequate consultation makes possible).
6.7.5 | Air quality management                        | S2      | S3        | consultation-reform, NEPA-timeline, S2-S3-boundary | S2 Primary: managing air quality compliance — characterizing air quality impacts of proposed projects, conducting air dispersion modeling, assessing Clean Air Act conformity, and producing air quality components of NEPA documents — is an environmental review function. Air quality analysis is a required component of NEPA review for projects with air quality implications, and it produces the regulatory characterization that precedes air permit issuance. S3 Secondary: air quality analysis directly enables air permitting — Prevention of Significant Deterioration permits, Title V operating permits, and minor source permits are issued based on the air quality analysis produced in Stage 2. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (air quality management has significant EJ dimensions; disproportionate air quality burden on communities of color and low-income communities is among the most documented EJ findings; consultation reform that compresses community engagement in air quality review affects whether cumulative burden is adequately characterized); NEPA-timeline (air quality modeling for complex projects requires specialized analysis that timeline compression constrains); S2-S3-boundary (air quality analysis is the paradigm case of S2 work that directly unlocks S3 authorization — air quality models produced in environmental review become the technical basis for permit conditions in air authorizations).

## SKILL AREA 6.8: CLIMATE FINANCE & INVESTMENT

6.8.1 | Climate finance mechanisms          | S1      | S3        | digital-permitting, one-federal-decision        | S1 Primary: designing and deploying climate finance mechanisms — structuring climate investment vehicles, developing blended finance arrangements for adaptation and mitigation, identifying climate finance opportunities across public and private sources — is a planning function. S3 Secondary: climate finance mechanisms require regulatory authorization — green bond prospectus registration, climate resilience fund instruments, and federal climate investment program authorizations are Stage 3 products. 6.8.2 | Green bonds and climate bonds       | S1      | S3        | digital-permitting                              | S1 Primary: designing green bond and climate bond frameworks — developing issuance criteria, structuring use-of-proceeds requirements, designing reporting and verification protocols, and advising on climate bond strategy — is a planning function. Green and climate bond strategy is a Stage 1 output. S3 Secondary: green and climate bond issuance requires regulatory authorization and disclosure — securities registration, exchange listing approval, and third-party verification are Stage 3 authorization functions. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (green and climate bond verification and reporting are increasingly conducted through digital platforms; the Climate Bonds Initiative's digital certification system and SEC's electronic green bond disclosure framework are live digital permitting infrastructure for this competency).
6.8.3 | Carbon markets and trading          | S3      | S1        | digital-permitting, S2-S3-boundary              | S3 Primary: operating in carbon markets — trading carbon credits, managing carbon accounts, executing compliance carbon obligations, and navigating carbon market regulatory frameworks — is an authorization function. Carbon market transactions are regulatory instruments; compliance carbon allowances and offsets are permits to emit, and their transfer and retirement constitute authorization events. S1 Secondary: carbon market strategy — assessing carbon price trajectories, designing organizational carbon management frameworks, integrating carbon market participation into organizational financial planning — is a planning function. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (carbon markets are digital-native systems; all major compliance and voluntary carbon markets operate through digital registries with regulatory compliance implications for transaction documentation and reporting); S2-S3-boundary (compliance carbon obligations are determined through regulatory review processes and fulfilled through market transactions, placing carbon trading at the S2/S3 boundary where regulatory determination (S2) enables market authorization (S3)).
6.8.4 | Adaptation finance                 | S1      | S3        | consultation-reform, digital-permitting         | S1 Primary: designing adaptation finance strategies — identifying fundable adaptation priorities, structuring climate resilience investments, developing adaptation finance business cases, and accessing climate adaptation funding streams — is a planning function. Adaptation finance strategy is the Stage 1 output that enables adaptation programs to be capitalized and implemented. S3 Secondary: adaptation finance programs require authorization — FEMA Hazard Mitigation Grant Program authorizations, USDA climate-smart agriculture program agreements, and state adaptation fund instruments are Stage 3 products that practitioners of this competency must navigate. Reform sensitivity: consultation-reform (adaptation finance for frontline communities requires meaningful community engagement to identify and fund genuine adaptation priorities; consultation reform that compresses engagement timelines risks funding adaptation programs that do not address community-defined vulnerabilities, reducing both effectiveness and equity); digital-permitting (adaptation finance programs are increasingly administered through digital platforms; federal climate resilience grant portals, digital adaptation program reporting systems, and automated compliance monitoring are active areas of digital government development for adaptation finance).
6.8.5 | Climate investment risk assessment  | S1      | S5        | digital-permitting                              | S1 Primary: assessing climate investment risk — characterizing physical climate risk, transition risk, and liability risk for conservation and environmental investments; applying climate scenario analysis to investment portfolios; developing climate risk disclosure frameworks — is a planning function. Climate investment risk assessment is the analytical foundation for climate-informed investment strategy. S5 Secondary: climate investment risk assessment also operates at the monitoring stage — tracking whether physical climate risks are materializing as projected, updating risk assessments as climate conditions evolve, and monitoring whether climate risk mitigation investments are performing as designed. Reform sensitivity: digital-permitting (climate risk disclosure is increasingly required in digital regulatory reporting; SEC climate disclosure rules, TCFD-aligned reporting, and digital ESG data platforms are restructuring how climate investment risk is assessed, reported, and verified in regulatory contexts).

## Structural Character
Domain 6 is governance architecture — the domain where regulatory frameworks are designed, applied, interpreted, and reformed. The three-way S1/S2/S3 Primary distribution (13/12/10) is unique in the taxonomy and reflects Domain 6's structural role as the domain that produces each of the first three stages as professional outputs. Domain 6 plans what will happen (S1), reviews whether it is legally permissible (S2), and authorizes it to proceed (S3). No other domain spans all three pre-implementation stages as Primary outputs.

The complete S4 absence is the structural inverse of Domain 4's complete S2/S6 absence. Domain 6 designs governance and Domain 4 executes operations; neither enters the other's territory as a Primary or Secondary. This is the sharpest functional boundary in the taxonomy — governance architecture and field operations are structurally and professionally distinct.

The S2-S3-boundary reform flag dominance (10/24 flagged competencies) is the structural argument about Domain 6's reform exposure: permitting reform targets the S2/S3 boundary most explicitly, and Domain 6 is where that boundary is defined, administered, and contested. The boundary-straddling competencies concentrated here — 6.1.2, 6.1.3, 6.1.4, 6.3.4, 6.3.5, 6.5.3, 6.6.1, 6.6.2, 6.7.5, 6.8.3 — are the competency-level evidence that permitting reform is a governance architecture problem, not a process efficiency problem. Reforming the S2/S3 boundary requires changing what practitioners of these competencies do and how they are trained.

The paradigm case (6.1.4) carrying four simultaneous reform codes names the practitioner who navigates all four reform vectors simultaneously as the workforce development argument's most concrete illustration. That practitioner is not rare; NEPA process implementation is the largest single professional competency cluster in the federal environmental workforce. Reform lands first and hardest on the professional who holds this competency.
